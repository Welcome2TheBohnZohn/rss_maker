TITLE:
Giant panda Ran Ran arrives in Belgium, extending bilateral partnership

PUBLISHED:

SOURCE:

ARTICLE TEXT:
A nine-year-old female giant panda named Ran Ran arrived at Brussels Airport in Zaventem, Belgium, on Saturday, marking a new chapter in the giant panda conservation and research cooperation between China and Belgium as the two countries celebrate the 55th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic relations this year.
Ran Ran will make her new home at Pairi Daiza zoo, where male giant panda Xing Hui has been living since 2014.
"We are very happy today to welcome Ran Ran to our Chinese garden. She will be surrounded by a small piece of China," Eric Domb, founder and chairman of Pairi Daiza, told China Daily in an exclusive interview. "I'm happy for us and happy for all the Belgians who will love this beautiful lady."
Born on May 31, 2017, Ran Ran comes from the China Conservation and Research Center for the Giant Panda in Southwest China's Sichuan province. Weighing about 110 kilograms, she is described by her keepers as having a gentle temperament, enjoying quiet surroundings and being particularly fond of tender bamboo shoots.
Her move to Belgium was officially announced on Oct 26 by the Chinese and Belgian partners, following a supplementary agreement signed between the China Wildlife Conservation Association and Pairi Daiza in September.
"The giant panda has always held a special place in the friendship between China and Belgium," Li Yan, chargé d'affaires ad interim of the Chinese embassy in Belgium, said in a speech delivered at the airport. She also recalled the inauguration of the giant panda pavilion at Pairi Daiza in 2014.
Giant panda conservation cooperation between the two countries dates back to 2013, when the two sides signed a 15-year agreement. In February 2014, giant pandas Hao Hao and Xing Hui arrived in Belgium. The pair subsequently had three cubs — Tian Bao, born in 2016, and twins Bao Di and Bao Mei, born in 2019 — all of whom returned to China in December 2024.
Following Hao Hao's return to China for medical treatment in late 2025, the two sides agreed to send another adult female giant panda to Belgium to continue the existing conservation program, which runs until February 2029. And now Ran Ran has arrived.
"Our friendship with China started when we began building a traditional Chinese garden in 2006, 20 years ago, even before Hao Hao and Xing Hui, our first giant pandas, arrived in Belgium," Domb said. "It's a beautiful and long friendship and collaboration for wildlife conservation."
