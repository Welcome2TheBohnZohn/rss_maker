TITLE:
Snow holidays see school kids hit ski slopes

ARTICLE TEXT:
Children with skis slung over their shoulders boarded free shuttle buses at Chongli's South Passenger Transport Hub and headed for the nearby slopes as Zhangjiakou, Hebei province, rolled out its first school snow holiday recently.
"I used to watch skiing on TV, but now I can try it myself," said Wang Ting, a student from Zhuolu county, who tried skiing at Snow Ruyi Ski Resort.
Zhangjiakou has arranged a three-day break for students from first to eighth grade. Primary school pupils had their holiday from Nov 18 to 20, while students in the seventh and eighth grades are scheduled to take theirs from Nov 25 to 27. Local authorities are encouraging employers to offer flexible leave and prioritize schedule adjustments for workers with younger children.
Zhang Litao, a staff member at the Nanzhan subdistrict office, said he had secured leave from work and booked a Wednesday skiing trip to a ski resort for his eighth grade child, who has been skiing for years.
To make skiing more accessible, local authorities and businesses have paired the holidays with free transportation, discounted services and access to selected cultural venues. Four Chongli ski resorts — Fulong, Yinhe, Snow Ruyi and Duolemeidi — are offering a combined 650 free skiing admissions per day for local students.
At Snow Ruyi, students can receive free ski passes and equipment, while accompanying parents can get 30 percent discounts, plus 15 percent off on accommodation. Students pay a 20 yuan insurance fee ($3), said Sun Yan, the resort's brand manager.
Safety is a priority. The city's education bureau instructed schools to provide preholiday safety education on winter sports and traffic, and to coordinate with parents on supervision. Schools were also asked to provide care for students who need supervision during the break.
At Fulong, staff members set aside beginner areas, prepared equipment in advance and arranged lessons covering safe skiing and basic techniques.
About 100 children signed up to ski at Snow Ruyi on the opening day, most of them accompanied by family members, Hebei Daily reported. Fulong staff reported that visitor numbers were up about 30 percent year-on-year after the break began.
Similar holidays were tried last winter in Urumqi and Altay of the Xinjiang Uygur autonomous region, in Jilin province and later in Hulunbuir, Inner Mongolia. This year, all these places have renewed or refined their arrangements, with many breaks extended to five days by linking them with adjacent weekends.
In Altay, the Dec 16-18 holiday will involve four ski resorts and public venues, as well as study tours and cultural activities. Local tourism businesses are developing dedicated skiing and study tour packages for the holiday.
The Keketuohai Study Tour Camp, operated by a State-owned tourism company, has introduced separate three — and five-day skiing programs and study tours covering local history, geology, ecology and ethnic culture.
"The programs are open to local families and visitors from outside the region," said Nana, a teacher at the camp.
In Jilin, bookings for ice-and-snow destinations rose more than 120 percent from the previous period ahead of the December break, according to Tongcheng Travel, as reported by Cailian News. The province is encouraging employers to accommodate parents' existing paid leave or flexible time off.
The initiatives draw on national education guidance that allows local authorities to arrange school calendars while maintaining required teaching hours.
A 2024 State Council guideline set a target of 1.2 trillion yuan for the ice-and-snow economy by 2027. In Chongli, a 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics host, around 30,000 residents work directly or indirectly in winter sports and tourism, Hebei Daily reported.
Liu Fan, an associate professor at the Communication University of China, told Guangming Daily that snow holidays offer more time for winter activities by extending consumption beyond ski resorts to accommodation, dining and cultural products.
