TITLE:
Night classes bring culture alive

ARTICLE TEXT:
If he hadn't tried it himself, Shareef Shabaz, a 24-year-old Pakistani student, would never have guessed that sugar could be more than a sweet treat — it can also become art.
The discovery did not come at a tourist stall or a museum, but during a night class in Beijing's Haidian district, where Shabaz drew his first sugar dragon and tried his hand at sugar blowing, a traditional technique used in Chinese sugar art.
"If it weren't for this vivid, fun class, I wouldn't have realized Chinese culture is tied to real life so much more than textbooks," Shabaz said.
Across China, night classes are giving learners a lighter introduction to Chinese culture. At Shabaz's school, for example, each class attracts 20 to 30 learners from countries such as Pakistan, Russia, Cuba and Sri Lanka.
For foreign visitors who want to try such classes, local district authorities' official social media accounts are a good place to look, with QR codes often provided for registration. In Haidian, classes range from tea culture and sugar art to making traditional Chinese clothing. Most of the classes are free.
The idea is not necessarily to turn newcomers into experts. A few hours of making something with their own hands can instead give them a reason to start asking questions about the culture behind it.
Russian learner Tatiana Kazakevich found that out with a ceramic cup. Before the class, she had seen similar cups with floral motifs in museums but never thought about the meanings behind the patterns.
In the class, she followed her teacher, painting lotuses and grapevines on her cup. As the patterns took shape, she started exploring the symbolism behind different floral motifs: the lotus stands for purity, while grapevines represent bonds and togetherness. She also painted a peony after learning that it symbolizes prosperity in China.
"Now I see the flowers not just as pretty, but as showing vitality and flourishing life energy," Kazakevich said. "As I made it with my own hands, I felt I was an ancient artisan, as if I was part of Chinese culture."
For some learners, a night class can also reveal unexpected connections with their homeland.
Madhushani Gunawardhana from Sri Lanka once thought that the culture of her country was vastly different from that of China. But a handicraft night class changed her mind.
At the class on chanhua, a traditional Chinese craft of making delicate flowers with wrapped silk, Gunawardhana watched her teacher twist thin threads to form delicate yellow petals. She then picked up the threads herself, and something about the technique felt strangely familiar.
"It instantly reminded me of our Beeralu lace in Galle," she said. "It struck me how much traditional heritage can have in common across different cultures and times."
That discovery stirred her interest in exploring more of Chinese culture. She said that, given the opportunity, she would like to travel around China and participate in night classes in other cities, hoping to find more moments of cross-cultural dialogue.
There are certainly many places to find such moments, as night classes have become a nationwide trend, with programs in cities including Beijing, Shanghai and Chengdu, Sichuan province. This year, Shanghai alone has 1,962 night-class venues, attracting 194,000 learners, according to the Shanghai Observer.
Behind the expansion is a broader push to make lifelong learning more accessible. In 2025, China unveiled a national blueprint on building the country into an education powerhouse by 2035. It aims to upgrade public services for lifelong learning and build a learning-oriented society where "everyone can learn, anywhere, anytime".
As night classes expand their courses and venues, international learners are also becoming part of that vision of learning beyond conventional classrooms.
Meanwhile, as China's visitor-friendly entry policies draw more overseas visitors to the country, trends such as "China Study" and "China Travel" are bringing a growing number of international learners to night classes, and their interests may offer new inspiration for future course design.
"I hope night classes can roll out more courses related to AI and the smart economy in the future," said Shabaz from Pakistan.
Service info
Foreign visitors can sign up for night classes in Beijing's Haidian district via QR codes posted on its official WeChat account, where class schedules are also available. Most classes are free.
Other districts in Beijing, including Chaoyang and Xicheng, have released their December night-class schedules. Foreign visitors can find registration information on Beijing Bendibao, a local lifestyle information platform.
In Shanghai, night-class listings are available through the official WeChat account of the Shanghai Public Art Center. Shanghai's community night classes cost 500 yuan ($75) for 12 sessions, and are open to all.
