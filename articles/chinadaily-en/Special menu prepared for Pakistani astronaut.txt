TITLE:
Special menu prepared for Pakistani astronaut

PUBLISHED:
2026-11-28

SOURCE:

ARTICLE TEXT:
Following his arrival at China's Tiangong space station with Chinese crewmates for the Shenzhou XXIV mission, Pakistani astronaut Muhammad Zeeshan Ali is already enjoying a selection of carefully prepared meals specially designed by Chinese space food researchers.
To accommodate Ali's dietary preferences and halal requirements, researchers from the Astronaut Center of China developed a customized meal plan in advance to provide "cross-cultural, in-orbit nutritional support", China Central Television reported.
Before embarking on the Shenzhou XXIV mission, Ali, the first Pakistani national to reach Earth's orbit, and fellow Pakistani astronaut Khurram Daud had received about seven months of intensive spaceflight training at a Beijing-based center.
CCTV reported that the food research team conducted in-depth studies of Pakistani food culture, halal regulations and folk customs, as well as local nutritional standards.
Drawing on the existing space food inventory, the researchers selected and modified recipes to create a dedicated food supply of more than 100 varieties for the Pakistani astronauts. They also developed seven Pakistani-style space foods, including beef nihari and lamb korma.
Chen Junli, a space food researcher at the center, told CCTV that "on the basis of ensuring that energy and nutrients meet standard requirements, we designed recipes suitable for the Pakistani astronaut's first several meals after entering orbit, when light, easily digestible food is needed, and make adjustments according to personal needs".
"Given their preference for spices such as turmeric ... we developed foods like curry chickpeas and butter chicken that fit their local culinary features. The entire production line is fully halal. The foods have gone through repeated taste tests and iterative formula optimization, and received positive feedback from the two Pakistani astronauts during trials," Chen added.
During the research and development process, Chen and her colleagues also incorporated dietary suggestions from Ali and Daud, making personalized adjustments to the recipes.
"An interesting detail is that in our initial recipe design, we planned to provide locally popular spiced milk tea. But after coming to China, they tried Chinese tea and found Chinese black tea very enjoyable. They also liked Chinese rice very much, so they requested more Chinese rice and tea", which was factored into the recipe design, the researcher recalled.
Chen said that following the arrival of the Shenzhou XXIV crew, the six astronauts — the three newcomers and the three members of the Shenzhou XXIII mission — were to share a dinner during the handover period.
"With full respect to Pakistani dietary habits, we designed a six-person shared meal, and at other times, astronauts can choose their own dining mode as they wish," she said. "All the dishes for the group meal are favorites of both crews, so they can enjoy the food together."
Chen said the Shenzhou XXIV mission marked the first real-world application of the Chinese space food researchers' efforts to develop nutritional plans.
"From preliminary investigation, ingredient selection, food development and recipe design to the selection of in-orbit dining modes, we have formed a set of reusable procedures and working paradigms, and we can use them to provide standardized support for future international cooperation aboard the Tiangong space station," Chen told CCTV.
Wang Ya'nan, chief editor of Aerospace Knowledge magazine, told China Daily that as international cooperation programs aboard the Chinese space station expand in the coming years, astronauts from more countries are expected to take part in joint missions there. This will pose greater challenges for Chinese space food researchers, but it is also a highly positive development, he said.
