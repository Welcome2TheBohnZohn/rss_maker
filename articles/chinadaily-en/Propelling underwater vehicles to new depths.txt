Propelling underwater vehicles to new depths

ARTICLE TEXT:
Han Ye vividly remembers the moment the underwater vehicle Wukong reached depths of nearly 11,000 meters in the Mariana Trench in November 2021, setting a new world record for unmanned untethered deep-sea diving.
"I had been anxious. Then the data came back, and anxiety gave way to elation," he said.
The underwater thrusters on the Wukong were developed by Han and his team over a four-year period.
Dozens of equipment sets had been crushed by extreme pressure during trials before the hardware faced its ultimate test.
The achievement remains a source of pride for Chinese enterprises and for the country's vocational education sector.
Drawn to machinery since childhood, Han graduated from Tianjin Vocational Institute in 2013, majoring in electrical automation technology.
The university's School of Mechanical and Electrical Engineering and Automation, one of its earliest faculties, is led by master technicians and experienced instructors.
National skills competitions opened his eyes to the possibilities of technical skills. He threw himself into contests and innovation, determined to make his mark in the mechanical and electrical field.
"We advocate learning through competitions and innovation driven by real-world research. We encourage students to turn hands-on training into practical projects and dare to try and fail in innovation," said a senior instructor at the school's training center, who declined to be named.
The university is a national high-level vocational institute under China's "Double-High Plan", a national initiative to boost vocational education excellence.
