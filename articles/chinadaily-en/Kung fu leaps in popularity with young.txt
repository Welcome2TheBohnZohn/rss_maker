ARTICLE TEXT:
The spear flashed forward. Opposite it, a pair of sabers swept through the air in rapid arcs. The two performers, dressed in camouflage uniforms, moved through a tightly choreographed exchange before breaking away and launching into the next sequence.
The intensity of the display had students watching nearby cheering. Filmed during a break in military training at Beijing Sport University in early September, the video soon went viral online, drawing an enthusiastic response from viewers far beyond the campus.
It was an impromptu performance, but one that seemed to strike a chord with young audiences.
Zhang Bohan, an 18-year-old freshman at the university's China Wushu School (Research Institute of Traditional Chinese Sports), was not surprised by the response.
"To many young people, wushu (martial arts) looks cool and impressive," she said. Zhang was drawn to the discipline after watching a live martial arts performance.
She first took up martial arts at 8, after coming across a class in her hometown of Zhoukou, Henan province. It piqued her curiosity and she moved from local classes to more intensive training and competitions.
Years of practice have given her not only technical skills but also confidence she lacked before. "Confidence and the determination never to accept defeat are among the greatest things martial arts have given me," she said.
In 2025, she and two teammates won third place in a three-person paired-practice event at a national championship. The result helped her gain admission to Beijing Sport University through its sports recruitment program.
"I owe a lot to the strict guidance of my coaches," she said.
