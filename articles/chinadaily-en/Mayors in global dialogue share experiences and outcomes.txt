Mayors in global dialogue share experiences and outcomes

ARTICLE TEXT:
The 2026 Global Mayors Dialogue took place on Thursday in Urumqi, Xinjiang Uygur autonomous region, bringing together mayors and other representatives from 26 cities across 13 countries to exchange views on modern urban governance concepts and share practical experience and outcomes.
Themed "Putting People First, Driving Innovation: Working Together to Build Modern Cities for the People", the event drew more than 200 participants from countries including Chile, Georgia, Pakistan, Thailand and the United States. Attendees discussed effective measures to promote green and low-carbon development, encourage innovation and uphold "virtue and goodness".
Samir Seyidahmadlil, deputy plenipotentiary representative of the President of the Republic of Azerbaijan in Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic, said the theme raises the practical question of how a modern city should improve people's lives — specifically measuring progress by how much easier, healthier and more secure people's lives become.
He said better coordination, faster responses and good decisions based on reliable information can drive innovation and make public services more accessible. Cities can embrace green practices while remaining true to their history and can renew neighborhoods while preserving architectural identity and community life. Seyidahmadlil called for further exchanges and cooperation between cities in Azerbaijan and China in all these areas.
Paul B. Thole, permanent secretary of Zambia's Eastern Province, said the theme speaks directly to the representatives' shared mission of ensuring that development improves people's lives.
"Our cities may be geographically apart, but we are united in our faith in progress, innovation and excellence," he said, while also stressing the shared emphasis on collaboration.
He added that Zambia's Eastern Province is a major food producer in the country, and agriculture remains the backbone of its economy and people's livelihoods.
"We see great opportunities for a people-centered friendship in key areas such as agriculture," Thole said, referring to Xinjiang.
Yakup Paydulla, mayor of Urumqi, the region's capital, said the city has leveraged opening-up as a significant advantage and opportunity in recent years. This has raised the level of opening-up in its pilot free trade zone, international land port area and comprehensive bonded zone, as well as driving a surge in both trade volume and international passenger numbers. It has built sister-city relationships with 39 cities in 27 countries.
He said Urumqi will further expand trade exchanges and cooperation with foreign counterparts, continue to improve services for foreign nationals and strengthen international business and investment capacity. It will also regularly organize mutual visits, touring performances, youth exchange activities (such as summer camps and sports events), while building online exchange platforms.
Ahead of the dialogue, the mayors and city representatives visited cities across Xinjiang, including Kashgar, Yining, Karamay and Kuqa and discussed related topics.
