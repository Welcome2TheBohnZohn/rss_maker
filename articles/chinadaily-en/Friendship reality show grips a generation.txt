Friendship reality show grips a generation

ARTICLE TEXT:
When Xue Siyuan finished his final class one Thursday afternoon in early November, he did not head to the library or meet friends for dinner. Instead, the 24-year-old master's student at Renmin University of China set aside an entire evening for a single purpose: to watch the latest episode of I Miss You, a reality show about estranged friends attempting to reconnect.
"I started counting down the night before," Xue said. "I've been watching clips and fan edits all week. By the time the full episode dropped, I'd already seen the viral moments dozens of times — but I still wanted to watch the whole thing properly without interruption."
Xue said the show has become a hot conversation topic among his peers. "Almost everyone in my social circle has heard of it, and most can engage with the memes. It's rare for a variety show to generate this kind of collective conversation about something as personal as friendship."
He is not alone. Since its premiere in late October on Mango TV, the friendship-themed reality show has become a genuine cultural phenomenon, dominating social media discourse and drawing audiences across generational lines. The numbers bear this out.
According to data released by the program's producer Mango TV, as of Nov 10, the show had been viewed 910 million times on the platform. Its hashtag had garnered 860 million views on Weibo, 10.4 billion plays on Douyin, and 3.87 billion views on Xiaohongshu. Daily discussions on Xiaohongshu were 23 times higher than the second-most-discussed topic.
On Bilibili, fan-generated content related to the show has accumulated over 170 million views. Enlightent, a Chinese entertainment data analytics platform, reported that the program captured a staggering 92.7 percent share of effective plays among new variety shows.
