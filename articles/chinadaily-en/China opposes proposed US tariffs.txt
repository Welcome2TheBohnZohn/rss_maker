TITLE:
China opposes proposed US tariffs

PUBLISHED:
2026-11-27

SOURCE:
China Daily English

CATEGORY:
Authoritative PRC Media

ARTICLE TEXT:
China has voiced firm opposition to the United States' latest proposals to ratchet up tariffs following its Section 301 investigations, the Ministry of Commerce said on Thursday, urging Washington to "correct its wrongdoings" and resolve trade differences through dialogue and consultation.
"The US Section 301 investigations are typical acts of unilateralism that seriously disrupt the international economic order and violate World Trade Organization rules," He Yadong, a spokesman for the ministry, said at a news conference in Beijing.
On Wednesday, the Office of the US Trade Representative announced the findings of its investigations into the so-called "structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing sectors" and proposed additional tariffs on imports from 14 economies, including China, the European Union and Japan, under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974.
For China, the proposal accounts for the commitments and 20 percent tariff rate announced as part of the Kuala Lumpur joint arrangement, as well as certain existing Section 301 duties, the office said. The proposed measures remain subject to hearings and public comment before they can be finalized.
However, the Office of the US Trade Representative said that it could consider tariffs of up to 40 percent on all Chinese goods if it determines that ongoing talks with Beijing have not adequately addressed the issues raised in the investigations.
Responding to the proposed tariff hikes, the ministry spokesman said China will closely monitor developments and reserves the right to take necessary measures to firmly safeguard its legitimate rights and interests.
The spokesman also noted that the US proposal includes exemptions for products on the $30 billion tariff reduction list, calling on Washington to expedite its internal procedures and work with Beijing to swiftly implement the agreed reciprocal tariff cuts.
Cui Fan, a professor of international trade at the University of International Business and Economics in Beijing, said that Washington is seeking to perpetuate its protectionist trade policies by reviving sweeping tariffs struck down by the US Supreme Court earlier this year.
"The measures and tariff rates were broadly in line with industry expectations," Cui said. "The investigations merely pieced together a case to support predetermined conclusions."
The National Foreign Trade Council, a US business association, warned that prolonged reliance on tariffs would also saddle US businesses and consumers with long-term costs while offering little prospect of resolving the root causes of "excess manufacturing capacity".
"We are troubled by the administration's continued reliance on tariffs as the tool of choice to address complex international policy challenges," Tiffany Smith, the council's vice-president of global trade policy, said in a statement.
That concern is shared by the American Soybean Association, which warned that the tariff proposals could expose billions of dollars in agricultural trade to retaliation.
"Soybean farmers are barely more than breaking even this year after prior losses due to retaliatory tariffs," Scott Metzger, the association's president and an Ohio soybean farmer, said in a statement. Fresh retaliatory tariffs, he warned, could impose "a continued burden they cannot afford".
Washington's tariff proposals came on the heels of the first meeting of the agricultural working group under the China-US Board of Trade in Beijing earlier this week, with the Commerce Ministry calling the exchanges "candid, in-depth and constructive".
However, such unilateral moves are disrupting bilateral consultations and pushing other countries to take measures in response, said Zhou Mi, a senior researcher at the Chinese Academy of International Trade and Economic Cooperation.
"The two sides should focus on further tariff reductions and deliver on the consensus reached by the two heads of state to build a relationship of strategic stability," Zhou said.
The two countries' economic and trade teams remain in close contact over a further extension of the Kuala Lumpur joint arrangement, He, the ministry spokesman, said at Thursday's news conference.
The arrangement, reached last year, suspended the implementation of certain tariff and nontariff measures, and has since been extended to Jan 10. A further extension would offer businesses a relatively stable and predictable policy environment for cooperation and serve both countries' interests, He said.
Rose Tu, Asia-Pacific vice-president of Americhem Inc, a US-based manufacturer of specialty plastic compounds and additives, said that its facility in Suzhou, East China's Jiangsu province, complements its manufacturing capabilities in the US and Denmark.
"This collaboration allows us to bring together expertise and experience from different regions," Tu said.
US aircraft engine maker GE Aerospace has also expanded cooperation with Chinese partners, signing more than $12.9 billion in sales and service agreements in Shanghai in early November.
Xiang Weiming, its vice-president, said the company will continue to deepen its presence in China and provide reliable power for exchanges and cooperation between China and the world.
