TITLE:
APEC growth forecast upgraded to 3.4 percent

ARTICLE TEXT:
The economy of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation region is projected to grow 3.4 percent in 2026, supported by strong technology investments even as energy costs and trade restrictions weigh on the outlook.
"We have a combination of upside opportunities but also downside risks," said Carlos Kuriyama, director of the APEC Policy Support Unit.
Speaking at an online media briefing held on Sunday by the APEC Secretariat ahead of the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting, which will be held on Wednesday and Thursday in Shenzhen, Guangdong province, Kuriyama said that regional economic growth has remained resilient, but the economic environment is becoming more challenging.
The latest growth forecast is higher than the earlier projection of 3.2 percent, according to the report released by the APEC Policy Support Unit on Sunday.
Trade in goods has provided a significant boost. In the first half of 2026, the export values of all APEC member economies combined surged 22.5 percent year-on-year, compared with 6.5 percent a year earlier.
While the trade outlook for this year is relatively good, Kuriyama said that he expects a moderation in the growth of export and import volume over the next two years, due to economic challenges, supply chain restrictions and trade policy uncertainty.
Inflation is also expected to increase to 3.2 percent in 2026, up 0.8 percentage point from 2025, driven by higher energy prices and electricity costs.
"Based on the current situation, we are advocating … for policy priorities," said Kuriyama, stressing the need for an effective mix of monetary and fiscal policy, diversifying investments and energy sources, strengthening the trade environment and fortifying multilateral links.
Also at the media briefing, Eduardo Pedrosa, executive director of the APEC Secretariat, said that building resilience is a key focus of this year's APEC discussions. Under China's rotating chairmanship, APEC has set openness, innovation and cooperation as priorities.
Pedrosa noted that APEC economies represent more than 60 percent of global GDP, and that roughly 300 meetings have been held this year, including a trade ministers' meeting in May in Suzhou, Jiangsu province.
"I think what makes APEC unique is that we are a process," said Pedrosa, adding that APEC provides a platform for regional economic leaders and the business community, enabling member economies to engage.
