China, APEC partners sail toward shared prosperity

ARTICLE TEXT:
At the APEC media center in Shenzhen, Guangdong province, nine digital flip screens rise like sails from a boat-shaped base. The installation, called "Asia-Pacific Sails", draws inspiration from a maritime image that President Xi Jinping evoked 13 years ago: the region's economies sailing forward together across a shared sea.
"Boundless is the ocean where we sail with the wind," Xi said at the APEC CEO Summit in Bali, Indonesia, in 2013.
Since then, Xi has attended or chaired successive Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Economic Leaders' Meetings, putting forward ideas, initiatives and proposals aimed at steering regional cooperation toward greater openness, shared development and mutual benefit.
The 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting will open in Shenzhen on Wednesday, marking the third time that China has hosted the gathering, following Shanghai in 2001 and Beijing in 2014.
Under the theme "Building an Asia-Pacific Community to Prosper Together", this year's APEC agenda centers on three priorities: openness, innovation and cooperation.
A consistent message in Xi's addresses over the years has been that China's door to the world will not close, but will only open wider.
At the APEC CEO Summit in Lima, Peru, in 2016, Xi emphasized that China is committed to a win-win strategy of opening-up. While striving for its own development, China would also work to promote the common development of all Asia-Pacific countries and create more opportunities for people in the region, he said.
Addressing the APEC CEO Dialogues in November 2020, Xi underscored that openness is important to a nation's development, saying that economic globalization was irreversible and no country could develop itself by keeping its doors closed.
Since China joined APEC 35 years ago, it has become increasingly integrated into the regional economy, with trade and investment ties linking its development ever more closely to that of Asia-Pacific economies.
China is now the largest trading partner of 13 APEC economies, while eight of its top 10 trading partners are APEC members.
Those ties have continued to deepen. In the first three quarters of 2026, China's trade in goods with other APEC economies rose 23.9 percent year-on-year to 24.07 trillion yuan ($3.6 trillion), accounting for 60.3 percent of its total foreign trade.
Alongside expanding trade, China has continued to widen market access and pursue institutional reforms for further opening-up. It has reduced its national negative list for foreign investment access to 29 items, removed all manufacturing-sector restrictions for foreign investment and expanded pilot programs for opening up the services sector.
Dun Zhigang, a senior research fellow at Renmin University of China's Chongyang Institute for Financial Studies, said China's opening-up increasingly extends beyond market access to encompass rules, regulations, management and standards.
Noting China's economic scale, manufacturing capabilities and role in supply chains, Gulnar Shaimergenova, director of the China Studies Center in Kazakhstan, said although Kazakhstan is not an APEC member, developments in the Asia-Pacific affect its economy through trade, investment and transportation.
Alongside openness, Xi has consistently emphasized innovation as a driving force for Asia-Pacific growth and the importance of ensuring that its benefits are widely shared.
At the 2013 APEC CEO Summit, he identified reform, readjustment and innovation as sources of renewed growth momentum.
Five years later, at the APEC CEO Summit in Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea, Xi warned that scientific and technological innovations should not be locked up or become profit-making tools for a few. He called for an environment of international cooperation that would deliver the fruit of innovation to more nations.
That emphasis on broadening access to innovation has taken on new significance with the rapid development of artificial intelligence.
At the second session of the 2025 APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting in Gyeongju, South Korea, Xi called for deeper cooperation on open-source technologies and said China was ready to work with other APEC members to enhance AI literacy and bridge the region's digital and AI divide.
China has also led the establishment of the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization to strengthen international cooperation on AI development and governance.
In July, China announced it would establish a center in Shenzhen's Qianhai area, providing a platform to promote APEC cooperation on cross-border data flows to support the region's digital development.
Lan Qingxin, director of the BRICS Research Center of the University of International Business and Economics said that policy exchanges, technical support and training could help developing economies participate more fully in digital transformation and industrial upgrading.
He called for greater technological cooperation and demonstration projects under APEC to help innovations move from experimentation to wider application.
Shenzhen's development offers valuable experience in how opening-up and institutional reform can translate investment and innovation into industrial strength, Lan said.
Jaspal Kaur Sadhu Singh, an associate professor at Canterbury Christ Church University in the United Kingdom, said China's experience could help emerging economies strengthen their own technological capabilities.
Other economies could draw lessons from China's application and governance of AI, while adapting those lessons to their own values, needs and circumstances, she said.
Paul Triolo, an honorary senior fellow on technology at the Asia Society Policy Institute's Center for China Analysis, said he expected discussions in Shenzhen to explore how governments could encourage innovation while establishing regulatory safeguards against the risks associated with AI.
At the APEC chairmanship handover session in Gyeongju in November 2025, Xi said China was ready to use its role as APEC host to work with other members toward building an Asia-Pacific community and promoting regional growth and prosperity.
He pledged to advance practical cooperation in a wide range of areas, with the aim of injecting fresh momentum into regional development and delivering greater benefits to people.
That commitment has been reflected in the agenda of China's APEC host year, during which it has held more than 300 meetings and events with other members.
In a signed article published on Monday, Foreign Minister Wang Yi said China would use the APEC leaders' declaration to draw up a road map for building an Asia-Pacific community and establish a framework for collective action.
The Shenzhen meeting also comes as 2026 marks the 20th anniversary of the vision for a Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific and the year of the final evaluation of the APEC Connectivity Blueprint for 2015-2025.
Wang said China would work to develop a framework document on trade digitalization cooperation and a new connectivity cooperation document based on the final review of the APEC Connectivity Blueprint.
The push for closer cooperation comes at a time when geopolitical tensions and differences in development are posing challenges to regional integration.
John Ross, a British economist, said APEC has demonstrated that economies with different political systems and levels of development can prioritize economic growth and pursue cooperation without setting aside differences.
APEC operates through consensus and voluntary, nonbinding commitments, allowing economies with diverse interests to pursue cooperation at their own pace. Dun, the researcher at Renmin University of China, said this approach could help members manage disagreements and find common ground while respecting their stages of development and interests.
Du Lan, deputy director of the China Institute of International Studies' Department for Asia-Pacific Studies, said that building an Asia-Pacific community could help to safeguard regional strategic stability amid growing pressures to form rival blocs.
By putting common development at the center of regional cooperation, such a community could help economies avoid being forced to choose sides and preserve the strategic autonomy of smaller economies, she said.
Guy Ryder, United Nations under-secretary-general for policy, said APEC provides an important opportunity for dialogue among influential international players. He expressed hope that the forum would help advance the UN Sustainable Development Goals and complement the world body's efforts to address shared challenges.
