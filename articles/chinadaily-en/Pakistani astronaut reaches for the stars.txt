TITLE:
Pakistani astronaut reaches for the stars

PUBLISHED:

ARTICLE TEXT:
Muhammad Zeeshan Ali finally realized his childhood dream on Wednesday morning by becoming the first Pakistani astronaut in space as a crew member of China's Shenzhou XXIV mission.
He also recorded another landmark by becoming the first foreign astronaut to visit the Tiangong space station.
The 40-year-old wing commander in the Pakistan Air Force said since he was a boy, he had closely followed projects such as the space shuttle program and the Mars rovers' landing.
"I was inspired by many astronauts worldwide," Ali told China Daily in an interview at the Astronaut Center of China headquarters in northwestern Beijing, several days before heading to the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in the Gobi Desert in northwestern China.
He said famous astronauts like Russia's Yuri Gagarin, the United States' Neil Armstrong, and China's Yang Liwei had "done wonders" for space travel. "All these people and programs inspired me to become an astronaut. Now, I can see that childhood dream coming true."
On a personal level, Ali said he has drawn inspiration from trips into the mountains, and looking at the clear night sky, the stars, the moon and the planets. Documentaries about the universe and books and movies have also fired his passion.
"Even in ancient days when there was no technology, there were still no limitations to human imagination. People have always been imagining new things, the stars and the planets," Ali said.
