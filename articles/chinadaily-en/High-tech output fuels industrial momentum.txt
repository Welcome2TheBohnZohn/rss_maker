TITLE:
High-tech output fuels industrial momentum

PUBLISHED:
2026-11-16

SOURCE:

Authoritative PRC Media

ARTICLE TEXT:
China's value-added industrial output rose 4.8 percent year-on-year in October, buoyed by double-digit growth in equipment and high-tech manufacturing, according to data released on Monday by the National Bureau of Statistics.
While the nation's high-tech manufacturing output surged 17.6 percent year-on-year in October, equipment manufacturing grew 10.6 percent, both outpacing the broader industrial sector, data showed. Overall industrial output rose 0.21 percent from September.
Industrial output is used to measure the activity of large enterprises, each with an annual main business revenue of at least 20 million yuan ($3 million).
In the first 10 months of 2026, China's industrial output rose 5.3 percent from a year earlier. Emerging growth drivers, including high-end and smart manufacturing, contributed more than half of the January-October increase, said Fu Linghui, spokesman for the NBS.
Wang Guanhua, spokeswoman for the NBS, said China's economy is expected to sustain its momentum toward higher-quality development as the coordinated implementation of macroeconomic policies continues to deliver results. "The country has the foundation and favorable conditions needed to achieve its annual economic growth target," she added.
Fu, the NBS spokesman, said the closer integration of technological innovation and industrial development has helped emerging industries expand and traditional industries upgrade. He, however, noted that the external environment remains complex and volatile, while domestic demand is still insufficient.
According to the NBS, retail sales rose 0.4 percent year-on-year in October, slowing from a 1.1 percent gain in September, while fixed-asset investment fell 8.1 percent in the first 10 months.
Amid continued industrial resilience but weak consumption, experts said the nation should accelerate its fiscal spending, front-load infrastructure investment and strengthen policy support for domestic demand to sustain growth this year.
They added that stronger policy coordination and more effective implementation will be key to turning policy support into real economic activity, while high-tech and emerging industries will continue to provide new growth drivers.
Wen Bin, chief economist at China Minsheng Bank, said the Chinese economy has remained broadly stable in the first 10 months, with industrial output and services maintaining steady growth, even though data indicates still-weak domestic demand.
He pointed out that October's consumption data was partly affected by the shift in timing of Mid-Autumn Festival, which pulled some holiday-related spending into September.
Wen said consumption is likely to remain structurally uneven in the coming months, with spending on services such as cultural tourism, catering and transportation showing greater resilience, while the recovery in durable goods consumption is more gradual.
"With macroeconomic policies placing greater emphasis on coordination, effective implementation and support for domestic demand, the key will be to accelerate project implementation and improve how policy measures translate into real economic activity," he added.
In the face of uncertainties, global business executives expressed confidence in China's long-term economic prospects, pointing to the country's vast market potential and opportunities arising from industrial upgrading and technological innovation.
Daniel Hui, president of Amorepacific China, said the country's huge consumer base, vibrant digital ecosystem and diverse demand offer substantial opportunities for foreign companies. "We remain confident in the long-term prospects of China's beauty market," he said, adding that competition is shifting from channel expansion toward research, quality and brand value. The South Korean beauty giant will continue investing in China, drawing on its Shanghai development and innovation center and local teams to incorporate consumer needs into product development.
Hui expressed the hope that China's innovation vitality and continued opening-up would create long-term opportunities for Amorepacific, with localized research, digitalization and sustainability guiding the company's future development in the market.
Susan Hughes, president of Asia Pacific at Johnson Controls, said that China's focus on industrial upgrading, wider artificial intelligence adoption and greener development aligns closely with the capabilities of the global industrial tech company.
Building on more than 30 years in China, the company will link its local research and development, manufacturing and service capabilities with its global network, serving growing demand in advanced manufacturing, data centers and life sciences while supporting its Chinese customers' overseas expansion, she added.
