Opening-up, tech strength fuel APEC

ARTICLE TEXT:
As the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation economies navigate a changing global landscape, China's continued opening-up and strengths in advanced manufacturing and green technologies are creating massive new opportunities for businesses across the Asia-Pacific region, executives of multinational corporations said.
These strategic developments are becoming increasingly crucial for enhancing regional productivity. By combining China's rapid technological innovation and immense market scale with the complementary resources of other APEC economies, businesses are moving beyond transactional relationships toward deeper partnerships to build a resilient and sustainable future for the region, they said.
For multinational companies, turning these shared opportunities into practical outcomes requires combining global operational experience with China's manufacturing ecosystems.
Mark Kouwenhoven, general manager of China operations at Australian metal mining and energy company Fortescue, noted that a major opportunity lies in connecting China's strengths with practical industrial needs across the Asia-Pacific.
Fortescue is actively working with Chinese partners on battery-electric mining equipment and integrated green grids to decarbonize its operations in Australia's Pilbara region.
This strategy was echoed by BHP, an Australian multinational mining and metals corporation, which sees vast potential in deepening its two-decade-long cooperation with China beyond traditional commodity trade.
Catherine Raw, chief development officer of BHP, highlighted the company's partnership with leading Chinese steelmakers to develop lower-emissions blast furnaces and carbon capture solutions.
China brings a strong problem-solving mindset and the ability to scale solutions quickly, which, combined with BHP's resources, creates real potential to solve shared decarbonization challenges, she said.
This accelerating cross-border integration is already evident in macroeconomic metrics.
China's trade in high-tech products with other APEC economies surged 50.3 percent year-on-year to reach 6.66 trillion yuan ($997.34 billion) in the first three quarters, exceeding the total for the whole of last year.
The country's imports of electronic components from other APEC economies increased 56 percent during the same period, according to data from the General Administration of Customs.
Henry Liu, vice-president for Asia-Pacific operations at Lubrizol, a global provider of specialty chemicals in the United States, said China's comprehensive industry infrastructure serves an exceptional range of applications where customer needs are first tested and validated.
As other APEC economies industrialize, matching products and supply chains refined in China with local requirements can significantly shorten the time taken to reach the market, he said.
Liu emphasized that China's "AI plus" initiative and proven green technologies allow neighboring economies to adopt established, commercially competitive solutions.
This enables a faster, more economic development path with lower investment requirements and faster deployment at scale.
This surge in technological exchange is also transforming the region's aviation and logistics infrastructure.
Cathay Group, a travel lifestyle brand based in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, is leveraging the mainland's artificial intelligence advancements by deploying over 120 AI models to optimize aircraft scheduling, personalize customer experiences and manage complex cargo requirements.
Arnold Cheng, Cathay Group director for the Chinese mainland, said that AI-assisted screening is proving particularly vital for Cathay in identifying shipments that may require additional import or export license checks.
To advance the green transition, Cathay fully supports the development of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area's competitive sustainable aviation fuel value chain, which is vital for the aviation sector to reach net-zero emissions, he said.
Similarly, ContiTech, one of the world's leading industrial specialists for material solutions, is applying its advanced materials expertise to support expanding AI infrastructure across the region.
The company is developing highly efficient liquid cooling systems for data centers, meeting the growing infrastructure demands of the digital economy.
Concurrently, ContiTech is expanding its localized industrial portfolio and developing high-pressure hydrogen refueling solutions in China to serve the broader Asia-Pacific market. Ultimately, capitalizing on these diverse trends requires a commitment to local adaptation.
