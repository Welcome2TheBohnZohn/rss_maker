Chinese star Lang Ping gets volleyball's highest honor

ARTICLE TEXT:
China's volleyball legend Lang Ping, who was awarded the Volleyball Order on Saturday, dedicated the honor to those who helped mold her remarkable career as congratulatory messages poured in.
The 65-year-old former Chinese player and coach received the Volleyball Order, the highest recognition of the sport's governing body, FIVB, at the 40th FIVB World Congress in Punta Cana, Dominican Republic.
Lang, a legend of the sport who has won Olympic gold medals as a player and coach, was long considered a top contender for the top FIVB award, and yet remained humble in her acceptance speech, attributing her success to teamwork.
"When I accepted this important award, I felt all my coaches, former teammates, players and partners I have worked with were standing on the podium with me," Lang shared on her Weibo account early on Sunday morning.
"I started playing volleyball in Beijing when I was 14. From my earliest training on the court, to putting on the Chinese national jersey, and then to coaching overseas, I have walked this path with volleyball for 50 years. Every step of this journey, I have traveled with a team," she said.
"None of my dreams could have come true without a group of like-minded people striving with me."
Looking ahead, Lang expressed her hope of seeing "many more women coaches stand on this stage and more people around the world get to fall in love with volleyball".
Known as the "Iron Hammer" in the volleyball world for her stellar playing career as a ferocious outside hitter, Lang spearheaded China's rise on the sport's global stage in the early 1980s, powering the Chinese women's squad with her explosive strikes and exceptional leadership to two World Cup crowns, a world championship trophy and the gold medal at the Los Angeles Olympics in 1984.
During the run of international titles, Lang and her teammates triggered a nationwide volleyball craze, turning her nickname into a timeless symbol of grit, unity and fighting spirit, which ignited the nation during a trying time and forged the core value of China's sporting endeavors in the decades that followed.
After retiring as an athlete, she built an international coaching resume rarely matched in the sport's history, taking the helm of the Chinese national team in 1995 and guiding her squad to a runner-up finish at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics.
Lang then expanded her influence overseas. She accepted an offer in 2005 to take charge of the United States women's team, after a successful stint with multiple Italian clubs at the league level, and led Team USA to a silver-medal finish at the Beijing Olympics in 2008.
Lang's coaching career peaked with her second spell at the helm of Team China, where she revived the struggling program and nurtured a group of talented young stars, such as Zhu Ting and Hui Ruoqi, who went in as pre-tournament underdogs, to a gold-medal finish at the 2016 Rio Olympics.
By immersing herself in different systems of the sport, Lang stood out as a genuine global ambassador for not just volleyball but also female empowerment in sports, freely exchanging ideas and bridging diverse cultures.
"Congratulations, coach Lang, on receiving the FIVB Volleyball Order. From you, we see how the spirit of Chinese women's volleyball can be passed down and take root globally, as well as the wider possibilities open to women," said the now retired Hui, captain of Lang's 2016 Olympic champion team.
"I hope your two cherished visions come to fruition: more female coaches stepping onto the world stage and volleyball drawing participants from all walks of life."
The Volleyball Order recognizes individuals who have made an exceptional and lasting contribution to the growth and advancement of volleyball and beach volleyball worldwide.
The FIVB described Lang as "a global volleyball icon whose achievements as both a player and coach have inspired generations".
