Wuzhong's morning tea offers a savory warmup

ARTICLE TEXT:
A bowl of beef noodles, lamb offal soup, plates of braised meat, freshly fried pastries, dumplings and an assortment of cold dishes — breakfast in Wuzhong is anything but a simple meal.
In Wuzhong, a city in Northwest China's Ningxia Hui autonomous region, tables are often filled with so many dishes that choosing where to begin can be a challenge.
For Iraqi food vlogger and actor Ahmed Jaber, better known by his Chinese name Wang Lixuan, one local specialty proved particularly memorable.
After trying hubo, a dish made by cutting flatbread into strips and stir-frying it with meat and seasonings, he was impressed by its chewy, tender texture and rich aroma.
"Really satisfying. This is already on another level in terms of flavor," he said, after enjoying his meal at a local joint in Wuzhong. "I'll have another small portion, with some extra meat. The flavor is simply incredible."
Sunday marked xiao xue, or Minor Snow, the 20th of China's"24 solar terms". As the weather turns colder, Wuzhong's morning tea offers a warming way to start the day.
With steaming bowls of noodles, local meat dishes and bowls of Babao, or Eight Treasures, tea, Wuzhong's breakfast culture is hearty and down-to-earth, offering a robust alternative to the delicate dim sum of Cantonese morning tea.
At the heart of the experience is Babao tea, traditionally served in a lidded bowl known as a gaiwan. Tealeaves form the base, while ingredients such as goji berries, red dates, sesame seeds, rose petals and walnuts add layers of flavor. Recipes vary from one establishment to another, and the tea is often refilled with hot water several times.
The tea is meant to be enjoyed slowly. As the ingredients gradually release their flavors, each infusion brings a slightly different taste. The lid is used to gently brush aside the tea leaves and foam as people sip.
Food is just as central to the experience. Wuzhong-style beef noodles are known for their rich, savory broth, made by simmering beef and beef bones.
According to Hu Jiandong, a local researcher, the emphasis is on the beef itself, with the meat and broth served alongside the noodles.
Lamb is another staple. Hand-grabbed lamb and lamb offal soup are common choices, while braised beef offers another meaty option. Local pastries add variety to the table, including youxiang, a fried dough snack, and flatbread flavored with locally grown perilla seeds. Fresh vegetables, sometimes served as simple cold dishes, provide a lighter contrast to the richer meat dishes.
For Hu, the distinction between an ordinary breakfast and morning tea lies as much in the pace as in the food.
"An ordinary breakfast is about eating quickly and getting on with the day. Morning tea means sitting down, having several dishes and pastries, and taking your time over a bowl of tea," he said.
Although drinking tea at home has long been part of local life, gathering in restaurants for morning tea has become more established over the past decade. Older residents are often among the regular customers on weekdays, while weekends bring younger diners and families together.
As Minor Snow settles in and the mornings grow colder, more people come in for hot soup, hot tea and hot food, Hu said. The end of the autumn harvest also gives residents more time to enjoy a leisurely breakfast.
