TITLE:
Xi hailed for bold vision on APEC progress

ARTICLE TEXT:
Amid ongoing geopolitical tensions and fragmentation, President Xi Jinping's emphasis on openness and economic cooperation, delivered in his speech at the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Economic Leaders' Meeting on Thursday in Shenzhen, Guangdong province, carries far-reaching global implications and could provide significant impetus for globalization, international experts said.
In the speech, Xi called for building an Asia-Pacific community characterized by solidarity and mutual assistance, openness and integration, enterprise and innovation, as well as inclusive development.
China is an advocate, promoter and forerunner of Asia-Pacific economic cooperation, and it is ready to work with all parties to pool wisdom and synergy in building the four key pillars of an Asia-Pacific community, Xi said. He called for strengthening the pillar of economy and trade, upgrading the pillar of connectivity, fostering the pillar of innovation and enhancing the pillar of development.
Jon R. Taylor, chair of the Department of Political Science and Geography at the University of Texas at San Antonio in the United States, said that Xi's speech offers a constructive framework for regional cooperation.
Taylor noted that the four key pillars proposed by Xi for an Asia-Pacific community present a compelling vision for open regionalism, deeper economic integration and broader cooperation. These are hopeful signs that could spur greater efforts toward closer alignment of regional trade agreements, trade digitalization and reduced investment barriers, he said.
"These sectors offer a rare combination of strong economic incentives and manageable political risks, making them the most likely areas where Xi's proposals can be translated into tangible results," he added.
Adhere Cavince, a Kenyan scholar of international relations with a focus on China-Africa development cooperation, said the pillars outlined by President Xi could "significantly drive open trade, stable supply chains and a rules-based multilateral system".
"Beyond the Asia-Pacific region, President Xi's remarks carry significant implications for Africa," Cavince said, noting that Xi's emphasis on economic and technological cooperation geared toward developing economies and infrastructure connectivity offers tangible channels for technology transfer, industrial upgrading and market access.
"For the global economy, China casts itself as a stabilizer upholding (World Trade Organization) rules and predictability amid fragmentation," he said, emphasizing that China's proposals have the potential to reinforce globalization.
Dicky Budiman, a senior researcher at both YARSI University in Indonesia and Griffith University in Australia, said the APEC meeting presents an important opportunity to reposition regional cooperation toward a more integrated, resilient and sustainable Asia-Pacific community.
"President Xi's emphasis on solidarity, openness, innovation and inclusive development provides a relevant strategic foundation for addressing increasingly interconnected global challenges," he said.
In his speech, Xi announced that in line with the needs of Asia-Pacific cooperation, China will set up cooperation institutions in areas such as digital technology, cross-border data flow, clean energy, and the development of small and medium-sized enterprises to contribute to innovation-driven and green growth in the Asia-Pacific region.
Lawrence Loh, a professor and director of National University of Singapore's Centre for Governance and Sustainability, said, "President Xi has articulated a bold vision outlining the next steps of continued progress for the Asia-Pacific community.
"With China's posture as a responsible global node for connectivity and innovation, Asia-Pacific economies stand to gain by actively participating in the emerging series of newly enabled collaborative initiatives," he said.
Luigi Gambardella, president of ChinaEU, a Brussels-based nonprofit international association that promotes Sino-European business cooperation in the digital sector, said that Xi's commitment to regional openness presents an important opportunity for Europe.
"By connecting our respective industrial strengths and innovation ecosystems, we can create new sources of growth and competitiveness on both sides," Gambardella said. "The message from Shenzhen is powerful: Governments may compete, but their economies must remain connected."
He added that "the significance of President Xi's speech lies not only in articulating a vision for the Asia-Pacific community, but also in proposing a concrete architecture to translate that vision into reality".
Xu Weiwei in Hong Kong and Xing Yi in London contributed to this story.
