TITLE:
Pakistani astronaut to be first foreigner aboard Tiangong

ARTICLE TEXT:
China's Tiangong space station is poised to welcome its first foreign visitor — Pakistani astronaut Muhammad Zeeshan Ali — who will also become the first Pakistani citizen to reach Earth's orbit.
The wing commander in the Pakistan Air Force is set to fly to the massive orbital outpost with two Chinese astronauts — Major General Tang Hongbo, commander of the Shenzhou XXIV mission, and Colonel Wu Yi, the crew's spaceflight engineer.
According to Zhang Jingbo, a senior engineer and spokesman for the China Manned Space Agency, their spacecraft is scheduled to lift off aboard a Long March 2F rocket at 8:18 am on Wednesday from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwestern China.
At a news conference on Tuesday morning at the Jiuquan spaceport, Zhang told reporters that after the Shenzhou XXIV spacecraft reaches its preset orbital position, it will activate its rapid autonomous rendezvous and docking mode, taking about three-and-a-half hours to approach and dock with the front port of the Tianhe core module.
After several days of handover work, the Shenzhou XXIV crew will take over the space station from the crew members of the Shenzhou XXIII mission, who would have spent six months in orbit, Zhang said.
Tang and his crewmates are tasked with maintaining the station's routine operations; carrying out repairs and regular inspections to ensure its safe, reliable and efficient operation; advancing scientific research and applications; and participating in science communication, educational and public welfare activities.
Ali will undertake a short-duration spaceflight as a payload specialist, Zhang said.
"While in orbit, in addition to taking part in the crew's routine work, he will primarily be responsible for China-Pakistan joint space science experiments, focusing on installing experiment samples and units and conducting in-orbit tests," he said.
After the handover, Colonel Zhu Yangzhu, commander of the Shenzhou XXIII mission, will remain aboard the station to complete China's first yearlong orbital mission. The Pakistani astronaut will return to Earth with the other two Shenzhou XXIII crewmembers, Zhang said.
Orbital partnership
In February 2025, the China Manned Space Agency and Pakistan's Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission signed an agreement in the Pakistani capital of Islamabad, launching bilateral cooperation in the process of selecting and training Pakistani astronauts.
Following a rigorous three-stage selection process — preliminary, secondary and final — Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud, both Pakistani military pilots, emerged from a pool of roughly 20 finalists selected from an initial group of aviators and scientists. Candidates were assessed for medical fitness, psychological resilience and their ability to withstand g-forces, according to officials with the Pakistani commission.
The two men arrived in Beijing in April for intensive training at the Astronaut Center of China.
Zhang said the joint mission "marks another landmark achievement of bilateral cooperation as China and Pakistan celebrate the 75th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic ties, and a powerful testament to the time-honored friendship between the two nations".
On major international social media platforms, many users identifying themselves as Pakistani nationals expressed anticipation, excitement and joy, and offered their blessings. Many also thanked China for including a Pakistani astronaut in its spaceflight mission.
For Tang, the mission will mark another professional milestone: He will become the first astronaut to have participated in three missions aboard the Tiangong space station.
During his first spaceflight, the three-month Shenzhou XII mission launched in June 2021, the Hunan native became one of the first inhabitants of the Chinese space station.
Two years later, he commanded the six-month-long Shenzhou XVII mission, becoming the first astronaut to return to Tiangong.
