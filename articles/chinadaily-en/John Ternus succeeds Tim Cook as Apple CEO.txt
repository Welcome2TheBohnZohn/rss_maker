John Ternus succeeds Tim Cook as Apple CEO
SAN FRANCISCO-- Apple Inc's leadership transition took effect Tuesday, with John Ternus succeeding Tim Cook as chief executive officer and Cook becoming executive chairman of the company's board of directors.
Apple's updated leadership page lists Ternus as CEO and a member of the board, while Cook is now listed as executive chair.
Apple announced the succession plan on April 20, saying the transition had been unanimously approved by its board and followed a long-term succession planning process. Cook remained CEO through the summer and worked closely with Ternus on the transition. As executive chairman, Cook will continue to assist with certain aspects of the company, including engagement with policymakers around the world.
Cook, who joined Apple in 1998, became CEO in 2011 and led the company for about 15 years. During his tenure, Apple introduced new product categories including Apple Watch, AirPods and Apple Vision Pro, as well as services ranging from iCloud and Apple Pay to Apple TV and Apple Music.
Apple said in April that under Cook's leadership, its market capitalization had grown from approximately 350 billion US dollars to 4 trillion dollars, while annual revenue had risen from 108 billion dollars in fiscal 2011 to more than 416 billion dollars in fiscal 2025.
In a public letter to the Apple community, Cook reflected on his 15 years as CEO and said the transition was "not goodbye." He described Ternus as a brilliant engineer and thinker who had spent 25 years helping build Apple products.
Ternus joined Apple's product design team in 2001, became a vice president of Hardware Engineering in 2013 and joined the executive team as senior vice president of Hardware Engineering in 2021.
He has overseen hardware engineering across a range of Apple products and played a key role in the introduction of iPad and AirPods, as well as multiple generations of iPhone, Mac and Apple Watch.
In his April statement, Ternus said he had spent almost his entire career at Apple, had worked under Steve Jobs and had Cook as his mentor. He pledged to lead the company according to the values and vision that have defined Apple for half a century.
