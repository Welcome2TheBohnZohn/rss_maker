One language, countless possibilities

ARTICLE TEXT:
Dressed in traditional qipao and sporting plush panda charms, young people from around the world greeted one another with a cheerful ni hao (hello) as they gathered at the China National Convention Center in Beijing.
Some were meeting for the first time, while others were catching up with old friends, sharing stories about their studies, career plans and experiences in China.
Among them was Edward John O'Brien III, a 27-year-old from the United States who began learning Chinese at 13. He earned a master's degree in international trade from Southwestern University of Finance and Economics in Chengdu, Sichuan province, and has lived in China for more than two years.
"Learning Chinese has given me many opportunities to make the most of my strengths in self-expression," he said. "I plan to set my sights on media production, cultural exchange projects, and social media content creation in China."
O'Brien was among about 5,000 participants from more than 160 countries and regions attending the 2026 World Chinese Language Conference, held in Beijing from Nov 13 to 15 under the theme "Chinese: More Than a Language".
Hosted by China's Ministry of Education, the conference brought together education officials, scholars, teachers and business representatives for eight specialized forums exploring the future of international Chinese education and global cooperation.
At the conference's plenary session, Minister of Education Huai Jinpeng highlighted the growing global significance of Chinese, not only as a means of communication but also as a driver of economic development and a bridge between civilizations.
Global interest in Chinese continued to grow this year, with the number of people overseas learning and using the language reaching 210 million. Meanwhile, participation in the Chinese Proficiency Test (HSK), a standardized assessment of Chinese language proficiency, exceeded 1 million for the first time in 2026, according to figures released at the World Chinese Language Conference.
For young learners, these figures reflect possibilities that extend far beyond the classroom.
The Chinese Bridge competition has helped young learners turn their language studies into meaningful international exchanges. This year marked the 25th anniversary of the event, which has attracted more than 1.8 million young participants from over 160 countries and regions.
Cameron Patterson of the United Kingdom took second place in the 14th Chinese Bridge competition in 2015.
"Learning a language is not just about mastering a tool for communication. More importantly, it allows us to build connections, participate in different cultural activities, and encounter people and experiences we might otherwise never have known," he said.
Over the years, Patterson has witnessed growing interest in Chinese language and culture in the UK, with more schools incorporating Chinese into their curricula.
Beyond cultural exchange and personal growth, Chinese proficiency is also becoming a valuable professional asset, particularly in countries with expanding economic ties to China.
For Moussa Ousmane Mahaman Sani, a master's student from Niger at the China University of Petroleum (Beijing), learning Chinese could open doors to a career in his country's energy sector.
Energy cooperation between China and Niger has expanded in recent years. In 2024, Niger signed a memorandum of understanding with China National Petroleum Corp (CNPC) concerning crude oil sales from the Agadem oilfield.
"We have deep cooperation with China," Mahaman Sani said. "If I can speak Chinese, I gain a competitive edge in my home country's job market."
As he prepares to complete his master's degree in 2027, Sani is considering further study and career paths that would allow him to contribute to China-Niger energy partnerships.
