The China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation (CASC) said on Friday that immediately after the disaster, it coordinated and deployed satellite resources to conduct emergency communication support and imaging of the quake-hit area.
The CASC said it has activated multiple high-throughput satellite internet emergency service lines for the Ministry of Emergency Management, local emergency management authorities, and major telecom operators. Additional service line terminals are on their way to the quake-hit area.

BEIJING -- The 7.0-magnitude earthquake that struck Baqen county of the city of Nagqu in Southwest China's Xizang autonomous region on Thursday was an extensional rupture, according to experts with the China Earthquake Networks Center (CENC).
The quake occurred at around 10:33 pm, causing heavy casualties. Focal mechanism solutions show the event was basically consistent with regional tectonic characteristics.
"An extensional fracture, in simple terms, occurs when the crust is stretched horizontally and, under tension and gravity, faultage slides downward along existing faultage, forming fractures and rupture surfaces," said Han Yanyan, a senior engineer with the earthquake prediction department of the CENC.
The CENC explained that the collision between the Indian and Eurasian plates had caused the uplift of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, crustal shortening and thickening, and widespread plateau deformation. These processes are the main dynamic mechanism controlling tectonic deformation in the western part of the Chinese mainland.
The interior of the plateau is subjected to both north-south compression and east-west extension, and two typical fault systems, trending roughly north-south and east-west, have developed there.
"The epicenter is located within the Changtang block of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, about 2 km from the nearest fault, the Baqen-Riwoqe fault," Han said.
Under such intense crustal deformation, the Changtang block and its surrounding fault zones are particularly active. Since 1900, the block has experienced six earthquakes of magnitude 7 or above, including this one. The largest was a 7.5-magnitude earthquake in Shuanghu on Nov 8, 1997.
"Therefore, this earthquake was an energy release of the Changtang block," Han said.
Authorities in Nagchu city, Xizang autonomous region, warned at a Friday news conference that aftershocks and secondary disasters remain major risks following a magnitude-7.0 earthquake in Drachen county on Thursday night.
The quake had killed 16 people and injured 76, said Dondrub, mayor of Nagchu.
The affected area averages nearly 4,800 meters in elevation, according to Meng Linghe, Drachen's Party secretary. Landslides, slope collapses, and falling rocks are the main secondary geological hazards. Low nighttime temperatures, scattered snow showers, and black ice on shaded high-altitude roads pose risks to rescue vehicles.
Dondrub said inspections have covered 12 roads spanning more than 1,700 kilometers, 201 bridges, and 324 culverts. Officials identified 11 hazards and cleared rockfalls at seven road locations.
Earthquake, meteorological, and emergency management departments are consulting and issuing aftershock and weather warnings, while police and transport authorities keep emergency routes open.
Round-the-clock monitoring and emergency repairs cover mountain slopes, reservoirs, power stations, river channels, unsafe buildings, and telecommunications base stations. Authorities are inspecting mountain slopes and roads alongside rescue teams, Meng said. Dangerous areas will be cordoned off, and search operations will be kept away from steep slopes.
Authorities will inspect geological hazard sites and vulnerable infrastructure hourly and continue issuing updated risk alerts, Dondrub said.

Level III is the second-lowest level in the ministry's four-tier response system, with Level I being the highest. Xizang's transport department activated its own Level II response after the earthquake.

Surveillance footage shared by local media showed buildings shaking violently, with goods tumbling from shelves as residents rushed outside to escape the quake on Thursday night. In Maru township, one of the hardest-hit areas, the earthquake cracked the ground and caused sections of road to sink, China Central Television reported.
"Suddenly, there was a tremendous rumbling. For a moment, it felt as if hundreds of yaks were running at once," said Dzondru Tseltrim, a resident of Garuthang village in the county.
His family had already gone to bed when the shaking began. Woken by the tremors, he heard cabinets and wooden pillars creaking inside the house. He rushed to wake his father, in his 80s, and helped him outside. Other family members soon followed.
Fearing aftershocks, the family decided not to sleep indoors that night. They arranged for his father to rest in a car while the others prepared to spend the night in the courtyard.
