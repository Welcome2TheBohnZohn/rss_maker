TITLE:
Chinese, Russian, US heads of state to hold landmark talks

ARTICLE TEXT:
President Xi Jinping, Russian President Vladimir Putin and US President Donald Trump will meet in Shenzhen, South China's Guangdong province, on Thursday for the first-ever face-to-face summit of the three countries' heads of state, a groundbreaking gathering that experts said could boost global stability, dialogue and cooperation.
Speaking at a regular news briefing on Monday, Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning said the three leaders will have an in-depth exchange of views on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability in major-country relations.
China, Russia and the United States are all founding members of the United Nations and permanent members of the UN Security Council, Mao noted, adding that in history, the three countries fought side by side to secure victory in the World Anti-Fascist War.
Under the current circumstances, the three countries shoulder important responsibilities to work together to address global challenges and promote world peace and stability, she said.
Strengthening communication, managing differences and stabilizing relations serve the interests of all three countries and meet the expectations of the international community, Mao added, saying that such efforts would help stabilize the global situation and advance dialogue and cooperation.
The trilateral summit could send a strong political signal of stability in major-country relations, bolstering international confidence in the prospects for global development, experts said.
Wang Yiwei, a professor at Renmin University of China's School of International Studies, said that the summit will demonstrate again the irreplaceable role of head-of-state diplomacy in providing strategic guidance.
Wang said China does not subscribe to the logic that the world can be run by major countries, but advocates stronger coordination and cooperation among them. In seeking to reform and improve global governance and build a community with a shared future for humanity, China champions an equal and orderly multipolar world and greater democracy in international relations, he added.
Konstantin Kosachev, deputy speaker of Russia's Federation Council, said the trilateral summit would carry significant symbolic weight, reflecting recognition of a multipolar world in which no single power can address all global challenges alone.
In an interview published by Russia's weekly newspaper Argumenty i Fakty on Saturday, he cautioned against describing the summit as a "new Yalta" — a reference to a major conference in 1945 of the leaders of the United States, the United Kingdom and the Soviet Union — saying he expected discussions to focus on coordinating interests and preventing confrontation rather than dividing the world into spheres of influence.
Gao Fei, president of China Foreign Affairs University, said China's comprehensive strategic partnership of coordination for a new era with Russia and its efforts to stabilize ties with the US helped create favorable conditions for the summit.
Peace and development are likely to be key topics, Gao said. With global growth weak and industrial and supply chains under pressure, the three countries could explore cooperation to advance openness and development and give fresh impetus to the world economy.
Zhang Tengjun, an associate research fellow at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences' Institute of American Studies, said trilateral dialogue offers value that bilateral talks alone could not provide. A third country's participation could encourage each side to look beyond bilateral gains and losses, reassess one another's interests and concerns, and clarify strategic expectations.
The format also offers an opportunity to explore more stable relations among major countries beyond bloc confrontation, Zhang said. Such dialogue would respond to widespread expectations that major powers should play a constructive role, a view held particularly among countries that want neither a return to Cold War-style divisions nor pressure to choose sides.
Possible progress could include agreement in principle on addressing shared security challenges, support for political solutions to regional flashpoints, and greater clarity about each country's concerns and red lines, he said. Such exchanges could help establish a framework for managing relations and reduce the risk of strategic miscalculation.
Zhang added that although the summit won't resolve all the challenges facing the international community overnight, holding it is nevertheless a positive and necessary step.
