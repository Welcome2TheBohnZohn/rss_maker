Major storm batters US on holiday

ARTICLE TEXT:
WASHINGTON — A powerful storm disrupted Thanksgiving celebrations in the United States on Thursday, as New York's famed Macy's parade was postponed for the first time in its 100-year history.
Heavy rain and strong winds from a nor'easter battered the central and eastern United States, creating treacherous travel conditions during one of the country's busiest holiday periods.
As families gathered for traditional holiday feasts, nearly a million homes and businesses were without power, with Pennsylvania, Maryland and Virginia particularly hard hit, according to tracking website PowerOutage.
At airports, more than 400 flights were canceled and over 2,000 were delayed, mostly along the East Coast, figures from FlightAware showed.
New York's famed parade, which sees brightly colored balloons travel through the heart of Manhattan, was pushed back to Saturday due to what organizers said was out of an "abundance of caution".
Millions of people typically gather to catch a glimpse of the festivities. The only other time the parade was not held on Thanksgiving Thursday was when it was canceled for three years during World War II.
While flooding was reported up and down the East Coast, impacts were also felt inland, with powerful gusts and inundated soil leading to trees falling onto power lines and homes.
Authorities in the Maryland suburbs of the US capital, worried about the impact of power outages on the water treatment network, asked nearly two million customers to conserve tap water consumption.
Officials urged people to limit road travel, while several major bridges were closed due to high winds.
New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill, who declared a state of emergency on Wednesday over the weather, urged residents to take precautions to stay safe.
"Keep your devices charged, report power outages directly to your utility provider, and stay away from downed power lines," she wrote on X.
The National Weather Service warned that the powerful coastal storm would bring strong winds, heavy rainfall and significant coastal flooding to East Coast of the US through Friday.
The East Coast disruption came as California also suffered weather woes this week with massive waves battering the coast, prompting evacuations in some areas and soaking San Francisco.
A series of Pacific storms is expected to continue to affect the West Coast into next week, bringing rain, mountain snow, strong winds, coastal flooding and high surf.
