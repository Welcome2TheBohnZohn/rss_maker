TITLE:
Bird-watchers flock to symphony of swirling ducks

ARTICLE TEXT:
A dark smudge appears above the horizon, then it becomes a line, and eventually a massive flying wave. Within seconds, the sky above Shandong Yellow River Delta National Nature Reserve appears to be alive, swelling and swirling with more than a quarter of a million birds flying as if they share a single nervous system.
British birdwatcher Paul Holt has watched birds for more than 50 years. He has seen intricate murmurations in Europe and Asia. But standing on the mudflat at dusk, he does not try to rank this one.
"The flock gets up and they move about, and they don't crash into each other," he said. "It's amazing. It's really spectacular."
The birds are Baikal teals, small ducks with patterned faces, and they are the swell driving the "bird wave". The reserve's monitoring put the single species flock at over 280,000 on Monday — a record for the site. Zhao Yajie, deputy director of the reserve's ecological monitoring center, has watched the number of ducks climb over the years from a few thousand, to 40,000, to 170,000, and now past 200,000.
The ducks roost in the reserve's wetlands during the day and fly out to feed along the riverbanks at night. At dawn and dusk, they move between the two, and the crossing is what visitors come to see. The flight lasts 15 to 25 minutes, sometimes less.
Holt first came to Dongying years ago and has returned four times. He calls the Yellow River Delta one of the most spectacular birdwatching sites he has seen.
