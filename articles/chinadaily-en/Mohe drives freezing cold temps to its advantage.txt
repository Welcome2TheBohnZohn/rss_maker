TITLE:
Mohe drives freezing cold temps to its advantage

ARTICLE TEXT:
As temperatures drop in Mohe, Heilongjiang province, test vehicles from major manufacturers make a beeline for the city, allowing automakers to push the cars to their limits on the icy test tracks.
Mohe, the northernmost city in China, experiences temperatures as low as -53 C and offers arctic-like test conditions for about seven months of the year, attracting engineers from automotive companies across the country to conduct extreme cold environment tests on new vehicles and components.
Every winter, Li Chuang, a cold-weather testing engineer at FAW-Volkswagen Automotive Co Ltd, leads his team to conduct durability tests in Mohe.
"The testing period in Mohe can last until April, providing an extended period of extreme cold weather to fully simulate various extreme low-temperature conditions and complete vehicle and component durability validation," he said. "Moreover, the complete infrastructure and detailed local support services also attract us to return every winter."
"Mohe's cold region automobile testing base is fully equipped with charging stations and dedicated workshops, and site maintenance is well-managed. From handling test-vehicle permits to responding to daily needs, everything is coordinated well, allowing us to focus entirely on vehicle testing."
The unique conditions have been transformed into an industrial advantage, allowing the city to boost economic development.
Data from the local government show that during the 2025-26 testing season, Mohe attracted 75 companies, over 1,600 test vehicles and more than 2,400 technical personnel.
This directly provided over 200 seasonal local job opportunities and boosted revenue in the catering, accommodation and repair industries by 70 million yuan ($10.5 million).
The number of car tests continues to rise, establishing the country's northernmost city as a natural extreme cold laboratory for automotive technology iteration.
Li Yumei, manager of Mohe Peninsula Holiday Hotel, said before the rise of the car-testing industry, the hotel mainly relied on revenue from tourist traffic, which was strong during the winter ice and snow season but primarily consisted of short-term visitors.
"Now, every testing season, a large number of automotive engineers stay for months," she said. "The stable, long-term guest base complements the ice and snow tourism traffic, providing us with two supporting pillars. The overall revenue has significantly increased compared to the past."
The thriving hotel industry is supported by a comprehensive government service system.
The local government has established a public welfare institution in coordination with the city's bureau of industry and information technology to advance industrial services support.
"We have focused on the cold region testing development track, providing precise full-chain services for companies settling here, and continuously nurturing a fertile ground for industrial development," said Wang Dongling, director of the bureau.
The local government has also set up a dedicated WeChat group for automotive companies, implemented a 24-hour response mechanism for car companies' needs, and promoted on-site, immediate processing of test vehicle permits to cut waiting times and operating costs.
"During the summer period, we comprehensively surveyed and integrated citywide catering, accommodation and maintenance support resources, compiling and distributing service manuals to provide clear guidance for automotive companies," Wang said.
The local government also regularly organizes forums and coordinates with companies to address various issues, such as site scheduling, safety training, road support and approvals, providing a one-stop service for processing, site coordination and logistics support.
Meanwhile, Mohe is looking beyond automotive testing to broaden the application scenarios of extreme cold testing.
Currently, the city hosts six permanent testing institutions, and during the 2025-26 testing season, it undertook testing for over 150 companies, completing low-temperature performance testing of more than 40,000 products.
