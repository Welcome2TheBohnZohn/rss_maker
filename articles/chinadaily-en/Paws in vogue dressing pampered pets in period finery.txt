TITLE:
Paws in vogue: dressing pampered pets in period finery

ARTICLE TEXT:
Liuliu's wardrobe can put many fashionistas to shame. She owns dozens of traditional Chinese outfits — from flowing skirts to elaborate cloud-collared hanfu — and even gets personal fittings whenever a new design comes off the sewing table.
The mixed British Shorthair cat is the star model of Chunfeng Daxiaoqiao, an online shop specializing in traditional Chinese clothing for pets.
The shop's 32-year-old founder, who goes by the name Qiu Qiao, designs every outfit herself — drawing inspiration from China's cultural heritage — and makes sure comfort comes first. If Liuliu cannot move freely in an outfit, it will not be offered for sale, she said.
"Pets are family to me. They are a responsibility. I want to protect them and give them the best life possible, because they bring me happiness and companionship," Qiu Qiao added.
At her shop, traditional Chinese outfits such as the mamianqun (horse-face skirt) and yunjian (cloud-shaped collar) have been reimagined for four-legged customers, with every piece of clothing tailored to allow them to walk, sit and play uninhibitedly.
"We want people to recognize the cultural characteristics of traditional Chinese clothing while ensuring that the outfits remain comfortable for pets to wear," Qiu Qiao said.
Her very first original design, titled When the Lychees Are Ripe, was inspired by Yang Yuhuan — better known as Yang Guifei — the celebrated consort of Emperor Xuanzong of the Tang Dynasty (618-907) and one of the Four Beauties in China. Taking this as a starting point, some of her collections draw on the legendary beauties, translating historical imagery into elaborate pet costumes.
The Chunfeng Daxiaoqiao brand name also carries a literary echo, drawing inspiration from a line by the Southern Song Dynasty (1127-1279) poet Xin Qiji about the legendary beauty of the two Qiao sisters.
Qiu Qiao's entrepreneurial journey began when she became disillusioned with office life in Beijing and Qingdao, Shandong province.
In 2019, after rescuing a stray kitten, she decided to combine her love of animals with her interest in traditional Chinese culture. She quit her job, returned to her hometown in Hunan province, and launched the online business — with her cat Liuliu as its first model.
"In the past, pet clothing was largely about keeping animals warm or providing basic protection. Today, consumers are also looking for distinctive designs, quality materials and outfits that reflect their own tastes," she said.
Pet birthdays, weddings and themed photo shoots have created further demand, as owners increasingly see clothing as a way to celebrate their furry companions and create lasting memories.
For Liu Yaoru, another pet entrepreneur, the opportunity came from a different problem — finding clothes that actually fit her greyhound's slender frame. She turned to her family's clothing business for women and asked factory tailors to make outfits for her dog based on her own designs.
"I posted some photos of my dog wearing these clothes on Xiaohongshu. I didn't expect so many people to ask where they could get the same outfits, or whether I could make something similar for their dogs," Liu recalled.
As word spread on social media and elsewhere, orders poured in. Liu now also offers upcycling, turning owners' unwanted clothing into new outfits for their pets.
The trend reflects the expansion of China's pet economy.
According to data highlighted in March at the 6th China Pet Industry Development Conference in Beijing, the nation's pet consumption market reached 312.6 billion yuan ($47 billion) by the end of 2025, up 4.1 percent year-on-year.
The pet clothing sector has emerged as a fast-growing market, with its value rising from 3.58 billion yuan in 2020 to 12.95 billion yuan in 2025, with an annual growth rate of more than 35 percent in overseas sales.
