TITLE:
Nation's opening-up fuels growth across Asia-Pacific

PUBLISHED:
2026-11-25

SOURCE:
China Daily English

CATEGORY:
Authoritative PRC Media

ARTICLE TEXT:
China's deepening opening-up and strengthening focus on innovation, advanced manufacturing and green technology are creating new development opportunities across the Asia-Pacific region, business leaders said.
Those opportunities are increasingly flowing in both directions as regional markets become more integrated, said Rose Zhu, chief country officer of Deutsche Bank China.
"For multinational companies, China remains an important market, innovation hub and part of global supply chains," Zhu said. "We see particularly strong potential along key growth corridors connecting China with other (Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation) economies, Europe and the Middle East."
At the same time, Chinese companies are increasingly looking overseas to diversify their markets, expand their global footprint and participate more deeply in international value chains, she said.
Alan Li, president of CBRE China, said, "Multinationals are no longer chasing low costs." Instead, they are seeking to tap China's advanced technology, talent pool and consumer market, while benefiting from a more open and unified business environment, he said.
New office leasing by foreign companies in major Chinese cities rose 26 percent year-on-year in the first half of 2026, the strongest increase since 2022, according to CBRE, a commercial real estate services and investment company headquartered in the United States. Demand was led by financial and professional services, up 31 percent, and consumer-sector companies, up 34 percent.
China's supply-chain strengths and talent base have continued to attract investment in advanced manufacturing, particularly in life sciences, advanced equipment and new energy vehicle components, Li said.
For multinational manufacturers, China's advantage increasingly lies in the speed at which technology can transition from development to commercial application.
"China's strong industrial ecosystem and diverse application scenarios help accelerate testing and adoption at scale," said Hendrik Kohleick, president of Henkel Asia-Pacific.
Fast-growing sectors including electric mobility, artificial intelligence, humanoid robotics and data centers are creating demand for new materials and industrial processes that improve performance, efficiency and sustainability, while consumers are seeking more personalized, high-quality and sustainable products, he said.
Henkel, a German multinational chemical and consumer goods company, sees China as both a market and an innovation hub, Kohleick said.
Its local research, manufacturing and technical-service capabilities allow solutions developed and validated in China to be deployed through regional networks, serving markets across the Asia-Pacific region with consistent quality and speed, he added.
For APEC economies with abundant renewable energy resources, Chinese technology and capital can help convert those resources into local economic opportunities, from electric vehicle supply chains to renewable power projects and data centers.
Fonterra, a New Zealand dairy cooperative owned by farmers, provides an example of how such industry links can support the low-carbon transition.
Teh-han Chow, CEO of Fonterra China, said that a China-made electric boiler installed at Fonterra's Edendale site in New Zealand had replaced coal-fired equipment, reducing the emissions from milk processing.
This project shows how cooperation across the Asia-Pacific region can draw on complementary strengths to accelerate the transition to a low-carbon economy while maintaining reliable and efficient production, he said.
