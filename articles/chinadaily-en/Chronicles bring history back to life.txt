TITLE:
Chronicles bring history back to life

ARTICLE TEXT:
In the early 20th century, geologist Ding Wenjiang made a field visit to what is now Yunnan province to survey local mineral resources. To gain a basic understanding of the region's historical heritage, he consulted a Qing Dynasty (1644-1911) chronicle of Yunnan, which quoted passages from another book that introduced metallurgy with exceptional clarity and detail.
Ding managed to track down the original book, titled The Exploitation of the Works of Nature, a centuries-old Chinese scientific classic known as Tiangong Kaiwu, and later facilitated its reprint. Since then, the long-forgotten masterpiece that systematically documents the agriculture and handicraft technologies of ancient China has gradually become known to contemporary Chinese.
Zhang Zhiqing, former executive deputy director of the National Library of China, said that local chronicles played a big role in the rediscovery of the book, adding that the story was precisely a microcosm of how local chronicles — reference books that comprehensively and systematically document the history and current state of the natural environment, politics, economy, culture and society of a given administrative region — preserve details of local history.
President Xi Jinping's consistent attention to local chronicles has greatly boosted their development, bringing continuous progress across China, Zhang noted.
In the 1980s, when Xi worked in Zhengding county of Hebei province and later in Fujian province, he paid close attention to local records.
Attracted by Zhengding's profound cultural heritage, he read the county's chronicles and other historical records thoroughly, surveyed historical sites and immersed himself in the county's culture, building a deep and comprehensive understanding of Zhengding.
In 1989, at a meeting in Ningde, Fujian, Xi noted that the first thing he did upon arrival was to read the local prefecture's and county's chronicles. He said that to grasp a region's defining features, one must first understand its history, and studying local chronicles offers a reliable way to do so.
A county's history is best recorded in its county's chronicle, while a prefecture's chronicle is even more comprehensive, recording positive as well as negative figures, Xi said. Chronicles can tell us what happened there, and we can learn from them, he added.
In 2006, when he inspected Cangnan county, Zhejiang province, to check the post-disaster recovery efforts following Super Typhoon Saomai, Xi, then secretary of the Communist Party of China Zhejiang Provincial Committee, consulted the county's chronicles and, in a meeting with local officials, read aloud historical records of typhoons that had struck the area. He urged the officials to learn from history, make science-based decisions and improve their ability to respond to typhoons and other natural disasters.
In 2014, during a visit to the Capital Museum in Beijing, Xi, who became general secretary of the CPC Central Committee in 2012, called for officials to attach equal importance to exhibition and the compilation of history in order to inspire national pride and confidence.
During the 23rd group study session of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee in 2020, Xi urged efforts to bring back to life relics collected in museums, legacies on the vast land of China and records in ancient books.
Zhang Yi, deputy head of the local literature group of the Library of Ancient Books of the National Library of China, said Xi's instructions have given a strong boost to the development of local chronicles.
A number of documents have been formulated to guide the development of local chronicles. "Consolidating and improving local chronicles" was included in the outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-30) issued in March.
Zhang Zhiqing, former National Library of China executive deputy director, said that local chronicles have a long history in China and developed into a form of local encyclopedia during the Southern Song Dynasty (1127-1279). Since then, the tradition of compiling local chronicles every 30 to 50 years under the central government's order took shape, with all regions following the same style.
With the emergence of local chronicles, ancient China's historical records came to comprise three tiers: The state had its national history; each region had its local chronicles; and each family had its genealogy, he said.
"Local chronicles are a literature form unique to China. Other countries certainly have local historical records as well, but compiling them regularly and continuously under the central government's order and in the same style is rarely seen elsewhere. This stems from China's pluralistic yet unified political landscape," he added.
Historical insight
A major function of local chronicles is to support political governance. "Historically, many local systems and customs did not emerge uniformly or remain unchanged. Policies took shape differently across regions and evolved over time, often being adapted, modified or supplemented to fit local realities. Politicians therefore need to understand how these policies evolved step by step before they can design policies that are realistic, effective and feasible, and turn historical experience into a useful reference for present-day governance," said Zhang Yi, from the Library of Ancient Books.
Moreover, details of history that can hardly be found in official history books can be traced in chronicles. For example, ancient Chinese had the habit of erecting stone tablets to commemorate notable figures. Many of them have disappeared over the long course of history, but much of their content has been recorded in chronicles and passed down.
President Xi has also stressed the importance of compiling local chronicles. At a meeting in Ningde in 1989, he said that compiling chronicles is a meaningful task, and that its significance, in plain terms, lies in helping us become people who see things clearly.
According to Liu Yongqiang, deputy director of the China Local Chronicles Work Office, since the beginning of reform and opening-up in 1978, two rounds of national efforts to compile local chronicles took place in China, with the latest round concluding in 2020.
During the two rounds, more than 10,000 provincial, city and county level chronicles were compiled, and nearly 4,000 old chronicles were collated.
The two rounds "systematically documented the history and current conditions of regions across the country, promoting cultural prosperity and laying a solid foundation for cultural construction, social development and the passing down of the national spirit", said Liu, who added that the third round is about to begin.
Xue Changlin, a member of the Sichuan Local Records Association, said that at a time of deepening reform and opening-up, profound social transformation and the wave of digital change, the third round, compared with the previous two, will be a more challenging task.
"It's not a simple continuation or comprehensive revision, but a systematic cultural project that will adapt to the development of the new era, reconstruct the local chronicle system and reflect the new face of the times," Xue said.
