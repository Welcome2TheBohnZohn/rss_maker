Nation further opens up vast mkt to imports

ARTICLE TEXT:
China is pressing ahead with efforts to expand imports, positioning its vast domestic market as an anchor of global demand and a rare source of certainty for overseas businesses as protectionist headwinds and geopolitical tensions roil international trade, government officials and experts said.
Expanding imports is a high priority for China, said Li Chenggang, China's international trade representative and vice-minister of commerce, noting that the country has remained the world's second-largest import market for 17 consecutive years and contributed to global trade growth.
Li made the remarks at an international symposium on the Export-to-China Capacity Development Report in Geneva on Wednesday, calling on trading partners to seize opportunities under the "Big Market for All: Export to China" initiative.
Launched in Shanghai in November 2025, the initiative was conceived from the perspective of China's trading partners and represents a proactive step to help them expand exports to China, Li said.
The initiative aims to create more opportunities for the world through China's own development, while strengthening collective efforts to uphold multilateralism and free trade, and address development challenges, he added.
China's growing appetite for imports is giving substance to its pledge to share market opportunities with the world, with goods imports rising 23.6 percent year-on-year to 18.82 trillion yuan ($2.82 trillion) in the first 10 months, outpacing the 17.6 percent gain in exports, according to the General Administration of Customs.
"The Chinese market is an anchor of resilience for the global economy," said Wang Xuekun, head of the Chinese Academy of International Trade and Economic Cooperation.
For global businesses, the opportunity lies in the combination of China's market scale and its evolving consumer needs, Wang said, pointing to the world's largest middle-income population, and consumer and import markets that both rank among the world's two largest.
As consumption becomes more sophisticated, China is unlocking fresh demand and creating new opportunities for investment, he added.
Li, China's international trade representative, said holding the symposium in Geneva as China marks 25 years of World Trade Organization membership reaffirmed the country's confidence and resolve to keep expanding imports.
China will carry that message to Zurich on Thursday, where the Ministry of Commerce and the Swiss-Chinese Chamber of Commerce will jointly host an "Export to China" event to help Swiss businesses tap the Chinese market.
The outreach builds on substantial commercial ties. China is already Switzerland's largest trading partner in Asia, while bilateral trade surged 127 percent year-on-year to $89 billion in the first nine months of 2026, according to the Ministry of Commerce.
The strong trade figures come as the two countries seek to turn their complementary economic strengths into broader opportunities through deeper market opening, with the Zurich event taking place nearly a month after they signed a protocol to upgrade their free trade agreement in Beijing in late October.
Under the upgraded agreement, zero-tariff treatment will ultimately cover more than 99 percent of bilateral goods trade, while further opening in services and investment will give businesses greater access to each other's markets, the ministry said.
That wider opening promises fresh opportunities for Swiss companies already betting on the Chinese market's long-term potential.
"China is one of our most important markets in the world and is strategically important for our international business," said Rick Kozloski, president of the China unit of Swiss eye care company Alcon.
Patients' growing expectations for better vision after treatment are sustaining demand for increasingly sophisticated eye care products and services, he said.
