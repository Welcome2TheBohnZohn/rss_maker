More steps in place to meet the goal of common prosperity

ARTICLE TEXT:
A decade-old liquor and tobacco shop near where I stay in Beijing shut down last year for unknown reasons. When it reopened recently, the glowing neon signboard outside announced that it was now a "heartwarming station" for people who work in the open air, such as street cleaners, delivery riders and taxi drivers.
Inside the 150-square-meter station there are some sofa sets, five or six tables with chairs, a water heater, a microwave oven, mobile phone chargers and a first-aid kit.
Outside, there is a toilet and several charging piles for e-bikes.
Such stations, as I learned from a volunteer, are designed to provide free service to street cleaners, delivery riders, taxi drivers and whoever needs help.
Since they are air-conditioned, those working long hours in the open can relax on the sofas, have a cup of tea, enjoy some snacks and have their phones and bikes recharged before returning to work.
According to a recent report, Beijing now has 1,364 such stations operated by subdistricts, neighborhood committees and delivery platforms whose employees benefit from such units. Many other cities in China also have similar government-supported projects.
Before these stations came up, some banks in Beijing were letting workers who slog it out in the open come in to escape the cold, heat, wind or rain.
The establishment of such heartwarming stations is only one of many measures the central authorities and local governments have taken to help disadvantaged groups.
Most cities in China offer free bus rides to senior citizens in their territory. That is why, on boarding a bus in China, one sees that most passengers are senior citizens.
While many retired people are joining square dancing or chorus singing activities, some others are engaging themselves in city tours by taking advantage of the free bus ride policies.
The fact that Beijing alone has 1,252 bus routes covering a total length of 28,928 kilometers should keep senior citizens busy for a couple of years.
Senior citizens also enjoy free entry or discounted tickets to most of the museums, parks, scenic spots and tourist attractions.
While others have to register beforehand and pay to get into popular tourist attractions, senior citizens mostly have to just show their ID card to get in without any appointment. At many places, senior citizens are also entitled to free annual physical checkups and vaccines.
When I entered a community clinic in Sanya, Hainan province, last year looking for a massage, the doctor told me they don't provide such services but could arrange for someone to give me a massage on the condition that I agree to a free physical checkup at the clinic.
It sounded odd to me, but I guess the local medical authorities must have set a goal for each community clinic to provide free medical checkups to a certain number of senior citizens.
In many cities, local governments are offering people above a certain age (80 in Beijing) subsidized renovation of their washrooms to make the facilities senior citizen-friendly. Senior citizens living alone at home are visited several times a day by volunteers and neighborhood committee members to make sure they are fine.
In rural areas, most farmers, unlike their urban counterparts, did not contribute to any social insurance during their working ages, but those aged above 60 are now entitled to a retirement pension ranging from the national minimum of 163 yuan ($24.46) to as high as 1,555 yuan ($233.3) in Shanghai, depending on financial conditions in different localities.
The rural pensions largely come from government subsidies.
Humble as the sum is at the moment, millions of elderly farmers have welcomed it as a good and right measure, as otherwise they would have had to be financially dependent on their children in their old age.
Building sidewalks for those who are visually impaired is becoming a normal practice in city planning and renovation while more and more buses are now equipped with wheelchair lifts to make movement more convenient for people who use wheelchairs.
Numerous employment fairs are organized each year by local governments to help the country's more than 10 million university graduates get jobs.
Free vocational training is provided to teach new skills to those who are unemployed, thus increasing their employability.
With 2026 coming to an end, the central authorities are holding meetings to bring in greater measures to help disadvantaged groups and to make sure that no one is left behind in the country's pursuit of common prosperity. I pray it is a success.
