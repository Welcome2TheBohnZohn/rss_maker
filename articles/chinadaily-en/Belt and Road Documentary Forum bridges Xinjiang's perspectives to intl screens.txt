Belt and Road Documentary Forum bridges Xinjiang's perspectives to intl screens

ARTICLE TEXT:
A compelling story, interesting characters, and international coproductions that share perspectives, talent, costs and risks offer promising opportunities for documentary filmmaking, said Paul Lewis, special board adviser and international documentary consultant at the World Congress of Science and Factual Producers.
Speaking at the inaugural Belt and Road Documentary Forum, held from Nov 11 to 13 in Urumqi, capital of the Xinjiang Uygur autonomous region, Lewis drew on his years of industry experience to encourage more productions showcasing the region and its people, technologies and green initiatives to global audiences.
Hosted by Xinjiang Broadcasting and Television Station and the Belt and Road Initiative Documentary Consortium (BriDoc) initiated by the Communication University of China, the forum brought together more than 200 documentary producers and directors, decision-makers from broadcasting and streaming platforms, industry representatives and scholars from 18 countries and regions. It aimed to promote international coproduction, copyright trading and global distribution, while exploring market trends and fostering industry connections.
Lewis, from Canada, said documentary filmmaking is expensive, labor-intensive and time-consuming, making it difficult for individual countries or organizations to shoulder the costs alone. "For many years, documentary makers have been going out and finding international partners. Now is the time for Xinjiang to become part of that process."
He said Xinjiang's extraordinary landscapes, challenges and human stories offered great content for documentary storytelling.
"It's a place where some of the most important questions about our relationship with nature are being confronted and answered every day. And the answers to these questions have the potential to inspire audiences far beyond Xinjiang and even far beyond China," he said, adding that the region's innovative solutions could offer lessons to other places.
Lewis also stressed the importance of telling intimate personal stories with universal resonance and historical journeys that remain relevant today.
