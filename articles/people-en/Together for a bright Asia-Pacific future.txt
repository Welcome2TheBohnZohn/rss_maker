Together for a bright Asia-Pacific future
The 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders' Meeting will be held in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong province, on Nov. 18 and 19.
As the host economy making its third appearance at the table, China will work with other member economies to chart a blueprint for Asia-Pacific cooperation, focusing on three priorities: openness, innovation, and cooperation.
Together, they will advance the building of an open, dynamic, resilient, and peaceful Asia-Pacific community, injecting fresh impetus into peace and development in the region and beyond.
At the first APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting in 1993, a landmark decision was made to move beyond the outdated mentality of bloc confrontation and zero-sum games, deepen economic cooperation and integration in the region, and build a dynamic, harmonious, and prosperous Asia-Pacific community.
Established amid the wave of economic globalization, APEC aims to promote trade and investment liberalization and facilitation, as well as support economic growth and prosperity. It has helped keep the Asia-Pacific at the forefront of global openness and development.
Home to about 40 percent of the world's population, the Asia-Pacific region accounts for more than 60 percent of global GDP and nearly half of world trade, making it a major engine of global economic growth, an anchor of stability for global development, and a hub of cooperation.
In 2020, APEC adopted the Putrajaya Vision 2040, reaffirming its founding mission to improve living standards across the region. The roadmap sets a clear target: to realize an open, dynamic, resilient, and peaceful Asia-Pacific community by 2040.
2026 marks another APEC "China Year." Under the theme "Building an Asia-Pacific Community to Prosper Together," member economies are advancing broader consensus on the community's institutional architecture and laying the groundwork for its long-term operational framework.
Sustainable regional progress hinges on enhanced connectivity. Greater openness and seamless cross-border linkages enable Asia-Pacific economies to overcome market fragmentation, deepen cooperative ties, and unlock new avenues for growth.
The APEC Connectivity Blueprint for 2015-2025, adopted in Beijing in 2014, mapped out a path toward a comprehensive, multi-tiered, and multidimensional Asia-Pacific connectivity network.
This year, drawing on the final review of the 10-year blueprint, member economies are developing an upgraded version.
Shenzhen, the host city of this year's APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting, is a vibrant city known for its openness and innovation. Where does the vitality of the Asia-Pacific come from? China's answer is innovation.
The Asia-Pacific boasts unique intellectual resources and a well-established tradition of innovation. It has given rise to numerous new technologies, industries, and mechanisms, and has long been at the global forefront of innovation.
According to the 2026 rankings released by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO), the Shenzhen-Hong Kong-Guangzhou cluster ranked first among the world's innovation clusters for the second consecutive year. It was followed by the Tokyo-Yokohama cluster, the San Jose-San Francisco cluster, Seoul, Beijing, and the Shanghai-Suzhou cluster. The rankings underscore the Asia-Pacific's weight in the global innovation landscape.
In 2014, APEC economic leaders endorsed the APEC Accord on Innovative Development, Economic Reform and Growth, charting a course for innovative development in the region.
In 2026, member economies are deepening practical cooperation in digital transformation and artificial intelligence, generating fresh momentum for a dynamic Asia-Pacific community.
Can resilience be maintained amid growing uncertainty? In October, the International Monetary Fund (IMF), in its Regional Economic Outlook: Asia and Pacific, highlighted three major factors shaping the region's economic outlook: the energy shock arising from conflicts in the Middle East, the AI-driven technology boom, and continued trade reconfiguration.
True resilience extends beyond shock absorption and short-term stability; it requires the capacity to foster long-term, sustainable development.
China advocates for universally beneficial, inclusive, green, and sustainable development. This approach, which strengthens the foundations for growth and is future-oriented, reveals what fuels development resilience and how to sustain it.
The Asia-Pacific has suffered from conflicts and wars in the past. It was only after emerging from the shadow of the Cold War that the region, particularly its small- and medium-sized economies, embarked on a fast track toward modernization and created "the Asia-Pacific miracle."
Building a peaceful Asia-Pacific community requires drawing wisdom from history, staying on course. Peace and development reinforce each other and must be advanced together. Peace does not come automatically; it must be actively built. Peace cannot be secured once and for all; it must be carefully safeguarded.
Asia-Pacific economies should jointly uphold peaceful development and win-win cooperation, and work together to implement the four major global initiatives. Only by doing so can they continue to build up collective strength to safeguard peace and stability in the region.
The official emblem for APEC China 2026 features 21 interlaced feathers arranged to form the silhouette of the Peng, a mythical creature from Chinese legend that transforms from a giant fish into a soaring bird. The design symbolizes the 21 APEC member economies harnessing the momentum of regional collaboration to rise together.
Setting fresh sail from Shenzhen, the vessel of Asia-Pacific cooperation will surge forward, charting a course toward the realization of an open, dynamic, resilient, and peaceful Asia-Pacific community.
