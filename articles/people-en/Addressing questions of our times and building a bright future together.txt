ARTICLE TEXT:
Addressing questions of our times and building a bright future together
On Nov. 18, Chinese President Xi Jinping delivered a keynote speech at the opening ceremony of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) CEO Summit 2026 in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong province.
In his speech, Xi focused on the questions of our times: What is going on with the world? What should we do in the Asia-Pacific? To open doors or close doors, work together or against each other, adapt to change or stick to old ways? He offered a keen assessment of regional development trends and the progress of the times.
He put forward China's proposals for building an Asia-Pacific community and bringing shared prosperity for all in the region, and reaffirmed China's commitment to sharing opportunities and pursuing common development with the Asia-Pacific and the wider world.
His speech charted the course for Asia-Pacific cooperation under the new circumstances.
The international landscape is marked by intertwined challenges and uncertainties. Global economic growth lacks momentum, while unilateralism and protectionism are rampant. At the same time, global trade continues to grow against headwinds, and breakthroughs are being made in emerging technologies at a faster pace, exerting a profound impact on global development.
History has shown that openness brings progress, while isolation leads to backwardness. Born amid the waves of economic globalization, APEC has enabled the Asia-Pacific to steer the course of our times toward greater openness and connectivity.
Drawing on this experience, Asia-Pacific economies should remain committed to openness and make fresh contributions to the world through more dynamic and resilient regional cooperation.
In his speech, Xi put forward four proposals: upholding peace and stability, building bridges of opening up and cooperation, boosting innovation to drive growth, and expanding ways for mutual benefits and win-win outcomes.
Together, they provide a clear action plan for building an Asia-Pacific community and promoting shared prosperity.
There can be no prosperity without security, and no security without stability. All parties should resolve differences through dialogue and consultation, and overcome challenges through solidarity and cooperation. This is the way to cement the foundation for sustaining peace and stability in the Asia-Pacific.
They should work steadily toward a Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific (FTAAP) and build a multidimensional connectivity network to pave the way for greater economic integration in the Asia-Pacific.
They should also forge science, technology, and innovation partnerships to turn strengths in innovation into drivers of growth, and provide strong impetus for economic growth in the Asia-Pacific.
In addition, they should follow a people-centered development philosophy and strengthen economic and technical cooperation geared for developing economies, so that development fruits are shared by all economies and all groups and communities.
Amid today's complex and volatile international environment, these four proposals provide the Asia-Pacific region with confidence and hope to stay on course and build consensus for cooperation.
Bay area economies represent a promising new frontier for development. With their openness, connectivity and positive spillover effects, they serve as important vehicles for dynamic Asia-Pacific cooperation.
The Asia-Pacific region is home to some of the world's most important bay areas. The Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area is one of China's most open and economically vibrant regions. The Shenzhen-Hong Kong-Guangzhou cluster, which has ranked first among the world's top 100 innovation clusters for two consecutive years, is a vivid example of how innovation and cooperation can drive regional development.
China supports the establishment of a world association for bay areas economic cooperation and stands ready to deepen exchanges and interactions among bay areas with all parties and explore new ways for stronger economic cooperation in the Asia-Pacific, thus injecting fresh vitality into regional cooperation.
This year marks the start of China's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030) for economic and social development. The plan not only serves as a blueprint for China's own development, but also as a source of opportunities for economic growth in the Asia-Pacific and the wider world. It represents an invitation to cooperation that China has extended to business communities around the world.
Drawing on the advantages of its super-sized open market and innovation testing ground, China will continue to increase imports of quality products and services and deepen integration between technological and industrial innovation, providing global business communities with a promising land for development.
This year also marks the 25th anniversary of China's accession to the World Trade Organization (WTO). Over the past 25 years, China has remained committed to true multilateralism, fully honored its WTO accession commitments and steadily brought stability and predictability to world economic growth and international trade and economic cooperation.
No matter how the international landscape may evolve, China will continue to expand high-standard opening up and stay firmly committed to high-quality Belt and Road cooperation, providing global business communities with an environment conducive to mutually beneficial cooperation.
A China committed to openness and innovation will continue to work with all parties to write a new chapter of shared prosperity for the Asia-Pacific and the world.
The Asia-Pacific is the common home of APEC economies, where all parties work together to pursue development and cooperation.
China stands ready to join hands with its Asia-Pacific partners to address challenges facing development through opening up, generate momentum for growth through innovation, and pursue prosperity for all through cooperation. Together, they can build an Asia-Pacific community and create a brighter future for the region and the world.
