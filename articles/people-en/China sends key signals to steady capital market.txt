China sends key signals to steady capital market
BEIJING, Nov. 27 (Xinhua) -- A State Council executive meeting on Friday sent key signals from Chinese policymakers on steadying the capital market, with major stock indices surging after the meeting.
The meeting, which studied work on further deepening comprehensive investment and financing reforms in the capital market, stressed that the capital market is vital to overall economic development. It called for fully leveraging the existing policies and underscored the need to mull and roll out new measures to promote the steady and sound development of the capital market.
More than 5,000 stocks on China's A-share market traded higher on Friday, with a marked increase in the trading volume. The benchmark Shanghai Composite Index closed 3.73 percent higher and the Shenzhen Component Index jumped 7.24 percent.
Analysts took note of the meeting's clear positioning of the capital market within the overall framework of economic development, pointing out that the market concerns corporate financing and industrial upgrading as well as household assets and property income.
"Stabilizing the stock market means stabilizing expectations, which is of great significance for consolidating the momentum of economic recovery and improvement," said Xie Yaxuan, head of the research and development center at China Merchants Securities.
Official data showed that since early October, the amount of share repurchases and shareholding increases implemented by listed companies has reached nearly 60 billion yuan (about 8.9 billion U.S. dollars), far exceeding previous levels.
Looking forward, there are greater market expectations for policies to support the capital market and the macroeconomy, analysts said.
Friday's meeting emphasized that it is necessary to enhance the role of state-backed funds in stabilizing the market, and optimize the quasi-"stabilization fund" function of the state-owned Central Huijin Investment Ltd. to provide sufficient liquidity.
Since early October, net subscriptions of equity exchange-traded funds have reached nearly 800 billion yuan, helping support major stock indices amid market fluctuations. Market observers predicted that the "national team" of state-backed funds will play a more prominent role in the market-stabilization mechanism in the future.
The meeting also called for improving policies to support and guide medium- and long-term funds, including insurance funds, enterprise annuity funds and social security funds, to invest in the capital market.
Institutional investors have been stepping up countercyclical investment. Publicly offered funds, securities firms' proprietary trading desks, private securities investment funds, social security funds and insurance institutions have all made net purchases of A-shares since early October.
The meeting demanded strengthening the capacity to guard against and respond to external risks. This requires taking a coordinated approach to addressing domestic and external risks, said Liu Yu, chief economist at Industrial Securities.
Since early October, global technology stocks have experienced sharp fluctuations at elevated levels, with volatility in overseas tech stocks causing a sustained impact on Chinese stocks. China's ChiNext Index, tracking the country's Nasdaq-style board of growth enterprises, has seen its trailing price-to-earnings ratio fall to a historic low.
On Friday, the ChiNext Index surged 11.61 percent, while the STAR Composite Index, which reflects the performance of stocks on China's sci-tech innovation board, jumped 14.21 percent.
Efforts are expected to strengthen cross-border risk monitoring and early-warning mechanisms as well as policy responses, while drawing on market-stabilization funds and medium- and long-term capital to bolster market resilience and prevent short-term external shocks from triggering sharp market swings, Liu said.
