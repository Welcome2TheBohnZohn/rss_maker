China firmly opposes U.S. additional tariffs on China, other economies: commerce ministry
BEIJING, Nov. 26 (Xinhua) -- China firmly opposes the imposition of additional Section 301 tariffs by the United States on China and other economies, a Ministry of Commerce spokesperson said on Thursday.
The Office of the United States Trade Representative recently announced the results of its Section 301 investigation into alleged "overcapacity," proposing to impose additional Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies, including China.
Spokesperson He Yadong said at a regular press briefing that the U.S. Section 301 investigation is a typical act of unilateralism that seriously disrupts the international economic order and violates World Trade Organization rules.
He noted that a certain country is using the pretext of "overcapacity" to pursue protectionism, politicizing normal market competition and turning industrial development issues into political tools.
Such practices do little to address its own structural imbalances, but will instead disrupt the stability and security of global industrial and supply chains, undermine industrial cooperation, push up global inflation, harm the interests of businesses and consumers, and pose long-term systemic risks to global economic growth, he added.
The spokesperson urged the United States to correct its erroneous practices and return to the right track of resolving differences through dialogue and consultation.
China will closely monitor the developments and reserves the right to take necessary measures to firmly safeguard its legitimate rights and interests, he added.
Responding to another media query on the arrangement for a 30-billion-U.S. dollar reciprocal tariff reduction reached by China and the United States in September, He noted that China believes its early implementation would help expand bilateral trade.
"We noticed that the United States had exempted products covered by the arrangement from the scope of its proposed Section 301 measures targeting alleged overcapacity," the spokesperson said.
"We hope the United States will expedite its internal procedures and work with China to bring the 30-billion-U.S. dollar reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement into effect at an early date," he added.
