Xi Story: Vision over a century old meets a changing China
BEIJING, Nov. 24 (Xinhua) -- Dr. Sun Yat-sen once envisioned an ambitious blueprint for China's modernization in a landmark work compiled from his writings finished between 1917 and 1919, featuring plans for an extensive railway and road network as well as world-class ports.
"Many of the goals Dr. Sun Yat-sen set forth have been realized, and what we have accomplished has gone far beyond anything he could have imagined," President Xi Jinping said last week at a museum in honor of Sun, one of the nation's revered revolutionary forefathers, in south China's Guangdong Province.
This year marks the 160th anniversary of Sun's birth. Upon arrival at the museum located in the city of Zhongshan, Sun's hometown, Xi, also general secretary of the Communist Party of China Central Committee and chairman of the Central Military Commission, laid a floral basket before the pioneer's bronze statue and bowed in tribute.
Born in 1866, Sun led the Revolution of 1911 more than a century ago to abolish the absolute monarchy that had ruled China for thousands of years.
Earlier in November, Xi addressed a gathering held in the Great Hall of the People in Beijing to mark Sun's 160th birth anniversary, hailing Sun as "a great national hero, a great patriot, and a great pioneer of China's democratic revolution."
At the gathering, Xi highlighted Sun's lifelong devotion to the country, noting that Sun's call for "revitalizing China" has inspired generation after generation of Chinese, taking on renewed significance with each passing era.
The president called for carrying forward Sun's legacy, working together to advance national rejuvenation, and turning into reality the great dream of all Chinese people since the advent of modern times.
Xi's visit to the museum, part of an inspection tour in Zhongshan on Nov. 16, offered a glimpse of how Sun's vision of a strong and prosperous China has taken shape today.
Sun once envisioned developing the country around three great ports -- in the north, the east and the south. The three powerhouses of China's economy today, the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, the Yangtze River Delta and the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area, echo Sun's original design, Xi noted at the museum.
"Dr. Sun's national railway map envisaged 160,000 kilometers," Xi said, noting that China's railway network has since surpassed that figure.
By the end of 2025, the country's railway network had spanned 165,000 kilometers in total, including 50,000 kilometers of operational high-speed railway, which accounted for 70 percent of the world's total.
"Today, the Chinese economy ranks second in the world. Chinese people have achieved moderate prosperity in all respects, and the Chinese nation has stood up, grown rich and become strong," Xi said.
China is also continuing to make new contributions to the development and progress of human society, he added.
This transformation Sun once dreamed of is also evident in his hometown and the surrounding area. Before visiting the museum, Xi inspected the Shenzhen-Zhongshan Link, a mega cross-sea passage connecting cities on opposite sides of the Pearl River Estuary.
The link cuts travel time between the technology hub of Shenzhen and the manufacturing city of Zhongshan from about two hours to roughly 30 minutes, facilitating integration across the Greater Bay Area.
Over the years, Xi has made several inspection tours of the Greater Bay Area.
Sun's former residence and the museum exhibition hall in Zhongshan are just a short distance apart. "I have been here before, but this visit has given me new impressions," Xi said.
"The great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation has entered an irreversible historical process," Xi said, calling for sustained efforts to advance the great cause.
