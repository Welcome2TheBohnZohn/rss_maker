Commentary: Resilient Chinese economy brings much-needed certainty to the world
A drone photo shows new energy vehicles to be exported at Yantai Port in east China's Shandong Province, Sept. 15, 2026. (Photo by Tang Ke/Xinhua)
BEIJING, Oct. 19 (Xinhua) -- Viewed as an ocean rather than a pond, China's economy may not be immune to headwinds, but it stands firm enough to absorb powerful waves and weather turbulent times. In a world starved of certainty, it remains a stabilizing force in the global economy.
Facts on the ground speak for themselves. China's economy expanded by 4.6 percent in the first three quarters of 2026, landing squarely within the full-year target range and remaining among the fastest of major economies.
Yet to read the economy solely through the lens of headline speed is to miss the more important story. China is in a critical period of accelerating the transition from old growth drivers to new ones. The quality of growth and the direction of structural change matter more.
New quality productive forces are gathering momentum, with new growth drivers including high-end manufacturing, the digital and intelligent economy and modern services contributing more than 40 percent of expansion. In particular, industrial robots, 3D-printing equipment and lithium-ion batteries all registered strong double-digit growth, helping steer the economy toward more innovation-led, quality-driven development.
The foundation of China's economic resilience also lies in consumption, which accounted for nearly half of the GDP growth in the first three quarters. Services retail sales, upgraded goods consumption, new formats, as well as lower-tier markets and inbound tourism, are all picking up steam. With policymakers leaning harder into consumption support, household spending appetite is showing steady signs of recovery, setting the stage to unlock the vast potential of the country's super-large market.
Foreign trade remains one of the brightest spots, with growth reaching its highest level for the same period in five years. From new energy vehicles to photovoltaic products, robotics and innovative pharmaceuticals, Chinese exports are increasingly defined by frontier technology, original innovation capability and complete industrial and supply chains.
Furthermore, China has ample and effective policy tools to shape its economic growth trajectory. Five-year plans, deepened reforms and wider opening up, along with targeted fiscal and monetary measures and considerable room for counter-cyclical adjustment, all provide predictability and stability for the market.
A clear-eyed understanding of the Chinese economy also requires acknowledging the real headwinds: a complex and volatile external environment, insufficient domestic demand, and a structural transition that isn't finished.
As the latest figures have demonstrated, the Chinese economy is resilient. This is contrary to the narratives hyped up by certain pessimists and doomsters in the West. Backed by a domestic market of 1.4 billion people, a solid manufacturing base, well-developed infrastructure, a strong talent pool, and complete industrial and supply chains, the underlying fundamentals and long-term positive trend remain unchanged.
Amid the sluggish global recovery and rising geopolitical risks, China's development continues to offer the world growth momentum and market opportunities. It is both a sustained driver of inclusive and shared growth and a reliable buffer against global economic volatility stemming from factors such as regional conflicts and the rise of unilateralism and protectionism. For many years, China has contributed around 30 percent of global economic growth, remaining a major engine of the world economy.
As the world's largest manufacturer, China continues to provide high-quality, affordable, and reliable product supply globally. No longer just the world's factory, China is becoming a market and a mega-buyer for the world as imports have surged over 20 percent, reflecting a more balanced and open trade structure.
China's commitment to opening up remains unwavering despite rising protectionism globally. This is evidenced by the establishment of more new foreign-invested enterprises, and also highlighted by Apple's executive chairman Tim Cook, who recently noted that the company remains optimistic about China's development prospects and will further expand its business in the country.
China's edge may lie less in its speed, but more in the quality and sustainability of its advance, and in its capacity to hit its full-year targets. "China Opportunity 2.0," boosted by the dividends of innovation and high-standard opening up, will surely create more win-win cooperation outcomes for the world.
