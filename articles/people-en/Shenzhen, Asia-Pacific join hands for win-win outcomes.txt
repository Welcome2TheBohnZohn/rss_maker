Shenzhen, Asia-Pacific join hands for win-win outcomes
Shenzhen, a coastal city in south China's Guangdong Province, is buzzing as it prepares to host the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting on Nov. 18-19, welcoming guests from across the Asia-Pacific.
An APEC-themed banner hangs at a metro station in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province, Oct. 14. (People's Daily Online/Wang Xing)
Foreign tourists wait to collect their luggage at Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province, Oct. 19. (People's Daily Online/Ha Jung-mi)
"We should stay true to the vision of harmony without uniformity, fully leverage the diversity of Asia Pacific economies, draw on each other's strengths, and jointly make the pie of Asia Pacific cooperation bigger. We should advance both our respective interests and those of others, and foster an environment of cooperation to achieve success and development for all economies," Chinese President Xi Jinping said in a written address at the APEC CEO Summit in Gyeongju, South Korea, in October 2025.
China has identified openness, innovation and cooperation as the three priority areas for the 2026 APEC "China Year." Cooperation means drawing on each member's strengths, aligning policies and sharing experience.
Shenzhen's comparative advantages lie in its comprehensive electronics industry chain and vibrant innovation ecosystem. The robot block in Bantian, Longgang district, offers a case in point. The world's first robot 6S store and AI 6S store stand side by side there. Overseas makers can turn ideas into products at the block, with services covering design, prototyping, customization and incubation.
Humanoid robots perform outside the world's first robot 6S store in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province, during an activity for the 2026 APEC "China Year," drawing Chinese and foreign tourists, Oct. 21, 2026. (People's Daily Online/Andrew Smith)
"We hope to share Shenzhen's complete industrial support system with APEC partners," said Zhou Fangzheng, executive store manager of the robot 6S store. More than 300 companies working in humanoid and specialized robotics have gathered at the store.
Since its opening, the store has received visits from executives from LG Group and Procter & Gamble, and several organizations from Malaysia and Singapore have expressed interest in cooperation.
The AI 6S store has attracted more than 350 partners, showcased over 600 AI products and received 300,000 visits, over 30 percent from overseas, since opening in September last year, said deputy manager Yu Fang. A popular robot duck, Microduck, developed by a team from the world's largest AI open source community, was brought to market with the help of Shenzhen's supply chain.
Longgang is home to more than 800 companies in the AI industry chain, 12,700 robot-related companies and more than 2,500 industrial enterprises above designated size, providing a strong foundation for supply chain collaboration across the Asia-Pacific.
Shenzhen's ties with the Asia-Pacific go beyond industrial collaboration and extend to deeper institutional cooperation. On July 26, 2026, the International Mangrove Center, the world's first intergovernmental organization exclusively dedicated to mangrove protection, held its first council meeting in Shenzhen and began full operations. It has 21 signatory countries, including nine members. The center has contributed to global ecological governance, holding six international workshops attended by 137 representatives from 30 countries.
At the APEC Ministerial Meeting on Forestry on July 27 and 28, heads of delegations from 21 APEC economies planted mangrove seedlings at Shenzhen Museum of Contemporary Art and Urban Planning. The seedlings will later be transplanted to the "APEC Forestry Ministers' Forest" at the Futian Mangrove National Nature Reserve.
Seedlings for the "APEC Forestry Ministers' Forest" at the Futian Mangrove National Nature Reserve in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province, Oct. 19. (People's Daily Online/Zhou Jielu)
Art has become a bridge for people-to-people exchanges in the Asia-Pacific. At the Guanlan Original Printmaking Base, artists from around the world create side by side. German artist Ute Wöllmann painted two lines from a poem by ancient Chinese poet Li Bai alongside two lines by a German poet. The base has welcomed more than 2,000 overseas artists since it opened.
German artist Ute Wöllmann introduces her works created at the Guanlan Original Printmaking Base in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province, Oct. 20. (People's Daily Online/Shen Yuhuan)
The Shenzhen SME Going Global Service Reception Hall at the Longgang Enterprise Internationalization Service Base in Longgang district has created a platform to connect supply and demand among small and medium-sized enterprises across the Asia-Pacific. The base has served 9,494 companies seeking to expand overseas and released orders worth more than 13.5 billion yuan (about $2.02 billion).
Photo taken on Oct. 21 shows a digital screen at the Shenzhen SME Going Global Service Reception Hall in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province. (People's Daily Online/Shen Yuhuan)
"The APEC meeting provides a valuable channel of communication between Shenzhen and Asia-Pacific economies. We look forward to more practical cooperation dividends from the meeting," said Li Qun, deputy general manager of Shenzhen Longgang Industrial Investment Service Group Co., Ltd.
In the first three quarters of this year, Shenzhen's imports and exports with other APEC economies reached 3.23 trillion yuan, accounting for nearly 70 percent of the city's total foreign trade.
