ARTICLE TEXT:
China's sector-specific plans map out path to high-quality development
BEIJING, Nov. 23 (Xinhua) -- China has rolled out a series of sector-specific plans to translate its broader development vision into concrete action during the 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026-2030), providing detailed roadmaps for key areas of economic and social development.
Covering fields ranging from manufacturing and energy to employment, education and health care, the plans set out specific targets, priority tasks and policy measures.
Together, they offer a more detailed look at how the world's second-largest economy seeks to cultivate new growth drivers, address emerging development challenges and improve people's livelihoods as it pursues high-quality development amid global uncertainties.
FROM BLUEPRINT TO ACTION
Five-year plans have long played an important role in China's governance, providing strategic direction and policy continuity for the country's development.
The overarching outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan, approved in March this year, sets out the country's overall development objectives through 2030.
Sector-specific plans complement this general framework by breaking down national priorities into detailed targets, major projects and implementation arrangements for individual fields.
Zhang Linshan, a researcher at the Academy of Macroeconomic Research under the National Development and Reform Commission, said sector-specific plans provide an important basis for guiding development in particular fields, allocating public resources, directing private investment and formulating relevant policies.
Several of the recently released plans have established measurable targets for 2030. China's dedicated consumption plan aims to raise total retail sales of consumer goods to around 60 trillion yuan (8.94 trillion U.S. dollars), while the plan for building a new-type power system targets a 50-percent share for non-fossil energy in electricity generation.
Other targets include raising average life expectancy to 80 years and reducing carbon dioxide emissions per unit of GDP by 17 percent from the 2025 level, reflecting a focus on both economic development and improvements in people's well-being.
BREAKING NEW GROUND
A notable feature of the latest round of sector-specific plans is that they cover certain fields for the first time, reflecting China's efforts to address emerging development challenges and respond to changing economic and social needs.
Among them is the country's first national-level urban renewal plan, released in May, which calls for the construction and renovation of approximately 770,000 km of urban underground pipelines.
The plan also calls for better use of existing land and stronger building safety management, reflecting a shift toward upgrading existing urban areas and improving the quality of urban life.
Consumption expansion, disease prevention and control, and cultural heritage protection are among other fields covered by national-level sector-specific plans for the first time.
The latest round also includes the first national-level plans dedicated to building China's strengths in finance, sports and tourism.
Tian Xuan, dean of Peking University's Guanghua School of Management, said the new plans cover areas crucial to China's economic and social development over the next five years.
Their introduction would further improve the country's planning system and help ensure the effective implementation of national development priorities, Tian said.
HIGH-QUALITY DEVELOPMENT
Beyond their expanding scope, the sector-specific plans share a central objective: advancing high-quality development by strengthening the real economy, fostering innovation and improving people's well-being.
A plan to improve industrial foundational capabilities calls for stronger innovation platforms and measures to address weaknesses in key technologies.
Another aims to consolidate China's competitive advantages in intelligent connected new energy vehicles and help the country join the ranks of global automotive powerhouses by 2030.
Technological innovation is a recurring priority across several sector-specific plans, with measures to promote artificial intelligence applications, breakthroughs in all-solid-state battery technologies and research into frontier technologies.
Green transition also features prominently, with plans calling for greener industrial development and closer coordination between electricity supply and computing power.
Xin Yongfei, an expert at the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology, said the sector-specific plans coordinate efforts in basic research, technological breakthroughs, commercialization and talent development to build momentum for high-quality development.
The pursuit of high-quality development extends beyond industrial upgrading to improving people's livelihoods. The plan for implementing the employment-first strategy calls for promoting employment and entrepreneurship in response to advances in artificial intelligence and expanding job opportunities in services and emerging industries.
Other plans seek to improve public services, including expanding enrollment capacity at general senior high schools, developing elderly-friendly communities and expanding access to affordable elderly care services.
The plans are also meant to deliver benefits well beyond China's borders, with measures to expand imports of quality foreign products and deepen international cooperation.
Koh King Kee, president of the Center for New Inclusive Asia, a Malaysian think tank, said China's sector-specific plans emphasize the development of new quality productive forces and signal a commitment to high-standard opening up and mutually beneficial cooperation.
By turning more Chinese demand into development opportunities, the plans will continue to inject much-needed certainty into the global economy, Koh said.
