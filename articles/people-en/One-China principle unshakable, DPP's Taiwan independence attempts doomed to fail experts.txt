Roundup: One-China principle unshakable, DPP's "Taiwan independence" attempts doomed to fail: experts
BEIJING, Nov. 21 (Xinhua) -- The recent meeting between the heads of state of China and the United States and the China-Russia-U.S. summit have sent a clear message: the Taiwan question is at the heart of China's core interests and the international community's commitment to the one-China principle is unshakable, experts and scholars across the Taiwan Strait said.
They said the Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) authorities' attempts to seek "Taiwan independence" by relying on external forces and military buildup are doomed to fail.
On Nov. 19, Chinese President Xi Jinping met with U.S. President Donald Trump, who was in the southern Chinese metropolis of Shenzhen to attend the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders' Meeting. Xi also hosted a historic summit with Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin on the same day.
During his meeting with Trump, Xi said that on the Taiwan question, the U.S. side should keep doing the right thing and avoid major setbacks in China-U.S. relations. Trump, for his part, said the United States does not support "Taiwan independence."
Chen Guiqing, a research fellow at the Beijing-based Institute of Taiwan Studies, Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, said Xi had once again made China's firm position on the Taiwan question clear to the United States while also setting out China's expectations.
The Taiwan question concerns not only the overall stability of China-U.S. relations but also global stability, Chen said. Doing the right thing means, among other things, that the United States should stop playing the "Taiwan card" and refrain from sending wrong signals to secessionist forces seeking "Taiwan independence," he said. Otherwise, it will affect efforts by the two countries to build a constructive relationship of strategic stability, Chen added.
Ni Yongjie, director of the Shanghai Institute of Taiwan Studies, noted that this was the first time during Trump's presidency that he had publicly stated at a meeting between the two heads of state that the United States does not support "Taiwan independence."
The statement represented a response by the U.S. side to China's major concerns over the Taiwan question, Ni said.
Experts said the Taiwan-related positions articulated by the two leaders dealt another heavy blow to secessionist forces seeking "Taiwan independence."
Chang Chun-kai, a research fellow at the Center for Cross-Strait Political and Economic Studies, Chengchi University, said the one-China principle constitutes the political foundation of China-U.S. relations and is indispensable to ensuring peace and stability.
Peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait depend on the one-China principle and the 1992 Consensus, Chang said, adding that this is also in line with the broader trend toward a stable relationship between China and the United States.
The Taiwan question stems from China's civil war fought between the forces led by the Communist Party of China (CPC) and the Chinese Kuomintang (KMT) about eight decades ago. In 1949, the defeated KMT retreated to Taiwan, and the People's Republic of China was founded under the leadership of the CPC.
The unresolved civil war, combined with foreign interference, has kept the two sides of the Taiwan Strait in a prolonged state of political confrontation. However, the fact that Taiwan is an inalienable part of China's territory has never changed.
At the trilateral meeting with Putin and Trump, Xi stressed that countries should not interfere in one another's domestic affairs or each other's internal affairs. On major sensitive issues concerning one another, it is important to communicate in advance, try to see things from one another's perspective, act with caution, and in particular, not to challenge the red lines of one another, Xi said.
Putin said the three countries should adhere to the principles of sovereign equality and noninterference in internal affairs, and look for ways to resolve concerns in a balanced manner through cooperation.
Li Huan, a research fellow at the China Institutes of Contemporary International Relations, said the summit carries an important message for the Taiwan question: major countries are willing to sit down and communicate directly to manage differences and avoid misjudgment and conflict, which shatters the attempt and illusion of secessionist forces to exploit competition among major countries to pursue "Taiwan independence."
Commenting on Trump's statement and the consensus reached by the three heads of state, Chen said that although secessionist forces on the island are trying to downplay their significance, more and more people in Taiwan will come to see clearly that "Taiwan independence" attempts find no support and will never succeed.
Xi noted at the summit that, as the main victors of World War II, China, Russia and the United States should steadfastly safeguard the postwar international order, uphold the purposes and principles of the UN Charter, and defend the hard-won outcomes of victory in World War II.
Li said Taiwan's restoration to China was one of the outcomes of victory in World War II and an important part of the postwar international order, adding that the historical and legal fact that Taiwan is an inalienable part of China is clear and indisputable.
By recalling the three countries' history of fighting side by side during World War II and reaffirming their commitment to safeguarding the postwar international order and the purposes of the UN Charter, the summit sent a stern warning to forces seeking to challenge that order and undermine the achievements of the war, including secessionist forces seeking "Taiwan independence," she said.
Feng Wang, a Taiwan-based history writer, said the history of World War II and the victory over fascism were shared historical memories of the international community that must neither be forgotten nor distorted.
People in Taiwan should not be misled by political manipulation or erroneous views of history, Wang said, adding that people from all walks of life in Taiwan should take a long-term and broad view, thinking in the direction that benefits Taiwan compatriots, the country and the Chinese nation.
"Secession aimed at 'Taiwan independence' is the root cause of turmoil across the Taiwan Strait. It harms the immediate interests of people on both sides of the Strait and is widely opposed by the international community. It is a dead end," Ni said.
The DPP authorities should abandon their secessionist stance as soon as possible and return to the path of peaceful development of cross-Strait relations, he added.
