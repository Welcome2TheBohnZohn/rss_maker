Shenzhou-24 astronauts, including first foreigner, enter China's Tiangong space station
JIUQUAN, Nov. 25 (Xinhua) -- The Shenzhou-24 crew, comprising Chinese astronauts Tang Hongbo and Wu Yi, and Pakistani astronaut Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, entered China's Tiangong space station on Wednesday.
Serving as a payload specialist, Ali has made history by becoming the first foreigner to board Tiangong.
The astronaut trio took off at 8:18 a.m. (Beijing Time) the same day aboard China's Shenzhou-24 spaceship from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwest China.
The Shenzhou-23 crew in orbit opened the hatch of the space station at 2:35 p.m. and welcomed the new arrivals, according to the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA).
After entering the space station, the Shenzhou-24 crew met with the three Shenzhou-23 astronauts, who have been in orbit for about six months. The two crews then took group pictures of the ninth space get-together in China's aerospace history. They will now start a new in-orbit handover round.
Ali will join in the crew's routine work and focus on joint China-Pakistan space science experiments while in orbit, primarily installing experiment samples and units, and carrying out in-orbit experiments, CMSA spokesperson Zhang Jingbo revealed at a press conference on Tuesday.
After completing a short-term joint flight, Ali will return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft alongside two Shenzhou-23 crew members. The other two Shenzhou-24 astronauts will stay in orbit for roughly six months along with Shenzhou-23 astronaut Zhu Yangzhu, who has already spent six months in orbit and will remain aboard Tiangong to complete a year-long stay experiment.
