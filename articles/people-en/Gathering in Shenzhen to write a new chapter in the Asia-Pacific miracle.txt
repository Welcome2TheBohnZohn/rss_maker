Gathering in Shenzhen to write a new chapter in the "Asia-Pacific miracle"
With momentum running high, the Asia-Pacific region is surging forward. The 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders' Meeting will be held from Nov. 18 to 19 in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong province. Chinese President Xi Jinping will chair the meeting and deliver a speech.
The 2026 APEC CEO Summit is being held in the city from Nov. 17 to 18. President Xi will attend the opening ceremony and deliver a keynote speech.
Leaders or representatives of participating APEC economies will gather in the city to deliberate under the respective themes of the two gatherings: "Building an Asia-Pacific Community to Prosper Together" for the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting, and "To Prosper and Win the Future Together" for the APEC CEO Summit.
Guided by the three priorities of openness, innovation, and cooperation, they will inject fresh impetus into regional economic integration and ensure that the building of the Asia-Pacific community yields deeper and more substantive outcomes.
Arising from the tide of economic globalization, APEC has a history of over 30 years, steering the Asia-Pacific to the forefront of openness and development, and helping forge the region into the world's most dynamic economic hub.
This year marks the 35th anniversary of China's accession to APEC. The upcoming summit will be the third time China has hosted the event, following the 2001 Shanghai summit that gave a fresh start to Asia-Pacific cooperation in the new century, and the 2014 Beijing summit that kicked off the Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific (FTAAP) process and laid out the APEC Connectivity Blueprint. Now, APEC welcomes esteemed guests to Shenzhen.
These milestones underscore China's increasingly important role and profound contributions on the APEC stage, demonstrating its leading role in regional cooperation, commitment to multilateralism, and support for regional economic integration.
Today, the Asia-Pacific continues to elevate its weight in the world economy. Comprising nearly 40 percent of the global population and generating over 60 percent of global GDP, APEC member economies are steadily driving the world's economic center of gravity toward the region.
Amidst a new round of scientific and technological revolution and industrial transformation, APEC member economies have embraced the pulse of the digital age, anchoring the Asia-Pacific as a global hub for innovation-led growth.
"We should stay true to APEC's founding mission to promote economic growth and improve people's lives, and champion open development where everyone shares opportunities and emerges a winner. We should promote a universally beneficial and inclusive economic globalization, and build an Asia-Pacific community." President Xi said when addressing Session I of the 32nd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting. These important remarks charted the course for APEC cooperation under the new circumstances.
Since the APEC "China Year" was launched, China has built multi-level dialogue platforms to turn consensus into pragmatic cooperation.
In May, the APEC Ministers Responsible for Trade Meeting opened in Suzhou, east China's Jiangsu province. The meeting released the Suzhou Statement, sending a strong signal to advance regional economic integration and uphold multilateralism.
In July, the 2026 APEC Digital and AI Ministerial Meeting was held in Chengdu, southwest China's Sichuan province. All parties agreed to jointly promote digital and AI technologies to empower the Asia-Pacific community.
In September, the 16th APEC Energy Ministerial Meeting adopted a joint statement that, for the first time, systematically introduced and defined the concept of an Asia-Pacific energy community.
All parties have expressed high appreciation for China's endeavors in advancing the development of an Asia-Pacific community. The vision of "building an Asia-Pacific community to prosper together" is steadily translating into an expanding array of substantive and tangible cooperation outcomes.
The Asia-Pacific is where China finds its footing and anchors its development and prosperity. China has consistently leveraged its own growth to fuel the region's development, providing fresh momentum for the prosperity of the Asia-Pacific through its ongoing reform and opening-up.
In 2025, China became the largest trading partner for 13 APEC economies. In the first three quarters of this year, China's imports and exports with other APEC economies reached 24.07 trillion yuan (about $3.6 trillion), up 23.9 percent year on year.
Enterprises from China and abroad have eagerly signed up for the APEC CEO Summit 2026, with U.S. companies registering in the highest numbers. This reflects the choice made by the Asia-Pacific business community through concrete action.
China will continue to advance high-standard opening-up to promote deeper reforms and high-quality development. By doing so, it will offer the Asia-Pacific and the wider world broader development opportunities, a better business environment, a wider stage for innovation, and firmer foundations for green growth, injecting greater stability into the world through the certainty of its own development.
All eyes are currently turned toward Shenzhen on the shores of the South China Sea. Participants from across the region eagerly anticipate a milestone gathering that will shape the future of the Asia-Pacific, expecting it to usher in a brilliant new chapter for APEC, steer the giant ship of regional cooperation toward the shared vision of an Asia-Pacific community, and make an even greater contribution to regional and global development.
