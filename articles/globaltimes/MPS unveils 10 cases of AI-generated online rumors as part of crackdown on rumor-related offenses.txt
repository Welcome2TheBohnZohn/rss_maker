TITLE:
MPS unveils 10 cases of AI-generated online rumors as part of crackdown on rumor-related offenses

The cybersecurity bureau of China's Ministry of Public Security (MPS) on Tuesday released 10 typical cases involving the fabrication and spread of AI-generated online rumors in 2026, as part of ongoing efforts to crack down on rumor-related offenses under the "Clean Internet 2026" campaign. 
In one case, an internet user surnamed Ma used AI tools to fabricate a video of the glass-bottom bridge at Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon in Central China's Hunan Province collapsing to attract attention and traffic. Falsely showing the bridge breaking apart and people fleeing in panic, the video carried the caption "There's no need to go out for fun unless you have to. It's safer to stay at home." The video sparked public panic and disrupted public order. Local police have since taken criminal compulsory measures against Ma in accordance with the law. 
In another case, an internet user surnamed Han used AI tools to create and post fake images and videos of himself in a police uniform against a backdrop of police insignia and vehicles to attract attention and traffic. The content misled users, disrupted public order, and had a negative social impact, and local police have since taken criminal compulsory measures against Han in accordance with the law. 
A netizen surnamed Shu used AI tools to generate a fake video of a production line for toxic, artificially produced rice in Central China's Hubei Province to attract attention. The video spread fabricated food-safety rumors, causing public panic and disrupting public order. Local police have imposed administrative penalties on Shu.
Another case involved an internet user surnamed Sun who used AI tools to fabricate false information about a case in which authorities had seized smuggled rare earths worth 4.6 billion yuan ($690 million) in Lianyungang, East China's Jiangsu Province. Local police have imposed administrative penalties on Sun in accordance with the law.
An internet user surnamed Wang used AI to fabricate screenshots of social media posts impersonating an entertainer in an attempt to express personal dissatisfaction. The fake posts, written in the entertainer's voice, expressed discontent and falsely claimed that a performance had been called off. The content had a negative impact and disrupted public order. Local police have imposed administrative penalties on Wang. 
In another case, a netizen surnamed Xing used AI to use a public figure's likeness in livestream sales on an online platform without authorization. The unauthorized use misled internet viewers and caused serious adverse impacts. Local police have imposed administrative penalties on Xing. 
The MPS reminded the public that deliberately fabricating or spreading rumors, or falsely reporting emergencies, epidemics, disasters, or police incidents can result in up to 10 days of detention and a fine of up to 1,000 yuan for minor offenses - or up to  seven years in prison for serious ones.
