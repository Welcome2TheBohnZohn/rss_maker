HKSAR Election Committee polls highlight strengths of new electoral system, high-quality democracy: central authorities

The 2026 Election Committee Subsector Ordinary Elections of Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) was successfully held on November 22, 2026. Photo: Screenshot from news.gov.hk
The 2026 Election Committee Subsector Ordinary Elections in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) were successfully held on Sunday, recording a 98.79 percent turnout, with 5,640 people voting. The election fully upheld the principle of "patriots administering Hong Kong" and highlighted the strengths of the city's new electoral system, marking another important step in advancing democracy with Hong Kong characteristics, a spokesperson for the Liaison Office of Central Government in the HKSAR said on Monday. 
The HKSAR Chief Executive John Lee on Sunday congratulated those elected, describing the poll as an example of healthy competition among patriots. He urged the new members to contribute to the long-term success of the "One Country, Two Systems" principle. 
Liang Haiming, chairman of the China Silk Road iValley Research Institute and dean of the Belt and Road Research Institute at Hainan University, told the Global Times on Monday that the new electoral system has three key strengths: establishing a common political foundation under the principle of "patriots administering Hong Kong" while safeguarding constitutional order; promoting balanced participation by accommodating the interests of different sectors; and enhancing political effectiveness by encouraging candidates to compete on the basis of their policy platforms, capabilities and performance.
According to Liang, the key to developing democracy with Hong Kong characteristics lies in upholding "One Country, Two Systems," taking local circumstances into account, and improving governance effectiveness. The new electoral system aims to ensure the selection of capable leaders, address public concerns and translate institutional strengths into tangible improvements in people's livelihoods and social stability.
The spokesperson of the liaison office said the elections were held at a significant time as HKSAR entered a new stage in implementing "One Country, Two Systems," rolled out its first five-year plan and prepared to mark the 30th anniversary of its return to China. 
The new members, whose term largely coincides with the five-year plan's implementation period, are expected to help the HKSAR government turn its development vision into reality, amid strong public attention and expectations.
According to Liang, the new members can help provide policy continuity and align national development priorities with Hong Kong's strengths in finance, innovation and technology, and shipping, while addressing local needs in housing, employment and elderly care. 
The elections demonstrated the strengths of Hong Kong's new electoral system, including broad representation, political inclusiveness, balanced participation and fair competition. A total of 31 subsectors saw more candidates than available seats, with some recording contests of 25 candidates for 15 seats and 23 for 15, according to the spokesperson.
According to the spokesperson, the diverse backgrounds of the candidates, ranging from young professionals and academics to Olympic medalists, musicians with disabilities and foreign bank executives, demonstrated balanced participation and merit-based selection.
Li Xiaobing, a Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan affairs expert from Nankai University, said the figures demonstrate broader and more extensive democratic participation, with more people willing to engage in the electoral process. 
The members drawn from Hong Kong's political, cultural, business and professional sectors represent key forces in the city's development. As a foundational institution in association with the chief executive election and the Legislative Council's nomination and election processes, the committee's active participation reflects confidence in Hong Kong's political future and development, Li told the Global Times on Monday.
