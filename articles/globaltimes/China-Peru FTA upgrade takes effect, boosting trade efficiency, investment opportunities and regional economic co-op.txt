China-Peru FTA upgrade takes effect, boosting trade efficiency, investment opportunities and regional economic co-op

ARTICLE TEXT:

With the upgraded China-Peru free trade agreement (FTA) taking effect on Sunday, bilateral trade is expected to become more efficient through streamlined customs procedures and improved supply chain connectivity, creating new opportunities for businesses in both countries and giving fresh impetus to China-Latin America economic cooperation.
The upgrade will help Peruvian agricultural products, including blueberries, avocados and grapes, reach Chinese consumers more efficiently, while facilitating Chinese exports of automobiles, machinery and electronics to Peru. Chinese analysts said the agreement could also deepen cooperation in industrial chains, investment and emerging industries.
The protocol improves seven existing chapters, including rules of origin, customs procedures, trade facilitation and investment, while adding five new chapters covering standards and conformity assessment cooperation, competition policy, electronic commerce, global supply chains, and environment and trade, according to China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM).
The ministry said the agreement will create a more favorable business environment, expand trade and investment opportunities and bring more tangible benefits to both countries.
New opportunities
For Peruvian fruit exporters, the upgraded agreement could help address the challenge of getting fresh produce to Chinese buyers with fewer delays and more predictable customs procedures.
Gonzalo Matamala, managing director of Southeast Asia and China at INNOF Ltd, a company engaged in fruit trade between China and Latin America, told the Global Times on Sunday that the upgrade's greatest value lies in improving trade efficiency rather than cutting tariffs, as the original agreement already provided substantial benefits.
"A container of blueberries, grapes or avocados can lose significant commercial value because of a few days of delays," Matamala said, adding that faster customs clearance and more predictable procedures could directly affect exporters' profitability.
According to MOFCOM, the upgraded agreement introduces provisions on electronic networking and detailed arrangements for customs information exchange, cargo clearance, express shipments and perishable goods. It also strengthens transparency and consultation mechanisms concerning sanitary and phytosanitary measures.
The new arrangements could complement the logistics advantages brought by Chancay Port, a landmark Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) cooperation project inaugurated in November 2024.
The direct shipping route between Chancay and Shanghai has cut transportation time from more than a month to around 23 days, while reducing shipping costs by at least 20 percent, according to a Xinhua News Agency report.
In June 2025, Peruvian fruit producer Fruitist shipped its first batch of blueberries for the new season to China through Chancay Port. The shipment arrived in Shanghai about 10 days faster compared to previous transportation arrangements, according to Peru's Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
In the first 11 months of 2025, the value of fruit imports from Peru through Shanghai ports rose 117.4 percent year-on-year to 2.6 billion yuan ($390.78 million), according to Shanghai customs data cited by local media.
Matamala said improved shipping connections and trade procedures could strengthen Peru's competitiveness in China, although the benefits would depend on how effectively customs authorities, shipping companies and exporters implement the new arrangements.
Jiang Shixue, distinguished professor from Hangzhou Normal University, also a Chinese expert on Latin American studies, said preferential trade arrangements could give Peruvian products a competitive edge over those from countries without similar agreements. However, he stressed that exporters must first identify market demand to benefit from such arrangements.
While improved customs procedures and shipping connections could benefit goods trade, the upgraded agreement also lays the groundwork for closer industrial and investment cooperation.
Pan Deng, director of the Latin America and Caribbean Region Law Center at the China University of Political Science and Law, told the Global Times that bilateral trade has traditionally centered on resources, agricultural products and manufactured goods, leaving considerable room for closer cooperation in industrial chains and investment.
The upgraded agreement provides an institutional foundation for closer cooperation in industrial production, supply chains and investment, Pan said. Improved rules of origin and customs procedures could facilitate trade in intermediate goods, allowing Chinese equipment and technology to support Peru's resource-processing industries and help the country develop local processing capabilities rather than relying mainly on raw material exports.
The new global supply chains chapter also encourages multimodal transport and logistics infrastructure development, while improved investment rules and cooperation on standards could reduce barriers to cross-border business, Pan said. The two sides have agreed to launch negative-list negotiations on services and investment within two years of the protocol's entry into force, according to MOFCOM.
Wider regional cooperation
The upgrade comes as China expands its economic ties with Latin America, where trade and investment links have grown considerably in recent years.
China's trade with Latin America reached 3.93 trillion yuan in 2025, up 6.5 percent year-on-year, according to Chinese customs data.
China has established FTAs with several Latin American countries, including Chile, Peru, Costa Rica, Ecuador and Nicaragua, according to MOFCOM's free trade agreement network. The latest upgrade with Peru could provide a reference for future efforts to deepen cooperation with other countries in the region.
Pan said Chancay Port, combined with the upgraded agreement, could help Chinese businesses expand into other South American markets, including Ecuador, Colombia and Chile. The port's shipping connections, together with improved trade and investment rules, could also encourage companies to establish local processing, warehousing and distribution operations rather than relying solely on exports.
Brazil and China were discussing a proposed railway linking Peru's Chancay Port to Brazilian regions, a project that could shorten maritime trade routes to China by at least 10,000 kilometers, Reuters reported in May 2025, citing Brazilian Planning Minister Simone Tebet.
Jiang said the China-Peru FTA upgrade could serve as a positive example for broader China-Latin America economic cooperation, expressing hope that more countries in the region would pursue similar arrangements with China.
The agreement also comes as rising protectionism and uncertainty over global trade rules pose challenges to international commerce and supply chains.
At an APEC trade ministers' meeting in Suzhou in May, participating economies reaffirmed their commitment to advancing regional economic integration, including through the Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific (FTAAP) agenda, while stressing the importance of resilient supply chains and keeping trade corridors open.
The China-Peru FTA upgrade reflects China's efforts to advance trade and investment liberalization through negotiations, offering businesses more predictable rules at a time of growing uncertainty, Chinese experts noted.
The Upgrade Protocol also marks a concrete step by China to firmly uphold the rules-based multilateral trading system, and demonstrates China's resolve to work with other countries through free trade negotiations to jointly safeguard multilateralism and free trade, MOFCOM said.
