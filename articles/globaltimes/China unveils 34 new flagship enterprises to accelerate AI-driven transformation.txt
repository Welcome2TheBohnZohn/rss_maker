China unveils 34 new flagship enterprises to accelerate AI-driven transformation

China on Thursday unveiled 34 flagship smart factories across 20 provincial-level regions, expanding the initiative into more consumer-facing industries as the country steps up efforts to integrate artificial intelligence (AI) technology into manufacturing, boost productivity and accelerate the digital transformation of its industrial sector.
The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) and five other departments released the 2026 flagship smart factory cultivation list, which includes major manufacturers such as Xiaomi Auto. Pharmaceutical and apparel manufacturers were included for the first time, highlighting the initiative's widening industry coverage.
As the second batch of flagship smart factories, they have reported an average 34.4 percent increase in labor productivity and a 19.3 percent rise in production efficiency, while helping nearly 4,000 enterprises carry out digital and intelligent transformation in coordination.
Notably, industry coverage has further expanded compared with the previous batch. Beyond equipment manufacturing, steel, electronic information and other fields, pharmaceutical and apparel enterprises in the consumer goods sector were included for the first time, according to the list released by the MIIT.
The list includes smart factory projects from leading Chinese manufacturers across the vehicle, power equipment and shipbuilding sectors, including Xiaomi Auto, FAW-Volkswagen, Harbin Electric Machinery and Hudong-Zhonghua Shipbuilding.
The announcement by the six departments calls for supporting flagship smart factories in building high-quality industrial datasets, developing industrial AI agents, exploring advanced manufacturing models and establishing smart supply chains. The requirements highlight a broader shift in smart factory development, from equipment automation toward deeper integration of AI, industrial data and supply chain coordination.
The MIIT announced 15 candidate factories on November 24, 2025, so the latest batch brings the total number to 49.
Li Changan, a professor at the School of Public Administration at the University of International Business and Economics, told the Global Times on Thursday that the integration of AI with manufacturing is a major technological force driving the development of modern manufacturing. 
The inclusion of pharmaceutical and apparel manufacturers in the latest list shows that AI technologies are expanding beyond a limited number of industries and becoming increasingly integrated into traditional manufacturing sectors, according to Li.
The expert further noted that the list not only reflects the progress of AI applications in manufacturing, but also serves as a demonstration and a model for the wider industry, encouraging more traditional manufacturers to accelerate technological upgrades and transformation. This trend is expected to further strengthen China's manufacturing capabilities, improve product quality and enhance the global competitiveness of its manufacturing sector.
China has been accelerating the development of smart manufacturing. As of the end of June, the country had more than 56,000 basic-level, 9,000 advanced-level and 500 excellence-level smart factories, according data released in October, highlighting the expanding scale of its manufacturing digitalization drive
China is stepping up efforts to integrate AI into industrial production. Under an action plan released in January, the country aims to develop 1,000 high-level industrial AI agents and 100 high-quality industrial datasets by 2027, while promoting AI applications across manufacturing. The expansion of flagship smart factories is expected to help turn technological advances into practical industrial applications and extend productivity gains across supply chains.
