China's top economic planner to work on new action plan to boost domestic market demand during 2026-2030

China is working on an action plan to expand domestic demand during 2026-2030, a senior official of the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), the nation's top economic planner, said at a monthly press conference on Tuesday.
The comment came a day after China released its economic data for the third quarter, which showed that domestic demand has served as the primary engine and stabilizer of economic growth this year, contributing 74.8 percent of China's GDP growth in the first three quarters, said Zuo Gang, an official of the NDRC.
To further expand domestic demand, China will boost consumption by stabilizing employment and household incomes, upgrading purchases of new-energy vehicles and smart products, expanding the supply of eldercare, childcare, cultural, tourism and sports services, and scrapping unreasonable consumption restrictions. Inbound consumption will also be further expanded, Zuo said.
China will advance effective investment by coordinating major projects under the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-30) and the "Six Networks" initiative. Funds from ultra-long-term special treasury bonds and policy financial instruments will be channeled to improve people's livelihoods, sci-tech innovation, industrial upgrading and security resilience. Mechanisms will be refined for private firms to participate in major projects.
Efforts will also be made to introduce regulations for developing a unified national market and reducing circulation costs to smooth the circulation of supply and demand.
China released its economic data for the third quarter on Monday. 
In the January-September period, China's fixed-asset investment dropped by 7.2 percent year-on-year, while total retail sales of consumer goods and services expanded by 2.6 percent year-on-year, data from the National Bureau of Statistics showed on Monday. 
Retail sales of services rose 5 percent year-on-year in the first nine months, 4 percentage points faster than that of goods.
Policies rolled out by the NDRC are well-targeted and practical, precisely addressing the core priorities of expanding effective investment and galvanizing domestic demand, Tian Yun, a veteran economist based in Beijing, told the Global Times on Tuesday.
The policies tap into the "Six Networks" initiative to expand high-quality investment, address development gaps and cultivate durable growth engines. They also target hurdles holding back private investment, a vital means to revitalize private firms and shore up their confidence and appetite to invest, Tian said.
In the fourth quarter, China will shore up economic stability by maximizing the impact of existing policies and rolling out targeted new measures.
Authorities will push ahead with major infrastructure projects, streamline approval procedures, guarantee factors of production and speed up funding disbursement via new policy-based financial tools. Private capital will be encouraged to participate in key nuclear, hydropower and water conservancy projects.
On the new policy front, recently introduced measures including expanded re-lending quotas, housing loan subsidies and financial support for the services sector will be implemented swiftly.
