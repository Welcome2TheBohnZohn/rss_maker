TITLE:
All-out search, rescue mobilized after massive mudslide hits border port in China's Xizang

PUBLISHED:
2026-08-26 16:00:00

SOURCE:

CATEGORY:

ARTICLE TEXT:
This photo taken on Aug. 26, 2026 shows an area near Gyirong Port in southwest China's Xizang Autonomous Region. A mudslide struck near Gyirong Port in southwest China's Xizang Autonomous Region on Wednesday, with people feared missing, local authorities said. Photo: Xinhua
Chinese President Xi Jinping has ordered all-out efforts to search and rescue people who are missing after a mudslide in Nepal caused casualties at Gyirong border port in Southwest China's Xizang Autonomous Region on Wednesday morning, according to the Xinhua News Agency. 
Xi, also general secretary of the Communist Party of China (CPC) Central Committee and chairman of the Central Military Commission, called for efforts to evacuate residents at risk and provide timely medical treatment to the injured to minimize casualties, Xinhua reported. 
He made the instructions after a mudslide occurred at around 10:30 am on Wednesday in Nepal, which caused severe casualties and left people unaccounted for at Gyirong border port in Gyirong county, Xinhua said.   
At 10:59 am on Wednesday, a major disaster occurred in the Gyirong border port area of Gyirong county, Xigaze, due to a landslide on the Nepalese side of the border, resulting in casualties and people reported missing, the publicity department of Xigaze said on its WeChat account. It said that roads leading to Gyirong Port on the Chinese side, as well as communications and power supplies, have been cut off.
Three people have been found dead and 265 missing as of 8 pm on Wednesday following the mudslide, according to the local authority of Xigaze. The exact casualty toll is still being verified, while rescue operations are underway, it said.
Following the disaster, Xigaze immediately activated its emergency response mechanism and coordinated professional rescue forces to carry out urgent rescue and relief operations. Multiple professional rescue teams have been mobilized and are making their way to the affected area to conduct search-and-rescue operations. 
Efforts are also underway to treat the injured, evacuate and relocate affected residents, and carry out other emergency response measures, said the government. 
Gyirong Port is a major land port connecting China and Nepal. 
Tenzin Norbu, head of the news division of the municipal publicity department of Xigaze in Xizang, told the Global Times that the local government had dispatched personnel to the disaster-hit area, where communications had been cut off, and that he did not yet have a full picture of the situation on the ground.
A total of 145 officers and soldiers from the Xigaze detachment of the People's Armed Police have been sent to the mudslide-hit border port to assist with emergency response operations, the Global Times learned from Xizang Corps of the Chinese People's Armed Police Force.  
The mudslide on the Nepali side of the border disrupted a road leading to the Gyirong Port in Gyirong county, Xigaze. Drone footage showed that some buildings at the port had been buried by debris.
Following the disaster, the Chinese People's Liberation Army (PLA) Xizang Military Command immediately dispatched a forward command team to the affected area and mobilized emergency rescue forces. More than 130 personnel from a second rescue contingent of a unit under the Xigaze Military Subcommand, along with engineering vehicles and equipment, have been placed on standby for deployment, the Global Times learned from the command. 
The forces will make every effort to search for missing people, identify and address potential risks and hazards, and protect the lives and property of local residents.
On Wednesday, China's national disaster prevention, reduction and relief authority, together with the Ministry of Emergency Management and the National Food and Strategic Reserves Administration, allocated 30,000 disaster-relief items to Gyirong county. 
Following the disaster, China's Ministry of Emergency Management immediately coordinated and deployed rescue and response efforts, sending a working group to the scene to assist with emergency operations, CCTV News reported. 
A total of 249 personnel from the national comprehensive fire and rescue force, along with 325 personnel from China Anneng and Power China and other engineering rescue teams, were dispatched to the disaster area with rescue equipment.
The ministry also deployed equipment including emergency communications system and airborne magnetic detection system to provide technical support for communications, emergency response and rescue operations.
The National Commission for Disaster Prevention, Reduction and Relief and the Ministry of Emergency Management activated a Level II national emergency response for disaster relief. In coordination with the National Food and Strategic Reserves Administration, they allocated 30,000 pieces of central relief supplies, including tents, heating stoves, clothing and bedding, folding beds and moisture-proof mats, to support the relocation and resettlement of affected residents and disaster relief efforts.
Under the coordination of the Ministry of Emergency Management, China Anneng Group activated its emergency response mechanism and mobilized rescue teams from Beijing, Lhasa, Chengdu and Chongqing, according to report from China Central Television. 
A first contingent of 200 geological and engineering experts and emergency rescue personnel was dispatched to the disaster-hit area with 120 pieces and sets of equipment, including multifunctional reconnaissance drones, 3D laser scanners, life-detection devices and heavy engineering machinery, to carry out rescue and relief operations.
Following the disaster, a unit under the Xigaze Military Sub-command dispatched personnel to survey conditions at the site and launched rescue operations, the Global Times learned from the command.
A second group of rescuers is expected to arrive at around 8:30 pm on Wednesday, bringing 15 command and transport vehicles, four pieces of engineering machinery, one field kitchen vehicle and one ambulance.
Additional supplies include more than 1,800 individual self-heating meal packs, over 40 tons of fuel and 40 boxes of canned food.
Further rescue personnel and relief supplies have been prepared and will be dispatched to the disaster-hit area in stages.
Also, the Lhasa Mobile Fire and Rescue Detachment promptly mobilized a total of 155 personnel and 38 vehicles carrying more than 1,000 pieces of rescue equipment were dispatched to the disaster area, the Global Times learned. 
China's Ministry of Natural Resources also activated on Wednesday a Level II geological disaster response in Xizang and dispatched a working team to the site to guide rescue operations and efforts to prevent secondary geological disasters, Xinhua said. 
Situation in Nepal
The Chinese Embassy in Nepal issued a notice on Wednesday that flash floods have struck the Gyirong-Rasuwa Port along the China-Nepal border in northern Nepal, prompting the embassy to activate its emergency response mechanism and assess the safety of Chinese nationals and businesses in the affected areas. 
The embassy renewed its safety advisory for Chinese nationals and organizations in Nepal, urging them to closely monitor weather forecasts, early-warning information and road conditions, and to avoid areas particularly vulnerable to natural disasters, including mountain valleys, riverbanks and low-lying areas prone to flooding.
Chinese nationals were also advised to avoid unnecessary travel to mountainous, waterside or remote areas with limited communications, and to follow instructions from local authorities in the event of an emergency, according to the embassy. 
Chinese companies and organizations were urged to strengthen workplace safety measures, identify potential hazards, prepare emergency supplies and improve their disaster-response capabilities to protect lives and property.
As many as 72 people have been found dead in the flash flood in the Bhote Koshi River in Nepal, local police said, per Xinhua.
The flood has devastated settlements and infrastructure along the river. At least 25 people have been rescued as of Wednesday afternoon, according to the Nepali Army spokesperson, the Kathmandu Post reported. 
The government has intensified search, rescue and relief operations, mobilizing the Nepal Police, Armed Police Force, Nepali Army and district administrations. Prime Minister Balendra Shah directed agencies to ensure coordinated operations, while President Ram Chandra Paudel urged government bodies, political parties, social organizations and citizens to intensify efforts, according to the Nepali media. 
The Nepali media also reported that nearly 400 pilgrims have been left out of contact in Rasuwagadhi after an early morning flash flood swept through villages and damaged roads and other critical infrastructure along the Nepal-China border in Rasuwa.
 As many as 384 tourists, including 291 foreigners, have been reported missing after the massive flash flood swept through north Nepal's Rasuwa district, Xinhua reported on Wednesday, citing the Nepal Tourism Board.
Sunil Sharma, spokesperson at the board, said the missing foreign tourists were from India, Australia, the Netherlands, the US, Malaysia, Britain and South Africa, among others.
