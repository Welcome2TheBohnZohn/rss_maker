TOKYO, Nov. 28 (Xinhua) -- The city of Nagasaki in southwestern Japan has unveiled updated exhibition materials for its Atomic Bomb Museum, in which references to the Nanjing Massacre are replaced with the term "Nanjing Incident," Kyodo News reported.
The materials were presented Friday at a meeting of the museum's operation council.
City officials claimed at the meeting that the change was based on descriptions in mainstream Japanese history textbooks and official terminology used by Japan's Foreign Ministry.
Several council members expressed concern, warning that the revised wording could draw international criticism.
Some of the exhibition updates have already begun, with the entire process expected to be completed by mid-March 2027.
The proposed change had already drawn opposition from various quarters when it was first disclosed.
Takakage Fujita, secretary-general of the Association for Inheriting and Propagating the Murayama Statement, pointed out that the museum's operation council had been infiltrated by right-wing forces.
He noted that the Nanjing Massacre was one of the most brutal crimes committed by Japan in its modern history and stands as an emblematic event demonstrating the nature of Japan's war of aggression.
Eighty-one years after the end of the war, Nagasaki is taking advantage of the museum's exhibit renewal to replace the historical description of "massacre" with the term "Nanjing Incident," which denies the aggressive nature of the brutal act, Fujita said, adding that such a move is unacceptable under any circumstances. ■
