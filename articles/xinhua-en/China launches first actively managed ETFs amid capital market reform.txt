ARTICLE TEXT:
BEIJING, Nov. 23 (Xinhua) -- China's first batch of actively managed exchange-traded funds (ETFs) began trading on the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges on Monday, marking a new step in the country's efforts to deepen capital market reforms and attract long-term funds.
Unlike traditional ETFs, which typically track an underlying benchmark, actively managed ETFs allow fund managers to select stocks and adjust portfolios in pursuit of returns that outperform a benchmark.
Global active ETFs had reached 2.7 trillion U.S. dollars in assets under management as of the end of September 2026, accounting for 13 percent of the global ETF market, data showed. The sector has recorded net inflows for 78 consecutive months, reflecting growing demand for diversified wealth management products.
The products combine the return potential of active management with the institutional advantages of ETFs, helping broaden equity investment options and channel more medium- and long-term capital into the market, said Jiao Caixia, an official with the China Securities Regulatory Commission.
Market institutions said the launch would broaden China's range of ETF products, and provide insurers, pension funds and individual investors with relatively low-cost, convenient access to active investment strategies.
China has stepped up efforts to deepen capital market reforms in recent years, including expanding the range of publicly offered equity funds and encouraging more long-term investment.
As of the end of September 2026, 943 ETFs were listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange, with combined assets of 3.35 trillion yuan (about 499 billion U.S. dollars). Holdings by medium- and long-term investors totaled nearly 1.7 trillion yuan, accounting for more than half of the total, according to the exchange. ■
