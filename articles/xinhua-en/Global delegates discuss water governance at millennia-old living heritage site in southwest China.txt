CHENGDU, Nov. 26 (Xinhua) -- The main forum of the Dujiangyan River Strategy Forum opened Thursday in the city of Dujiangyan, southwest China's Sichuan Province, drawing about 200 scholars, officials and other delegates from home and abroad, organizers said.
Gathered in the city, known for the Dujiangyan irrigation system that was built around 256 BC and remains in use today as part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the representatives exchanged river management plans, devised strategies for ensuring water security, and promoted mutual learning among global civilizations.
A think tank report on Chinese wisdom in river governance as seen from Dujiangyan was also unveiled at the forum.
The report noted that the Dujiangyan irrigation system is the only ancient water conservancy project in the world still in use and one of the earliest successful practices of harmonious coexistence between humanity and nature. Its construction fully reflects the Chinese nation's spirit of unremitting self-improvement, fearlessness in the face of difficulties, and pioneering endeavor.
It highlighted the wisdom embodied in the ancient project, such as adapting measures to local conditions, following natural trends, harmony between man and nature, and managing water for the benefit of the people.
The report was co-authored by the Xinhua Institute, a national high-level think tank affiliated with Xinhua News Agency, and the publicity and education center of the Ministry of Water Resources.
Hu Yun, vice governor of Sichuan, said in his address that the province invested 286.9 billion yuan (around 42.76 billion U.S. dollars) in water conservancy from 2021 to 2025, with the ecological protection and governance of rivers and lakes seeing historic improvement.
The province will seize opportunities to fully promote the construction of water network pilot zones and backbone water network projects, and strive to contribute more to the practice of water conservancy quality development, Hu added.
Huo Xiaoguang, vice president of Xinhua News Agency, said the agency will fully leverage its advantages to better tell the story of China's water management in the new era to the world, share China's experience in river governance, and promote exchanges and mutual learning among civilizations around the globe.
Xinhua will also aim to turn Dujiangyan into a window for the world to understand China, thus contributing Chinese wisdom and solutions to global water governance, he noted.
Liu Dongshun, vice minister of water resources, said that since the 18th National Congress of the Communist Party of China in 2012, the country has greatly enhanced its capacity to prevent floods and droughts, further optimized its water resources allocation, and fundamentally improved the overall condition of its rivers and lakes.
Philippe Gourbesville, president of the International Association for Hydro-Environment Engineering and Research, said the Chinese government has clear goals and firm determination, and stands as an excellent model of river management internationally.
He added that China has efficiently implemented many solutions, like establishing a system of river chiefs to coordinate action and management, while effectively managing and utilizing important water conservancy infrastructure, giving full play to its role in agricultural irrigation and river transport.
China's rich experience can serve as a source of insights for France and other European countries, Gourbesville noted.
At the main forum, attendees also saw the release of an initiative calling for safeguarding water security, protecting river ecology, using technologies for river governance, channeling cooperation to pool wisdom for global governance, and fulfilling the promise to manage water for the benefit of the people.
The forum featured a main forum, a sub-forum and supporting activities, including on-site inspection of the Dujiangyan irrigation system and exhibitions showcasing achievements and innovative practices in China's river governance.
The event was co-hosted by Xinhua News Agency Sichuan Branch and the Sichuan Provincial Water Resources Department.
After more than two thousand years of continuous protection and iterative development, the Dujiangyan irrigation district, the country's largest, has expanded to 11.945 million mu (roughly 796,333 hectares), stably guaranteeing water security for production and daily life for 32 million people. ■
