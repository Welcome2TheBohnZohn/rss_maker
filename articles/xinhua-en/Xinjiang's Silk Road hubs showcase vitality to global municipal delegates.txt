ARTICLE TEXT:
URUMQI, Nov. 27 (Xinhua) -- At a Starbucks store in the Xinjiang International Grand Bazaar in Urumqi, the coffee comes with a distinctly local flavor. Opened this autumn, the outlet is the coffee chain's first intangible cultural heritage flagship in China.
From its design to its menu, the store is infused with Xinjiang elements, and many of its staff are young people from multiple ethnic groups. Since opening, it has drawn steady crowds, a vivid sign of the region's appeal to international brands.
"The design reflects the culture, and local architecture comes together in one place," said Hanum Ain Binti Zainal, director of the Project Implementation and Building Maintenance Department of Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. "For tourists, at a glance, they can understand, 'Oh, this is Urumqi' through the taste of the coffee and the interior of the space."
The store was among the stops for foreign mayors and city representatives attending the 2026 Global Mayors Dialogue in Urumqi, capital city of Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region in northwestern China. Held from Nov. 23 to 26, the event gathered more than 200 guests from 26 cities in 13 countries for a series of visits and exchanges in the region.
At the opening ceremony, representatives shared their visions for connectivity and Belt and Road cooperation. "Transport connectivity and the Belt and Road Initiative are of great significance in Azerbaijan-China relations," said Samir Seyidahmadli, deputy plenipotentiary representative of the president of Azerbaijan in the Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic.
Roundtables covered green and low-carbon urban development, mutual learning among civilizations and win-win cooperation. Saysavanh Phomeviengsay, deputy chief of the Vientiane Provincial Administration office in Laos, said the province has a solid foundation of cooperation with China in transport infrastructure, and hoped the event would deepen exchanges with Urumqi.
Tea Dzuliashvili, head of the Office of the State Representative in Georgia's Kakheti, introduced her region's efforts to cut emissions by expanding recreational spaces, restoring historical parks and rehabilitating windbreak belts. "Strengthening cooperation and sharing experience with the international community is of great practical significance."
During field visits, guests split into three groups and traveled to cities including Urumqi, Kashgar, Karamay, Yining, Horgos, Korla, Kuqa and Aksu, witnessing how the millennia-old heritage and modern vitality of Silk Road cities complement each other.
In Karamay, they stopped at the Black Oil Mountain scenic area, where bubbling oil springs and shimmering pools of crude mark the region's petroleum heritage.
"Seeing how petroleum heritage has been carefully preserved and turned into an attraction that tells the story of oil's origins gives me genuine satisfaction," said Kadyrova Rozaliia, head of the Investment Center at the Orenburg Region's non-profit Center for Entrepreneurship and Export Development, Russia.
"The shortest route from the Orenburg Region to China runs through Xinjiang. The Silk Road links our regions, and we look forward to deepening cooperation along this corridor," she said.
Junaid Alam, additional deputy commissioner for municipal services in Pakistan's Rawalpindi, was impressed by Urumqi's urban development. "Urumqi would be a good case study for my city, Rawalpindi, which also sits amid hills and terraces of the Potohar Plateau."
In Kashgar, guests watched painted-pottery demonstrations at the flowerpot bazaar, learned about ironware craftsmanship and toured a memorial hall on the old town's renovation, which follows the principle of "repairing the old as it was."
"Xinjiang has legislated to protect the ancient city of Kashgar while advancing urban modernization. This experience offers valuable lessons for Recoleta and other cities around the world," said Galvarino Iván Melo Sepúlveda, chief of staff to the mayor of Recoleta in Chile.
"I have read much about China, and it is pleasing indeed. Now I have seen what I read come true in reality -- the strength of the Chinese people, their wisdom, their love for their land, and their connection with the outside world," said Clement K.K Chilembo, assistant secretary of Zambia's Eastern Province.
Launched in 2024, the Global Mayors Dialogue has been hosted in multiple Chinese cities, evolving into a key platform for cities to share governance experience and advance practical cooperation. ■
