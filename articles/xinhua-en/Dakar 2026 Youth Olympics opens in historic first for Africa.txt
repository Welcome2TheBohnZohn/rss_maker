Dakar 2026 Youth Olympics opens in historic first for Africa

ARTICLE TEXT:

DAKAR, Oct. 31 (Xinhua) -- The Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games (YOG) opened on Saturday, marking the first time an Olympic event has been held on African soil.
The opening ceremony took place at the 55,000-seat Abdoulaye Wade Stadium in Diamniadio, a new city located 30 km from Senegal's capital of Dakar.
According to the organizing committee, Khady Niang, the ceremony director, said the show allows Africa to "tell the world who we are, where we come from and where we want to go."
The two-hour show started at 6:30 p.m. local time, marked by the arrival of International Olympic Committee President Kirsty Coventry and Senegalese President Bassirou Diomaye Faye.
Following the parade of athletes, the spotlight turned to a story of three generations passing the baton from one to the next: Alkebulan, the mother, represented the values of ancestral Africa; her son, Keb, embodied Africa today; and Sitoe, a young Senegalese girl, symbolized hope for the future.
The opening act paid tribute to the Africa of the past, immediately electrifying the crowd as Alkebulan rode into the arena on horseback.
Iconic instruments, including the sabar, the tama (talking drum) and the djembe, introduced the continent's rich musical heritage, while traditional attire from Mali, Ghana, Senegal and South Africa showcased the diversity of African cultural traditions.
The momentum built with performances of Senegal's traditional faux-lion dance and wrestlers' dance, culminating in a finale in which 360 dancers came together to form the map of Africa.
In the second act, Keb arrived on a motorcycle in a scene inspired by the Senegalese film Touki Bouki. He led Sitoe and the spectators into the world of contemporary African art, striking a chord with younger generations.
As Keb and Sitoe strolled through the streets, a series of artistic performances featuring live painting, music, dance and hip-hop brought the scene to life.
The festivities were followed by the parade of flags representing the participating national Olympic committees, with Greece and the Refugee Olympic Team leading the procession and host nation Senegal bringing up the rear.
"For years, we have been talking about this moment -- the first Olympic sporting event on African soil and now here we are. This historic moment is for Senegal, for Africa, for our entire Olympic movement, and to all of our young amazing athletes," Coventry said in her speech.
"This is the Africa that I am so proud and so excited for the world to experience," she added, voicing hope for young talents to "believe in yourselves, to dream big and to do things you never thought were possible."
After Senegalese President Faye officially declared the fourth Summer Youth Olympic Games open, the stadium erupted in vibrant Afrobeats music.
The celebration continued with sports demonstrations, a choreographed formation of the Olympic rings and the Dakar 2026 emblem, the Olympic anthem, the raising of the Olympic flag and the solemn reading of the Youth Olympic oath.
The much-anticipated lighting of the Olympic cauldron came last.
Senegalese fencer Abdourahmane Marieme Sy, 17, and judoka Aicha Diop, 15, jointly carried the final torch, receiving the flame from canoeist Yves Bourhis, triple jumper Sally Sarr and Amadou Dia Ba, Senegal's first and, to date, only Olympic medalist.
As the Olympic cauldron was lit, dancers wearing masks and futuristic costumes created a pathway for an Afrofuturism-inspired fashion show. The act also featured a standout performance by a 12-year-old drummer whose solo drew loud applause.
At 8:30 p.m., the performers took their final bow, bringing the ceremony to a jubilant close.
Held in Dakar, Diamniadio and Saly from Oct. 31 to Nov. 13, the fourth edition of the YOG will bring together more than 2,600 athletes from over 200 delegations. A total of 25 sports will feature on the official competition program, alongside 10 engagement sports.
Dakar 2026 will also feature the Youth Olympic debuts of wushu, baseball5 and skateboarding, as well as the Refugee Olympic Team's historic first appearance at the Games.
China has sent 66 athletes to compete in 21 sports at the Dakar Youth Olympics. Wushu athlete Lin Yilei and swimmer Chang Mohan, both 15, were named China's flagbearers for the opening ceremony. ■
