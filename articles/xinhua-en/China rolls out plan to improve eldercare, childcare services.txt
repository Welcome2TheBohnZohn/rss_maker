China rolls out plan to improve eldercare, childcare services

ARTICLE TEXT:
BEIJING, Nov. 23 (Xinhua) -- China on Monday unveiled an action plan to improve services for the elderly and young children, as the country responds to an aging population and seeks to ease the burden of raising children.
The plan, jointly issued by four government departments including the National Development and Reform Commission, sets a series of targets for 2030, including more accessible community-based eldercare services and a broader supply of affordable childcare.
By 2030, the coverage rate of community-based eldercare institutions and facilities is expected to reach 70 percent. The enrollment rate of children under the age of three in childcare services is targeted to rise by 6 percentage points from its 2025 level.
The plan aims to essentially establish a framework for inclusive eldercare and childcare services that are accessible in both urban and rural areas, reasonably distributed, affordable and of assured quality.
The measures come as China faces growing demand for care services amid demographic changes. At the end of 2025, the country had 323.38 million people aged 60 and above, accounting for 23 percent of its total population, according to the National Bureau of Statistics.
Experts believe the figures underscore the increasing need for accessible and professional eldercare services.
To improve elderly care infrastructure, the plan vows to support building new facilities and upgrading existing ones. Particular attention will be given to 2,000 public elderly care institutions that are aging and in need of renovation, targeting improvements to the standard of care they provide and tightening their safety management measures.
On childcare, the plan responds to widespread public concern about the difficulty of raising young children. It supports the conversion of eligible kindergartens to also serve children aged two to three, and calls for the development of nursery facilities within a 15-minute reach of residential areas -- the "15-minute childcare circle."
In recent years, China has worked to encourage births and ease the strain on families. The government has extended maternity and paternity leave, introduced cash subsidies for newborns and simplified marriage registration. ■
