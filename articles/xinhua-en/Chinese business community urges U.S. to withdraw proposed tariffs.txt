Chinese business community urges U.S. to withdraw proposed tariffs

ARTICLE TEXT:
BEIJING, Nov. 27 (Xinhua) -- The Chinese business community has urged the United States to withdraw its proposed additional tariffs on China and 13 other economies following a Section 301 investigation into alleged "structural excess capacity" in manufacturing sectors, the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT) said Friday.
The Office of the United States Trade Representative recently announced findings of its Section 301 investigation and proposed imposing additional tariffs on 14 economies, including China.
"The Chinese business community firmly opposes this," a CCPIT spokesperson said, adding that the community rejects the claim of "structural excess capacity" as a false narrative and opposes the U.S. side using the allegation as a pretext to interfere in normal China-U.S. economic and trade exchanges.
Assessments of industrial supply and demand conditions should take into account factors including global market demand, technological progress and investment cycles, the spokesperson said. Trade surpluses are shaped by multiple factors such as the international division of labor, market demand and macroeconomic conditions, and trade surpluses alone are insufficient to determine structural excess capacity.
Tariff wars and trade wars benefit no one, the spokesperson said, adding that broad-based tariff hikes will push up procurement costs for U.S. downstream enterprises, place additional burdens on consumers and disrupt global industrial and supply chains.
The Chinese business community calls on the United States to withdraw the proposed tariff measures and resolve relevant issues through consultations with China on the basis of equality, respect and mutual benefit, to maintain stable and sound China-U.S. economic and trade relations, the spokesperson said.
The CCPIT will continue to follow the concerns of Chinese and U.S. enterprises and foster practical cooperation and communication among the business communities of China, the United States and other countries, the spokesperson added. ■
