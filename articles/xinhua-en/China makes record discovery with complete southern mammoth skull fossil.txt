China makes record discovery with complete southern mammoth skull fossil

ARTICLE TEXT:
XINING, Nov. 28 (Xinhua) -- Chinese researchers have discovered the country's first complete fossilized skull of a southern mammoth in the Gonghe Basin, located on the northeastern edge of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau.
The skull, measuring 75 centimeters long and 70 centimeters wide and paired with a set of tusks, was unearthed in August by a team from the Qinghai provincial geological survey institute. Researchers spotted a 15-centimeter bone fragment at a cliff while tracing paleontological clues in the Gonghe Basin.
Subsequent identification by the Institute of Vertebrate Paleontology and Paleoanthropology under the Chinese Academy of Sciences confirmed the specimen as a southern mammoth dating back approximately 2 million to 1.3 million years.
Remarkably well-preserved and highly complete, the fossil represents a departure from previously found fragmented pieces, offering crucial evidence for understanding the migration and evolution of mammoths across the Eurasian continent.
This newly discovered specimen is expected to enable the first direct comparison between East Asian and Western European skull specimens from the same evolutionary stage, noted Pan Tong, chief scientist at the Bureau of Geology and Mineral Exploration and Development of Qinghai Province.
It adds a crucial link to the physical evidence chain needed to verify the west-to-east migration route of southern mammoths and to reconstruct the timeline of their spread to the northeastern end of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, according to Pan.
Zhang Hailong, head of the research team, said the team will conduct meticulous specimen restoration, 3D scanning, and multidisciplinary testing to deepen research into the paleoecological evolution of the plateau. ■
