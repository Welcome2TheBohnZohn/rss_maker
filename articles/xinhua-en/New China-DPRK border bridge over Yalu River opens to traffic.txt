TITLE:
New China-DPRK border bridge over Yalu River opens to traffic

SHENYANG, Oct. 15 (Xinhua) -- The new China-DPRK Yalu River bridge officially opened to traffic on Thursday, with both sides jointly holding an opening ceremony on this cross-border artery.
The cable-stayed bridge over the Yalu River serves as a cross-border transport link between the city of Dandong in northeast China's Liaoning Province and the city of Sinuiju in North Pyongan Province of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK).
The double-pylon and double-cable-plane bridge features a 28.5-meter-wide deck designed with four lanes for two-way traffic, while its water navigation clearance is designed for vessels of up to the 3,000-tonne class.
Chinese Premier Li Qiang hailed the bridge as a new landmark of China-DPRK friendship. "The bridge is bound to become a new landmark symbolizing the traditional friendship between China and the DPRK," Li said in a video speech at the opening ceremony.
In his remarks, Li called on China and the DPRK to take the opening of the new bridge as an opportunity to continuously optimize port clearance, enhance connectivity, tap the potential for cooperation in border areas, and improve the well-being of the two peoples.
China is willing to work with the DPRK to continue making efforts to implement the important consensus reached by the top leaders of the two parties and countries, strengthen strategic communication, consolidate political mutual trust, expand practical cooperation, and enhance multilateral coordination, so as to continuously deepen the development of China-DPRK relations and bring more benefits to the two peoples, Li said.
The new bridge will further facilitate trade and people-to-people exchanges between the two countries, Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said on Wednesday.
China and the DPRK are friendly neighbors connected by mountains and rivers, and the new bridge is an important facility for connectivity between the two countries, Mao told a regular press briefing.
Dandong boasts China's largest border trade port with the DPRK. Before the new road bridge, the Yalu River Bridge, also known as the China-DPRK Friendship Bridge, served as a combined road-rail bridge. The old one, however, could only accommodate trucks weighing no more than 20 tonnes for one-way road traffic.
China and the DPRK agreed in 2010 to build a new road bridge downstream from the old one. ■
