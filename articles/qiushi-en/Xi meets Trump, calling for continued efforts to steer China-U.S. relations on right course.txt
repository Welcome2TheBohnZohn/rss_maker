Xi meets Trump, calling for continued efforts to steer China-U.S. relations on right course
Chinese President Xi Jinping meets with U.S. President Donald Trump, who is in China for the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders' Meeting, in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province, Nov. 19, 2026. [Xinhua/Yan Yan]
SHENZHEN -- Chinese President Xi Jinping met with U.S. President Donald Trump on Thursday in Shenzhen, calling for continued efforts to jointly steer the giant ship of China-U.S. relations in the right direction toward the future.
Trump is in the southern Chinese city for the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders' Meeting on Nov. 18-19.
During the meeting, Xi said that China-U.S. relations have generally maintained a stable and positive trajectory, bringing tangible benefits to people of both countries and injecting stability and certainty into the world.
Xi said that he and Trump had jointly explored the right way for China and the United States, as two major countries, to get along, established a new positioning of a constructive China-U.S. relationship of strategic stability and given it new meaning based on respect, fairness and reciprocity.
The new positioning has been welcomed by the people of both China and the United States, as well as by the international community, he added.
Xi stressed that building a constructive China-U.S. relationship of strategic stability is an unprecedented and pioneering endeavor, adding that the key to sustaining the current positive momentum lies in taking concrete action to implement the consensus reached by the two heads of state.
The two sides agreed to map out an action plan for building a constructive China-U.S. relationship of strategic stability.
Xi called on the two sides to strengthen communication on economic and trade matters, address each other's concerns, and work toward mutual benefit.
The cooperation between the two countries on counternarcotics, the repatriation of illegal immigrants, and combating telecommunications and online fraud has yielded positive results, Xi said, adding that it can be further deepened based on respect, fairness, and reciprocity.
China and the United States should strengthen communication and coordination to guard against safety risks of artificial intelligence (AI) and ensure that AI always remains under human control, he said.
On the Taiwan question, the U.S. side should do the right thing and avoid major disruptions to China-U.S. relations, Xi said.
Trump said he and Xi have jointly guided the development of U.S.-China relations, and both sides should maintain the positive momentum of the stable development in bilateral relations.
The United States does not support "Taiwan independence," Trump said.
