Xi says China, Australia should be mutually trusted partners
Chinese President Xi Jinping meets with Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province, Nov. 20, 2026. Albanese is in China for the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders' Meeting, held from Wednesday to Thursday. [Xinhua/Li Xiang]
SHENZHEN -- Chinese President Xi Jinping said Friday that China and Australia should be cooperative partners who trust each other and help each other succeed, as the two countries have no historical grievances or conflicts of fundamental interests.
China-Australia relations are stable at present, said Xi when meeting with Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese in Shenzhen, south China's Guangdong Province.
Albanese is in China for the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders' Meeting, held from Wednesday to Thursday.
As next year marks the 55th anniversary of diplomatic relations between China and Australia, Xi called on the two sides to achieve greater progress in bilateral relations from a new starting point.
Xi said that in a volatile and turbulent world, the two countries should enhance mutual trust, accommodate each other's core interests and major concerns, and keep to the right direction of friendship and cooperation.
He called for deepening mutually beneficial cooperation in pursuit of the two countries' respective development goals, advancing traditional cooperation in agriculture, energy and minerals, and expanding cooperation in emerging areas such as the green economy, new energy, the digital industry, artificial intelligence (AI) and climate change response.
Efforts should be made to promote friendship between the two countries through expanded people-to-people exchanges, Xi said, noting that the two sides had agreed to designate 2027 as the China-Australia Year of Tourism.
He also emphasized the need to strengthen multilateral coordination in addressing global challenges, advance the building of an Asia-Pacific community, and jointly uphold the international system with the United Nations at its core and the international order based on international law.
Albanese said that the improvement and development of Australia-China relations serves the interests of both sides and brings tangible benefits to the peoples of both countries.
He said that Australia stands ready to maintain close high-level exchanges with China, strengthen practical cooperation in trade, investment, AI and other areas, deepen people-to-people exchanges, respect each other and properly handle differences, so as to make Australia-China relations an example for countries with different systems to develop constructive relations.
