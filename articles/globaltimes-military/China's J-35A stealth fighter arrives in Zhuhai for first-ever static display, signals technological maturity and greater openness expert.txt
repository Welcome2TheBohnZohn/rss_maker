China's J-35A stealth fighter arrives in Zhuhai for first-ever static display, signals technological maturity and greater openness: expert

China's J-35A stealth fighter arrives in Zhuhai, South China's Guangdong Province on November 28, 2026. Photo: CCTV News
China's J-35A stealth fighter arrived in Zhuhai, South China's Guangdong Province, on Saturday morning. It will make its first-ever appearance in a static display at the 16th China International Aviation and Aerospace Exhibition, offering visitors from around the world a close-up look at the aircraft, CCTV News reported on Saturday.
Notably, the aircraft displayed this time bears the insignia of Aviation Industry Corporation of China (AVIC), the developer of the aircraft, on its vertical tail fins rather than that of the Chinese Air Force. This could indicate that AVIC is presenting the aircraft in its capacity as a potential export product, observers said.
"Whether the J-35A will eventually be exported remains to be seen. However, I believe an export version of the J-35A would demonstrate stronger overall combat capabilities than other fifth-generation fighters currently available on the international arms market," military expert Fu Qianshao told the Global Times on Saturday.
Compared with other mainstream fifth-generation fighters, the J-35A has advantages in several areas, such as engine performance, structural weight, combat radius, maneuverability, power supply capacity, radar detection capabilities and stealth performance, making it a potentially strong competitor in the global arms market, Fu said.
He also noted that the J-35A outperforms foreign counterparts in maneuverability, power supply capacity and radar detection capabilities. "In particular, it is equipped with a gallium nitride active electronically scanned array radar, which gives it stronger long-range detection capabilities," he said.
Images released from Saturday's display also show that the J-35A features a notably smooth exterior design and more refined treatment of its panel joints. Fu said these details indicate a smaller radar cross-section. "Its sophisticated manufacturing standards determine that its stealth performance will be superior to that of comparable foreign fifth-generation fighters," he said.
In Fu's view, if the J-35A is introduced to the international arms market, it will have broad export prospects. "If it is exported, I believe priority will be given to countries friendly toward China, helping them significantly enhance their air defense and combat capabilities," he said.
The J-35A made its public debut and delivered an impressive flight demonstration at the 15th China International Aviation and Aerospace Exhibition two years ago. Performing a series of maneuvers, including large-radius rolls, high-angle climbs and near-vertical ascents, the silver-gray fighter streaked across the sky, leaving a deep impression on spectators, according to CCTV News.
Over the past two years, the fighter aircraft has attracted widespread attention both in China and overseas for its sleek appearance and combat capabilities.
The J-35A is a new-generation medium-sized stealth multirole fighter independently developed by AVIC. Featuring an integrated design combining its overall configuration, aerodynamics and stealth capabilities, the aircraft has a single-seat, twin-engine configuration with a blended wing-body design, twin outward-canted swept vertical tails and all-moving horizontal stabilizers, per report.
As an important component of China's stealth and counter-stealth combat systems, the aircraft is primarily designed for air-superiority missions while also retaining air-to-ground capabilities. Its main tasks include gaining and maintaining air superiority, engaging enemy fighter jets and ground- and sea-based air defense forces, as well as intercepting airborne targets such as fighter jets, bombers and cruise missiles, military expert Song Zhongping told the Global Times on Saturday.
The fact that the J-35A is being presented in a static display for the first time demonstrates that the aircraft has reached a relatively mature stage of technological development and that a high degree of openness is now possible, Song added.
Song noted that the J-35A's naval variant, the J-35, has already completed takeoffs and landings on the Fujian, China's electromagnetic catapults-equipped aircraft carrier.
Wang Yongqing, chief expert at AVIC's Shenyang Aircraft Design and Research Institute, previously told media that the J-35A is designed to match the world's advanced aviation equipment standards. Based on a design philosophy that balances individual aircraft capabilities with coordinated operations within an integrated combat system, the fighter incorporates a range of emerging technologies to meet the demands of new operational scenarios.
The development program has also made extensive use of model-based systems engineering methods and approaches to accelerate development and iteration, Wang said, adding that the aircraft is designed to meet China's operational requirements.
