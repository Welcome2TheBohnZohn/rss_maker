ARTICLE TEXT:
At the invitation of Deputy Prime Minister of Singapore Gan Kim Yong, Member of the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee and Vice Premier of the State Council Ding Xuexiang will visit Singapore from November 22 to 23, and co-chair with Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong the 22nd meeting of the China-Singapore Joint Council for Bilateral Cooperation, the 27th meeting of the China-Singapore Suzhou Industrial Park Joint Steering Council, the 18th meeting of the China-Singapore Tianjin Eco-City Joint Steering Council, and the 10th meeting of the China-Singapore (Chongqing) Demonstration Initiative on Strategic Connectivity Joint Steering Council.
Xinhua News Agency: The 33rd APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting was successfully held in Shenzhen. We notice that the Shenzhen Outlook on building an Asia-Pacific community put forward at the meeting has sparked heated discussion worldwide. Could you share more details on that?
Guo Jiakun: On November 19, the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting was successfully held in Shenzhen. President Xi Jinping chaired the meeting and delivered important remarks, in which he elaborated on China’s proposals on building an Asia-Pacific community and announced China’s voluntary measures to support Asia-Pacific cooperation. The meeting adopted the 2026 APEC Leaders’ Shenzhen Outlook — an ambitious, pragmatic and comprehensive document. This is the most important outcome of APEC “China Year,” and marks a new milestone in APEC cooperation.
The Shenzhen Outlook focuses on four major areas “trade and economy, connectivity, innovation, and development,” and eight key aspects “Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific, physical connectivity, institutional connectivity, people-to-people connectivity, digital economy, emerging technologies, capacity building, and sustainable growth,” setting up the pillars for building an Asia-Pacific community. The APEC members reaffirmed their commitment to APEC as the premier forum for regional economic cooperation, and to making continuous efforts to move toward the goal of building an Asia-Pacific community. They also extended their sincere appreciation to China, the host country, and the people and city of Shenzhen, Guangdong Province for their warm hospitality and thorough arrangements.
China will work with Asia-Pacific partners to implement the common understandings reached at the meeting, work steadily toward the goal of Asia-Pacific community, strive for the wellbeing of the people in China and the Asia-Pacific, and make greater contributions to world peace and development.
Asahi Shimbun: Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said at a press conference in Shenzhen yesterday that the Japanese government’s position on the Taiwan question remains consistent with the 1972 Japan-China Joint Statement. She also indicated that there has been no change in the status of the four political documents between Japan and China, which serve as the foundational documents for bilateral relations. What is China’s comment?
Guo Jiakun: Abiding by the four political documents between China and Japan is the political responsibility and legal obligation that Japan must fulfill. This should be not just reflected in remarks, but more importantly, implemented with concrete actions. For Japan, the pressing priority is to correct the erroneous words and moves on the Taiwan question immediately, adopt a correct attitude on historical issues, and form an objective and rational perception on China.
JoongAng Ilbo: Yesterday the leaders of the U.S., China and Russia held a successful summit in Shenzhen. It’s reported that one of the four points made by China is that the three countries should maintain regular high-level exchanges. How would China respond if the U.S. were to host a second trilateral summit during the G20 Miami Summit next month?
Guo Jiakun: The China-Russia-U.S. Summit was successfully held yesterday. China has released a readout, which you may refer to.
AFP: The United Nations International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar is guilty of genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority. What is China’s comment on the ruling?
Guo Jiakun: China has noted the judgment of the International Court of Justice. Given its complex historical, ethnic and religious dimensions, the Rakhine state issue needs to be resolved prudently through a holistic approach. The international community should make joint efforts to this end and avoid complicating the issue. China stands ready to continue supporting relevant parties in stepping up dialogue and consultation, and to play a constructive role in promoting the repatriation of the displaced people from Rakhine state.
It needs to be stressed that China believes international judicial institutions, as important instruments for upholding international fairness and justice, should exercise their powers with full prudence in accordance with law. China has concerns regarding such issues as ultra vires jurisdiction and overreach in interpretation, which are not conducive to the settlement of disputes.
Global Times: The Chinese side just announced that Vice Premier Ding Xuexiang will visit Singapore and co-chair the mechanism meetings on bilateral cooperation. Could you share more information on this?
Guo Jiakun: China and Singapore are friendly neighbors and important cooperation partners. Under the strategic guidance of the leaders of both sides, China-Singapore relations have maintained a sound momentum of growth, yielding fruitful outcomes in cooperation across various fields. President Xi Jinping just had an important meeting with Prime Minister Lawrence Wong this morning, charting the course for a new stage of development for bilateral ties. The mechanism meetings on bilateral cooperation are the apex platform coordinating collaboration between our two countries. China stands ready to work with Singapore to make good use of the meetings to deliver on our leaders’ strategic common understandings, formulate coordinated plans for cooperation in the next phase, unlock fresh impetus for the modernization drive in both countries, seek greater progress in our all-round high-quality future-oriented partnership, and contribute to development and prosperity in the region.
AFP: This is a follow-up on the question about the International Court of Justice’s ruling on Myanmar. The Chinese ICJ jurist Xue Hanqin voted in Myanmar’s favor on all but one count. So my question is, was Ms. Xue acting on the instructions from the Chinese government?
Guo Jiakun: The International Court of Justice is the principal judicial organ of the United Nations. Its judges serve in their individual capacity and perform their duties independently. Judge Xue Hanqin’s expression of her views is grounded in international law and the facts of the case, and informed by her expertise. This is consistent with the court’s established procedures and practices, and reflects the proper fulfillment of her responsibilities.
Beijing Youth Daily: A total of ten foreign nationals received the Third Orchid Awards, according to selection results released by the Chinese side today. Would you like to comment on this?
Guo Jiakun: We congratulate the laureates of the 2026 Orchid Awards. Hailing from all over the world, they share the same commitment to dispelling prejudice and nurturing understanding through cultural exchanges. Through decades of dedicated efforts, they have built bridges of friendship connecting the people of China and other countries.
Established in 2022, the Orchid Awards have become an international cultural platform that delivers on the Global Civilization Initiative. China is ready to work with all parties to foster solidarity and enhance cooperation among different civilizations, cultures and peoples to seek common progress and build a community with a shared future for humanity.
Asahi Shimbun: The China-Russia-U.S. Heads of State Declaration toward Peace said that the International Criminal Court and other similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction. I have two questions. First, how would China define the “abuse” of jurisdiction? Second, what exactly do “other similar bodies” refer to?
Guo Jiakun: On the International Criminal Court, China’s position is consistent, clear and above-board. China supports international judicial bodies in playing a constructive role for upholding international rule of law. That said, China stands that no international judicial body should violate the principle of state consent, abuse their jurisdiction, engage in politicization, or apply double standards.
