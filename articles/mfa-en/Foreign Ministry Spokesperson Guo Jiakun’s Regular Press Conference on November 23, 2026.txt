ARTICLE TEXT:
At the invitation of Member of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee and Minister of Foreign Affairs Wang Yi, Minister of Foreign Affairs of the Netherlands Tom Berendsen will visit China from November 23 to 25.
CCTV: To follow up on your announcement that Minister of Foreign Affairs of the Netherlands Tom Berendsen will visit China, how does China assess its relations with the Netherlands? What is China’s expectation of the visit?
Guo Jiakun: China and the Netherlands, both major economies in the world, are important cooperation partners. Over recent years, under the strategic guidance of the two heads of state, the two countries have engaged in close high-level exchanges, made steady progress in practical cooperation in areas of agriculture, water conservation, environmental protection, and science and technology, and consistently deepened cultural and people-to-people exchanges.
This will be Foreign Minister Berendsen’s first visit to China after taking office. China hopes to have in-depth exchanges of views with the Netherlands during the visit on bilateral relations and international and regional issues of mutual interest, to enhance understanding and mutual trust, deepen cooperation in various areas, and promote the sustained, sound and steady development of the open and pragmatic partnership for comprehensive cooperation for greater benefit to both peoples.
Anadolu Agency: About the trilateral meeting in Shenzhen last week, the leaders’ declaration was comprehensive in terms of scope affecting a wide range of issues about global governance. Do you think this Group 3 (G3) type of dialogue, if established as a regular mechanism, might weaken existing multilateral settings, considering that international organizations and other countries are left out of the table?
Guo Jiakun: We have released information on the China-Russia-U.S. summit. China always maintains that all countries, big or small, are equal, and stays committed to fostering greater democracy in international relations and building an equal and orderly multipolar world. China does not engage in geopolitical rivalry and seeks no sphere of influence. What it pursues are friendly exchanges with all countries on the basis of mutual respect and equality. We will continue to practice true multilateralism and contribute to joint efforts towards building a community with a shared future for humanity.
Polish Press Agency: Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said last week that he will visit China in early December. Could the Chinese side confirm this and provide details? Against the backdrop of China-EU trade frictions and the Ukraine crisis, what concrete outcomes does China hope to achieve through this visit?
Guo Jiakun: High-level exchanges are vital to the development of China-Poland relations. On the visit you mentioned, I have no information to share at the moment.
China Daily: On November 22, 2021, President Xi Jinping and leaders of ASEAN countries held a special summit to commemorate the 30th anniversary of China-ASEAN dialogue relations and announced the elevation of the China-ASEAN relationship to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership. A few days ago, Premier Li Qiang attended the 29th China-ASEAN Summit, where both sides issued a joint statement on commemorating the 5th anniversary of the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership. How does China assess the cooperation over the past five years, and what are its expectations for the future development of China-ASEAN relations?
Guo Jiakun: The establishment of the China-ASEAN Comprehensive Strategic Partnership is a milestone in our bilateral relations. Over the past five years, under the strategic guidance of leaders on both sides, China and ASEAN have promoted the tradition of friendship and mutual assistance, upheld the fundamental principles of openness and mutual benefit, and embraced the trend of innovation-driven development. As a result, our pragmatic cooperation has reached a new height. Trade between the two sides last year exceeded US$1 trillion. The China-ASEAN Free Trade Area 3.0 Upgrade Protocol will soon enter into force. Major projects such as the Jakarta-Bandung high-speed railway and the China-Laos railway have been implemented. And the Pinglu Canal has officially opened to navigation. Our two sides have successfully held the China-ASEAN Year of People-to-People Exchanges and implemented the “ASEAN Visa” program, further facilitating exchanges between the peoples. Solid progress has been made in building a community with a shared future between China and ASEAN countries both in bilateral and plurilateral formats, laying a solid foundation for building a closer China-ASEAN community with a shared future.
This year marks the beginning of China’s 15th Five-Year Plan and the ASEAN Community Vision 2045. Next year will mark the 60th anniversary of the establishment of ASEAN. All these present important opportunities for the development of China-ASEAN relations. China will work with ASEAN countries to enhance solidarity and mutual trust, expand consensus on cooperation, build a robust China-ASEAN Free Trade Area 3.0, and effectively implement the plan of action for the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership. We stand ready to deepen cooperation in key fields such as artificial intelligence, digital economy, connectivity and energy security, jointly build a peaceful, safe and secure, prosperous, beautiful and amicable home, and bring greater stability and certainty to regional peace and development.
Reuters: The UN climate conference COP31 ended on Sunday without any deal, which many countries are saying is a disappointing outcome. What is China’s reaction to COP failing to arrive at a deal this year?
Guo Jiakun: On November 22, the 31st meeting of the Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change was concluded in Antalya, Türkiye. At the meeting, decisions were adopted on issues such as just transition, mitigation, adaptation and finance. The meeting, however, ended without an overarching political deal. Despite being somewhat disappointed, China believes there is no reason to lose faith in the multilateral process. Let me stress that tackling climate change requires global effort. Green and low-carbon transition is an irreversible trend. All parties need to commit to multilateralism, uphold the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities, and find more political will and common ground to tackle climate change in solidarity and deliver the Paris Agreement on the basis of respecting multilateral rules, science and national realities.
China made enormous effort to facilitate a successful COP31. President Xi’s Special Representative and Vice President Han Zheng attended the Antalya Leaders’ Summit, who reaffirmed China’s commitment to carbon peak and carbon neutrality and put forward China’s proposal on global climate governance in the national statement. The Chinese delegation played a constructive role as well by engaging fully and substantively in the negotiations, reaching out to relevant parties, and moving the course of the meeting in a positive direction.
China is committed to multilateralism, international cooperation and green transition, and will continue to act responsibly, work with all parties to tackle challenges and difficulties and promote the green and low-carbon transition and sustainable development worldwide.
Anadolu Agency: In the trilateral summit, leaders of China, the U.S. and Russia declared that they recognize the principle of state consent and respecting immunities of state officials and concurred that the International Criminal Court and other similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction. The ICC led investigations of war crimes and crimes against humanity and issued arrest warrants for Israel’s prime minister and former defense minister. Does China see these court orders as abuse of judicial power? What should the limits of immunity for state officials be regarding these cases?
Guo Jiakun: On the Palestinian question, China supports all efforts that are conducive to realizing fairness and justice and upholding the authority of international law. China always maintains that the International Criminal Court should adhere to an objective and impartial stance, exercise its mandate in accordance with law, and interpret and apply the Rome Statute and general international law in their entirety, in good faith and in line with consistent standards.
Anadolu Agency: Ukrainian leader Volodymyr Zelenskyy said that it was a mistake to talk about the Ukraine issue in a meeting where his country was not on the table. So my question is, in the trilateral summit, did the leaders talk about the situation in Ukraine?
China’s position on the Ukraine crisis has been consistent and clear. We always believe that dialogue and negotiation is the only viable way out of the crisis, and support all efforts for peace.
Reuters: The Chinese delegation at COP31 was mostly absent from public events during the two weeks of conference. Why has China chosen to have such a low-key presence? And why has China decided against hosting COP in 2028?
Guo Jiakun: On your first question, as I just said, China has made enormous effort to facilitate a successful COP31. President Xi’s Special Representative and Vice President Han Zheng attended the Antalya Leaders’ Summit, who reaffirmed China’s commitment to carbon peak and carbon neutrality and put forward China’s proposal on global climate governance in the national statement. The Chinese delegation played a constructive role as well by engaging fully and substantively in the negotiations, reaching out to relevant parties, and moving the course of the meeting in a positive direction.
As for your second question, I have no further information beyond our earlier response.
Reuters: U.S. President Trump has said the U.S. wants to sell only older chips to China, and that his administration was in discussion with China about this. What is China’s reaction to that comment, and is China indeed engaging in such talks for older chips?
Guo Jiakun: The Chinese side has made clear its position on U.S. export control measures.

The following question was raised by Reuters after the press conference: It’s reported that Nepal plans to convene an international summit to raise money for reconstruction after the recent floods. Nepal said China has indicated its willingness to support the effort but hasn’t quantified that yet. Nepal also said China had agreed to share more early warning and climate data. Does the ministry have any comment?
Guo Jiakun: China and Nepal are friendly neighbors connected by mountains and rivers. In the wake of the large-scale mudslide disaster in northern Nepal, China immediately announced emergency assistance for the affected areas in Nepal and conducted international rescue cooperation. The Chinese government provided Nepal with cash assistance and five batches of emergency relief supplies, sent over 20 experts specializing in tunnel rescue and forensic identification, and continuously shared data on meteorological, hydrological and geological conditions. Meanwhile, people from various sectors in China also extended a helping hand, conveying their care and support to the people in Nepal.
The disaster has caused severe damage, and reconstruction faces enormous challenges. China is closely following the developments and feels for Nepal over the disaster. On top of the emergency assistance already provided, China will actively support Nepal’s reconstruction effort to help people in the disaster-hit areas rebuild their homes and return work and life to normal as early as possible. China will also help Nepal improve capability in disaster prevention and mitigation to better address the risks and challenges brought by natural disasters.
