ARTICLE TEXT:
CCTV: To follow up on the announcement of the China-Russia-U.S. Shenzhen Summit, could you share with us the detailed arrangement and China’s expectation?
Mao Ning: This will be the first ever face-to-face meeting between the Chinese, Russian and U.S. heads of state. The three presidents will have an in-depth exchange of views on safeguarding world peace and enabling strategic stability in major-country relations. The summit is of groundbreaking significance.
China, Russia, and the U.S. are founding members of the United Nations and permanent members of the Security Council. In history, the three countries fought side by side to win the World Anti-Fascist War. Under the current circumstances, the three countries shoulder important responsibilities to work together in addressing global challenges and promoting world peace and stability. It is in the interest of the three countries to strengthen communication, manage differences and maintain stable relations. This is also the expectation of the international community and is conducive to global stability and dialogue and cooperation.
CGTN: Recently, Chinese special envoys and special representative visited countries in the Middle East, the Gulf region, the Red Sea and the Horn of Africa. Could you share more details?
Mao Ning: Recently, Special Envoy of the Chinese Government on the Middle East Issue Zhai Jun, Special Representative of the Chinese Government on African Affairs Liu Xianfa, and Special Envoy for the Horn of Africa Affairs of the Foreign Ministry Hu Changchun visited a total of seven countries in the Middle East, the Gulf region and the Horn of Africa. It is a major diplomatic effort taken by China under the guidance of initiatives and propositions on safeguarding and promoting peace and stability in the Middle East and the four principles on the constructive participation of relevant parties in the resolution of African hotspot issues put forward by President Xi Jinping, to ease tensions in the region, and bring an end to hostilities.
During the visits, the special envoys and special representative have met with relevant countries’ leaders, foreign ministers and other officials in charge of foreign affairs and national security. They engaged in friendly exchanges on deepening bilateral relations, had comprehensive and in-depth exchanges of views on promoting deescalation of regional hotspot and sensitive issues, and reached a number of common understandings regarding strengthening solidarity and collaboration, safeguarding peace, and promoting development.
As a permanent member of the UN Security Council and a major responsible country, China is committed to supporting, promoting, and safeguarding world peace and development. On addressing challenges to peace and security and the hotspot issues in the Middle East and Africa, China always stands shoulder to shoulder with Middle Eastern and African people, stands for peace and cooperation, and stands on the right side of history. China will work with regional countries and the international community to implement the Global Security Initiative, follow the new vision of common, comprehensive, cooperative and sustainable security, and continue playing a constructive role in achieving enduring peace and security, development and prosperity in the Middle East and Africa.
NHK: Regarding the China-Russia-U.S. Summit, the U.S. side indicated that topics such as nuclear arms control and the situation in Iran will be discussed. Could you specify which issues China plans to raise during the meeting? Furthermore, could you provide more details on any bilateral meetings that China may have scheduled with countries such as Russia and Japan?
Mao Ning: The three heads of state will have an in-depth exchange of views on important issues bearing on global peace. We will release information about the summit in a timely manner. Please check back for updates.
Regarding the bilateral meetings on the sidelines of APEC, I have no information to share at the moment.
Beijing Daily: The international community is paying close attention to the evolving situation in the Middle East and the Gulf region and attaching high importance to China’s role there. Could you share more information on the trip to regional countries by the Special Envoy of the Chinese Government on the Middle East Issue?
Mao Ning: Special Envoy Zhai Jun visited Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia, where he had in-depth exchanges with relevant parties over the situation in Iran and Yemen. He elaborated on China’s positions and propositions and worked actively to promote peace and facilitate talks, contributing to efforts towards bringing about a deescalation in the Gulf region and a political settlement of the Yemen issue. Relevant parties spoke highly of President Xi Jinping’s initiatives and propositions on promoting peace and stability in the Middle East, and commended China’s constructive role in promoting peace and security in the region. The most pressing task at hand, they believe, is to rebuild trust as soon as possible, bring the temperature down, and prevent a resumption of fighting. They look to the international community to pool greater synergy and help deescalate the situation.
China will continue to be guided by President Xi Jinping’s initiatives and propositions and facilitate a dynamism where parties stay committed to dialogue and consultation, work to bridge differences, accommodate each other’s concerns and build consensus on peace. We will support regional countries in jointly creating a new regional architecture for common, comprehensive, cooperative and sustainable security, and join international efforts to bring about the early restoration of peace and tranquility in the region.
Beijing Daily: Did Special Envoy Zhai Jun have contact with the Houthis during his visit?
Mao Ning: While visiting regional countries, Special Envoy Zhai Jun had communication with various parties of the Yemen issue on the situation in the country. China supports the political settlement of the Yemen issue, calls for upholding the sovereignty, unity and territorial integrity of Yemen, and hopes to see security and stability restored in the country at an early date. China will continue playing a constructive role to this end.
China News Service: From November 9 to 11, the Institute of Japanese Studies at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences and the Exhibition Hall of Evidences of Crime Committed by Unit 731 of the Imperial Japanese Army co-hosted the Symposium on the Atrocities of the Imperial Japanese Army’s Bacteriological and Chemical Warfare in Harbin. Do you have any comment?
Mao Ning: During World War II, the Imperial Japanese Army conducted bacteriological and chemical warfare, which inflicted untold suffering on the Chinese people and constitutes heinous crimes against humanity. To this day, Japan’s abandoned chemical weapons (ACWs) in China continue to pose a serious threat to the health and safety of the Chinese people and the environment in affected regions. Japan committed innumerable and undeniable crimes during its militarist aggression. We urge Japan to deeply reflect on its war crimes, fully and faithfully fulfill its responsibility and obligations under the Chemical Weapons Convention and the MOU between the two countries’ governments, including thoroughly removing the hazardous ACWs as soon as possible, so that the Chinese people no longer have to live on soil tainted by ACWs, and earn the trust of its Asian neighbors and the rest of the world with concrete actions.
Bloomberg: The U.S. signed an agreement with nine other parties on AI-related supply chain. Do you think this agreement will have any impact on the upcoming meeting between Chinese and U.S. presidents? Will China bring up this agreement in the meeting?
Mao Ning: China believes that artificial intelligence is a shared asset of humanity, and that there should be no drawing lines along ideological differences or forming exclusive groupings. All parties should work together for the development and governance of AI, so that it can better serve the common welfare of humanity.
As for the meeting between the two presidents, the two sides are in communication.
