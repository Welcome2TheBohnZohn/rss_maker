ARTICLE TEXT:
China News Service: We noticed that China organized a side event on Japan’s abandoned chemical weapons (ACWs) in China on the sidelines of the 31st Session of the Conference of the State Parties to the Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC) in The Hague, Netherlands. Can you brief us on the relevant information?
Guo Jiakun: On November 25, China organized a side event on Japan’s ACWs in China on the sidelines of the 31st Session of the Conference of the State Parties to the CWC and briefed the delegates attending the meeting on the fact that Japan’s ACWs in China are large in quantity, severe in harm and slow in disposal.
China stressed that early and complete destruction of these weapons is Japan’s due obligation under international law in accordance with the CWC. The destruction process is now seriously lagging behind schedule. The root cause is that Japan insists on its distorted view of history, fails to genuinely reflect on its war crimes, stays indifferent to the harm of chemical weapons, flouts international obligations, and invests insufficiently in this matter. OPCW’s Director-General Sabrina Dallafior, chairperson of the Executive Council permanent representative of Morocco Ambassador Mohamed Basri, and delegates from many countries pointed out that Japan’s ACWs in China pose the greatest practical impediment to the goal of a world free of chemical weapons.
China once again urges Japan to earnestly assume its historical, political and legal responsibilities, faithfully fulfill the obligations under the CWC and the MOU between the two countries’ governments, comprehensively scale up its human resources and financial inputs, actively provide information on burial sites, and speed up end-to-end disposal process, so that the day will come sooner when the hazardous Japanese ACWs are destroyed completely and thoroughly, when the Chinese people no longer have to live on soil tainted by ACWs, when justice prevails in the international community, and when humanity lives in a world free of chemical weapons.
Bloomberg: The U.S. has announced plans to impose a fresh round of tariffs on goods from China and other economies. The rate of imports from China would rise to 20 percent according to the USTR. That would be in line with the country’s prior agreement with the Trump administration. But the U.S. also said that that rate could double to 40 percent if talks between the two countries fall apart. We wonder if the foreign ministry has any comment.
Guo Jiakun: We’ve made clear China’s position yesterday. Tariff wars and trade wars serve no one’s interests. Relevant issues should be resolved through consultation on the basis of equality, mutual respect and mutual benefit. So-called “industrial overcapacity” does not really exist and should not be used as a pretext for political manipulation. China and the U.S. need to jointly deliver on the important common understandings between the two presidents, and maintain the steady growth momentum of China-U.S. economic and trade ties. China will continue doing what is necessary to defend its legitimate rights and interests.
Reuters: A Canadian NATO intern arrested in Belgium on suspicion of espionage will be placed under electronic monitoring while awaiting trial. This was a Canadian national of Chinese origin. What is China’s response to this?
Guo Jiakun: We are not familiar with the specifics you mentioned.
AFP: Japan’s coast guard said today that they had warned two Chinese ships to leave waters near the disputed Senkaku or Diaoyu Islands. I wonder if the foreign ministry has a comment on this apparent incident.
Guo Jiakun: Diaoyu Dao and its affiliated islands have always been part of China’s territory. The Japanese vessels illegally intruded into the territorial waters of Diaoyu Dao in an act of provocation. The move seriously violates China’s sovereignty and undermines stability in the waters off Diaoyu Dao. China has lodged stern representations with the Japanese side. China Coast Guard vessels took necessary control measures against the relevant vessels in accordance with law. China firmly safeguards its territorial sovereignty and maritime rights and interests. We urge the Japanese side to abide by the four-point principled consensus between China and Japan, stop all infringement activities and provocations, and take effective measures to prevent the recurrence of similar incidents.
Beijing Daily: Recently, the 10th China-Africa Youth Festival was held in Beijing and Shaanxi and received extensive coverage by African media. Can you share more information about the event?
Guo Jiakun: The China-Africa Youth Festival is a regular event under the framework of the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation. Ten consecutive editions have been held so far and the event is widely popular among young people from both China and Africa. Recently, the 10th festival was successfully held in Beijing and Shaanxi. More than 60 African youths visited the Museum of the Communist Party of China and the Yan’an Revolutionary Memorial Hall to learn about the party’s great journey, and visited the National Committee of the Chinese People’s Political Consultative Conference, where they learned about China’s socialist consultative democracy. They also explored the rich heritage of the Chinese civilization at the Palace Museum, and visited high-tech companies to gain firsthand insight into the dynamism of China’s economy. Through a wide range of engaging activities, young people from both sides had in-depth and heartfelt exchanges, further strengthening their bonds of friendship.
This year marks the 70th anniversary of the establishment of China-Africa diplomatic relations and the China-Africa Year of People-to-People Exchanges. China stands ready to continue working with the African side to promote mutual learning and exchanges, deepen mutual understanding and friendship, advance our respective modernization drives, and together build a community with a shared future for humanity.
