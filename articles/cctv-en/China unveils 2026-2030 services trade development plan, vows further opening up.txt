BEIJING, Nov. 26 (Xinhua) -- China will expand the opening up of its trade in services during the 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026-2030), according to a sector plan released on Thursday.

The plan, jointly issued by eight departments including the Ministry of Commerce, says China will refine the negative list management system for cross-border trade in services, expand market access in an orderly manner, and improve the standardization level of this sector.

The plan also aims to ensure the efficient, convenient and secure cross-border flow of factors including data, personnel, capital and technology. In particular, China will develop service infrastructure for cross-border data flows, deepen international data cooperation, and foster a more digital-friendly environment for trade in services, to facilitate the cross-border flow of data.

"China's opening up in the services sector provides vital certainty to global trade in services," said Yang Zhengwei, vice president of the Development Research Center of the State Council.

This opening up is expected to drive the growth of services trade, and the plan sets corresponding targets. By 2030, China's trade in services should achieve a better balance between imports and exports, according to the plan.

In the first three quarters of 2026, China's imports and exports of services reached 5,768.77 billion yuan (about 859.78 billion U.S. dollars), up 8.5 percent year on year, while the deficit in this sector came in at 1,225.97 billion yuan.

To boost services exports, the plan encourages professional service providers in consulting, accounting, legal, intellectual property and other fields to enhance their international service capabilities. It will also launch dedicated initiatives to promote exports in producer services, tourism services and cultural industries.

Meanwhile, China will expand imports of producer services such as R&D and design, resource recycling and environmental governance. It will also encourage imports of more high-quality consumer services, including healthcare, cultural performances and sporting events. In addition, outstanding foreign audiovisual programs and films will be introduced to Chinese audiences, the plan notes.

Li Bin, deputy head of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, said China's services imports reached more than 620 billion U.S. dollars in 2025, covering travel, intellectual property, transport and business services, making China the world's second-largest importer of services.
