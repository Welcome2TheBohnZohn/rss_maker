Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO after board reversal

ARTICLE TEXT:
Nvidia has reinstated co-founder Jensen Huang as president and chief executive officer, reversing its earlier position on his departure. The company's board of directors voted unanimously to restore Huang to the positions, effective immediately, and reinstate him as a board member, the company announced on November 23.

The decision followed a series of discussions between Huang and the board, which said it was satisfied with his candor and leadership capabilities. The board also consulted senior US government officials and concluded that Huang's return would pose no national security risk.

Jensen Huang gives a keynote address at the Nvidia Berlin GPU Technology Conference in Berlin, Germany, October 21, 2026. /VCG

Board Chair Harvey C. Jones said Huang was the right person to lead Nvidia and that his reinstatement was in the best interests of the company's shareholders, employees, partners and customers. Jones will remain board chair. Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO during Huang's absence, will continue in her role as chief financial officer.

Huang voluntarily resigned on November 12 after the board raised concerns about his personal conduct, which it said was inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards and corporate culture. At Nvidia's quarterly earnings call on November 17, Jones said the board had unanimously accepted the resignation and did not intend to reinstate Huang. Kress was appointed interim CEO, and the company began searching for a permanent successor.

The board's latest decision came after a wave of public support for Huang's return. Reuters reported that a petition calling for his reinstatement had attracted more than 180,000 signatures, including one from Tesla CEO Elon Musk. US President Donald Trump, who had urged Nvidia to bring Huang back the previous week, welcomed the board's decision on Monday, calling it the "correct and proper decision" in a post on Truth Social. Trump also said the US government had no reason to believe Huang's return posed a national security risk and urged Nvidia to continue ensuring compliance with US export controls.

Despite Huang's return, an independent investigation launched by the board will continue. Nvidia said the committee overseeing the inquiry, composed entirely of independent directors and supported by outside legal counsel, would carry its investigation through to completion. Huang has pledged full cooperation.

The company has also established a separate National Security and Export Control Committee, made up exclusively of independent directors, to oversee trade compliance and national security risks. The committee is engaging legal counsel with relevant expertise, while Nvidia is seeking to appoint a chief compliance officer to oversee compliance across the company and report to the board's Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee.

Nvidia said it would continue cooperating with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.
