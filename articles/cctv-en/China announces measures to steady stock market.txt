China announces measures to steady stock market

ARTICLE TEXT:
BEIJING, Nov. 22 (Xinhua) -- China will roll out a slew of measures to steady the stock market, including lowering the minimum margin requirement for leveraged stock purchases to generate incremental funds, the country's securities regulator said Sunday.

With approval from the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC), the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing stock exchanges will lower the minimum margin ratio from 100 percent to 80 percent, effective from Nov. 30, according to the CSRC.

The adjustment is estimated to theoretically bring in nearly 400 billion yuan (about 59.58 billion U.S. dollars) in incremental funds.

Adjustments to the ratio are seen as one of the key tools for conducting counter-cyclical regulation on China's stock market. Previous downward adjustments in history have increased margin balances and trading activity, playing a notable role in supporting the market, experts said.

The Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges also introduced a new batch of fee reduction measures, including waiving the participant business unit usage fee, according to the CSRC. The measures waiving listing fees for listed companies and bond transaction handling fees, which were already implemented earlier this year, will be extended for two years to 2028.

The Shenzhen Stock Exchange on the same day began soliciting public opinion on the market-making rules for stocks on the ChiNext board, China's Nasdaq-style board of growth enterprises.

The introduction of the market-maker system for the board is an important institutional arrangement to bolster market-support forces, strengthen market resilience and improve the quality of operations, according to the exchange.

The measures came after China's major stock indices moved lower in recent days, while U.S. stocks plunged on Friday, unsettling investors.

Earlier this month, China's central bank stepped up monetary policy support for the capital market, conducting the third operation of the Securities, Funds and Insurance companies Swap Facility to help financial institutions secure funding and boost their capacity to buy stocks, and reducing the interest rate of a relending facility for share buybacks and shareholding increases.
