BEIJING, Oct. 3 (Xinhua) -- Although called Tiangong, or "Heavenly Palace," China's space station is becoming less distant and more like home, as astronauts bring the comforting tastes into orbit.

The Chinese space kitchen now serves nearly 200 food items. Alongside dumplings and mooncakes, home-style dishes such as Kung Pao chicken, mapo tofu and fried rice have made it onto the station's menu.

Videos showing astronauts savoring freshly cooked meat and cakes in orbit have gone viral. Their smiling faces and bright eyes make the aroma almost leap off the screen.

Yang Liwei, the country's first astronaut, joked at a recent press conference that he was envious. He said astronauts can now use air-frying equipment in space and enjoy baked chicken wings in orbit. During his Shenzhou-5 mission in 2003, which lasted less than one day, his diet was limited to packaged instant food that could not be heated.

Microgravity, cosmic radiation and the enclosed cabin environment impose strict requirements on space diets. Floating particles and droplets may drift into instruments and trigger malfunctions. Since Yuri Gagarin, the first human to travel into space, paste-like food and compressed energy bars had long served as the standard meals for astronauts.

In June 2013, Wang Yaping, together with two fellow male crewmates, carried out the 15-day Shenzhou-10 space mission. People remembered this female astronaut with a ponytail for her space lecture. At the same time, the image of her floating a "zongzi," a leaf-wrapped rice dumpling, by hand also left a deep impression on the public.

Zongzi is traditionally enjoyed during the Dragon Boat Festival, which happened to coincide with the Shenzhou-10 mission that year. To help the astronauts celebrate, this special snack was added to the spaceship pantry.

Students watch Wang Yaping (R) demonstrating drinking water in China's space station, at the China Science and Technology Museum in Beijing, capital of China, Dec. 9, 2021. (Xinhua/Jin Liwang)

Eight years later, Wang spent Spring Festival aboard the Tiangong space station during the Shenzhou-13 mission. It is also China's first six-month-long space stay. Scientists customized the cuisine: for instance, they prepared dumplings with seafood fillings because Wang grew up by the sea, and plenty of spicy food for crew member Ye Guangfu, who was born in chili-obsessed Sichuan Province.

At that time, the core module Tianhe had several sections, including those for working, sleeping, dining and exercising. The videos of Shenzhou-13 crew practicing calligraphy and playing Taichi became social media hits.

China completed the construction of its space station in 2022, with one core module and two lab modules forming a 110-square-meter orbital structure. Oxygen can be fully regenerated, and water recovery exceeds 95 percent.

Since then, long-duration spaceflights have become routine, and the Chinese "space home" can host up to six astronauts in orbit at a time.

Space residency is also set to last even longer. One member of the Shenzhou-23 crew, currently aboard the Tiangong, will conduct a one-year in-orbit stay. Moreover, as China pushes further into deep-space exploration, these extended stays will become the norm, which means astronauts' diets will be more important than ever.
