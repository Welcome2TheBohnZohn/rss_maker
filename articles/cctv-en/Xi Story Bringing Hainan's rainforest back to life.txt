ARTICLE TEXT:
HAIKOU, Nov. 22 (Xinhua) -- Zhou Zhaoli, a member of the Hainan gibbon monitoring team, starts his day at 4:30 a.m., long before sunrise.

With flashlights in hand, he and his teammates pick their way through dense undergrowth in the mountainous Bawangling area of south China's Hainan Province, braving thorns and leech bites.

Their reward is sometimes the sight of Hainan gibbons high in the forest canopy, and the sound of their distinctive calls echoing through the trees at first light.

The world's 20 gibbon species live in 11 Asian countries, and most are in decline. Native to the island province's tropical rainforests, the Hainan gibbon was once pushed to the brink of extinction by commercial logging, deforestation and poaching. Today it is the only gibbon species in the world whose population has kept growing.

During an inspection tour of Hainan in April 2022, Chinese President Xi Jinping visited part of these rainforests and asked local villagers whether they had seen the rare primates. At that time, the habitat hosted 36 gibbons in five groups.

By the end of 2025, the figure reached 44 gibbons in seven groups, up from fewer than 10 in two groups in the early 1980s.

Hainan's tropical rainforest is China's largest, best-preserved and most concentrated stretch of contiguous island-type tropical rainforest.

The rainforest was once fragmented across more than 20 nature reserves and other protected areas in nine cities and counties. In 2019, Hainan began piloting an integrated approach, placing previously separate nature reserves, forest parks and state-owned forest farms under unified management.

About two years later, the Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park was established as one of China's first five national parks, which together span ecosystems from giant panda habitats in Sichuan, Gansu and Shaanxi provinces to the only home of the Hainan gibbon.

Xi has closely followed the park's development. In 2018, he called for establishing national parks, including those protecting tropical rainforests. In 2019, he chaired a key reform meeting that approved a pilot plan for the Hainan national park. In 2021, he announced the establishment of China's first five national parks at the COP15 leaders' summit.

During that 2022 inspection tour, Xi walked along a boardwalk in the national park, taking a close look at its natural environment.

"He slowed his pace, studying the trees and listening closely to the sounds of nature as we walked," said Zheng Yan, a member of the park's public education and outreach team, who accompanied Xi and introduced the park to him during the visit.

The park has improved markedly in both infrastructure and management capacity since its establishment, said Zheng, who has worked there for more than a decade.

Xi's interest in rainforest conservation extends beyond China. On a visit to Brazil in July 2014, he recalled his 2009 trip to Manaus, a city deep in the Amazon rainforest, saying that protecting this vast expanse of pristine forest was vital to the global ecosystem.

Protecting tropical rainforests is closely tied to humanity's survival and well-being, and it is a shared responsibility that calls for concerted global efforts.

In recent years, China and Brazil have deepened cooperation on climate change and sustainable development. With support from the international community, the two countries are exploring new approaches to rainforest conservation and development, including measures that help local farmers improve their livelihoods and reduce deforestation while safeguarding the world's precious ecological assets and advancing global climate goals.

Greice Quele Aparecida Barboza de Oliveira, an environmental analyst with Brazil's Chico Mendes Institute for Biodiversity Conservation, visited Hainan recently to learn about China's approach to biodiversity conservation and to see how the national park protects the rare primate.

"These are areas I knew little about before. I am studying them closely so that I can take China's experience in wildlife conservation home with me," she said.

She was in Hainan for the 2026 Tropical Rainforest Ecosystem Partners Conference, which ended on Sunday and drew international officials, experts and representatives from more than 20 countries.

Climate change is becoming an increasingly serious challenge in Brazil and around the world, she said, adding that international cooperation and exchanges like those at the Hainan conference can help share effective conservation practices with other countries.

During his 2022 visit to Hainan, Xi also asked about forest rangers' income and village-level public spending. He stressed the need to strictly protect tropical rainforests while ensuring that ecological conservation, green development and improvements in people's livelihoods go hand in hand.

Hainan's tropical rainforest lies in one of the world's 34 biodiversity hotspots and serves as a reservoir of water, a source of food and wealth and a carbon sink.

"Hainan's tropical rainforest does not belong to Hainan alone. It belongs to the people of the whole country and to Mother Earth as a whole. It is a national treasure," Xi said.
