by Xinhua writers Li Minghao, Yi Ling and Quan Xiaoshu

JIUQUAN, Nov. 25 (Xinhua) -- China launched the Shenzhou-24 crewed spacecraft on Wednesday, sending three astronauts, including a Pakistani astronaut, into the orbiting Tiangong space station.

Zhang Jingbo, spokesperson for the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA), described the joint flight as a landmark achievement in bilateral cooperation as the two countries mark the 75th anniversary of diplomatic relations this year, and an important manifestation of their traditional friendship.

China's manned space program has consistently adhered to the principle of peaceful utilization, equality and mutual benefit, and common development, while actively promoting international exchanges and cooperation and the building of a community with a shared future for mankind in outer space, Zhang said at a news conference on Tuesday.

UN spokesperson Stephane Dujarric said the United Nations welcomed the peaceful use of outer space and the international cooperation this mission represents.

Aboard a Long March-2F carrier rocket, the spaceship blasted off at 8:18 a.m. (Beijing Time) from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwest China. About 10 minutes after liftoff, the spaceship separated from the rocket and entered its designated orbit. The CMSA declared the launch "a complete success."

The spacecraft completed a fast, automated rendezvous and docking with the front port of Tianhe, the station's core module, in about 3.5 hours. The Shenzhou-23 crew aboard Tiangong opened the hatch at 2:35 p.m. and welcomed the new arrivals.

The two crews then took group pictures marking the ninth space get-together in China's aerospace history.

After completing a short-term joint flight, Ali will return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft alongside two Shenzhou-23 crew members. The other two Shenzhou-24 astronauts will stay in orbit for roughly six months along with Shenzhou-23 astronaut Zhu Yangzhu, who has already spent six months in orbit and will remain aboard Tiangong to complete a year-long stay experiment.

JOINT EXPERIMENTS

More than 90 scientific experiments and technology tests are planned for the Shenzhou-24 mission, covering space life sciences, space material science, microgravity fluid physics and combustion science, fundamental physics, space medicine and new space technologies, according to the CMSA.

Four China-Pakistan cooperative projects in the fields of biotechnology and materials will be conducted during the Shenzhou-24 mission, and Ali will conduct an experiment on photosynthetic cyanobacteria, which was proposed by the Pakistan Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (SUPARCO).

The experiment is expected to provide a biological basis and data support for oxygen regeneration and biomanufacturing at future space stations and deep-space bases, said Chen Mengyun, who is in charge of international cooperation projects at the Technology and Engineering Center for Space Utilization under the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Another cooperative project, led by the University of Agriculture, Faisalabad in Pakistan, will expose 32 quinoa seed samples to the space environment outside the station for about one year to investigate their stress tolerance and longevity, with the aim of screening for superior varieties.

Describing Pakistan as one of the countries most vulnerable to climate change, Pakistani Federal Minister for Planning, Development and Special Initiatives Ahsan Iqbal said that the country needs smart and climate-resilient agriculture.

"The mission gives Pakistani scientists rare access to space laboratories, offering potential breakthroughs to boost productivity and climate resilience," he told Xinhua.

The other two experiments will focus on multilayer graphene-based hybrid composites and solar-heated self-healing carbon fiber composites, Chen said.

Pakistani Ambassador to China Syed Ali Asad Gillani said the four experiments in orbit will not only benefit Pakistani researchers, but also help advance the prosperity and development of all humanity.

China-Pakistan space cooperation spans more than three decades. In 1990, Pakistan's first experimental satellite, Badr-1, was launched aboard a Chinese rocket from the Xichang Satellite Launch Center. More recently, China launched Pakistan's cube satellite aboard the Chang'e-6 probe in 2024 and placed a Pakistani remote-sensing satellite in orbit via a Long March-6 rocket in April 2026.

COOPERATIVE TEAM

To support the joint flight, the Chinese side prepared bilingual Chinese-English manuals, provided food tailored to Ali's cultural and dietary habits, and tailored training and ceremonial attire for the Pakistani.

According to Shi Yusheng, an expert with the Astronaut Center of China, the center devised multiple methods to help Ali master Chinese commands and terminology critical for spaceflight. Beyond basic language courses, Ali received intensive training in high-frequency spaceflight vocabulary and commands. Later in the program, he was also taught classical Chinese poetry.

"With tight timelines and heavy tasks, we focused on spacecraft operations, pressure emergency response, fault handling and scientific experiments, and conducted high-frequency joint training to ensure the mission's success," Tang, the Shenzhou-24 mission commander, said at Tuesday's media briefing.

Wu, the mission flight engineer, said Ali had worked diligently to learn Chinese, while the Chinese crew used English as a supplementary communication tool. Through sustained effort, the crew progressed from merely understanding each other to anticipating each other's actions.

Ali can now communicate with his Chinese counterparts in Chinese. He described mastering flight skills, learning Chinese and forging close bonds with his Chinese crewmates as key gains from his training.

Ali said China and Pakistan have turned a pilot into an astronaut in a remarkably short span of time. "Now it is time to show this great achievement in orbit and send the world a message that we work hand in hand for the peaceful utilization of space," he said.

In an interview with Xinhua, the Pakistani ambassador to China described the joint flight as taking bilateral relations "from Earth to space," calling it a dream come true for more than 250 million Pakistanis and "an excellent model for South-South cooperation."

MULTIPLE TASKS

The Shenzhou-24 mission's main objectives are to complete an in-orbit crew rotation with the Shenzhou-23 crew, maintain routine station operations, conduct space science and application work, and carry out maintenance, public outreach and payload tests.

One of the highlights is China's first year-long in-orbit stay experiment. The year-long test will establish a new system for studying the human body in space, conduct interdisciplinary frontier scientific experiments, explore and answer intrinsic mechanisms of human adaptation and remodeling in a spaceflight environment, build a space-human-body research database combining macro and micro perspectives, and produce a batch of original outcomes, said Zhang, the CMSA spokesperson.

In addition, the test will further accumulate practical experience in astronaut support technologies, enrich and improve the major safeguarding systems for in-orbit health, work and daily life, and steadily enhance the astronaut residency support capabilities of China's space station, he added.
